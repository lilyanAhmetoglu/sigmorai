## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Spot a reward that pays for activity, and change it to pay for a team result.
- Pick the right tool for a cost worry: an enforced limit, not an alert or a nudge.
- Decide who may see per-person usage data, and protect people who report AI mistakes.

## The problem

A recruitment agency has 70 recruiters in 7 teams. They use an AI assistant to write job adverts, screen CVs and draft emails to candidates. Finance has a fixed yearly credit budget. Its draft cost controls for next quarter:

1. A monthly default limit for each user.
2. Higher group limits for the two sourcing teams.
3. A personal override for one team lead who runs a trial.
4. The workspace **overage** limit left at **No limit**, "because the defaults already cap use". (Overage is paid use after the credits run out.)

The managing director also wants a reward for teams that adopt the tool well. An **incentive** is a reward or a rule that makes people more likely to do something. This lesson shows how to point incentives at results, and how to control cost with limits that really stop spending. The facts come from the Claude Code docs and the OpenAI ChatGPT Enterprise docs (October 2026).

## The idea

**People do what is rewarded.** This is our own framing, not a vendor rule: if you pay for "using AI", you get more AI use. That is not the same as better work. The vendors' pages tell you what their numbers can and can't show.

**Usage numbers show activity.** Look at what the dashboards are for:

- The OpenAI [Usage Insights page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/usage-insights) says: "A message count doesn't tell you how much work the team finished." It also says activity counts "don't measure completed work". It asks you to pair usage and spend with team records about effort, quality and results.
- The OpenAI guide [ChatGPT Work: usage and cost](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/chatgpt-work-usage-and-cost) warns: "Usage alone doesn't establish savings", and that projected time savings are not guaranteed money.
- The [Claude Code analytics page](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/analytics) has a leaderboard of the top 10 users. The page says it helps you spot heavy users who can teach their methods, give feedback and help new users start.

Our reading: so the leaderboard is a way to find helpers, not a grade for each person. A recruiter who sends 300 messages is not ahead of one who sends 60 and fills more jobs.

**Reward the result, at team level.** These are our suggestions for this course. They follow the vendors' advice to read usage next to results:

1. **A team result compared with a baseline,** for example time to fill a job, compared with last quarter.
2. **A guard number** next to it, so the outcome can't be bought with lower quality. For example: candidates who leave in the first 90 days.
3. **Sharing,** such as a reusable technique posted for others. The [champion kit](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/champion-kit) says these are the most useful posts.

Rewarding a team, not one person, makes it less useful to copy a colleague's volume or to hide a mistake.

**Protect people who report errors.** Human review finds AI mistakes. That is its job. If the person who reports a mistake gets blamed, reports stop, and the mistakes reach customers instead. Our rule, not a vendor rule: count error reports as a good sign, and measure what happens next, such as how fast the cause is fixed.

**Limits control cost; rewards steer behaviour.** The OpenAI usage and cost guide separates the controls:

- **User usage limits** manage each user's eligible usage. They can be set as workspace defaults, group limits or individual overrides.
- The **workspace overage limit** controls how much use can continue after the shared credits run out.
- **Usage alerts** only warn. The guide says: "Alerts notify; they don't stop spending."
- A model default or staff guidance can lower use, but the guide says "neither replaces an enforced usage limit or workspace overage setting".

The guide also warns that "**No limit** isn't a zero-spend cap". On the Anthropic side, the [Claude Code admin guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/admin-setup) lists spend controls, "Spend limits and rate limits", as part of usage visibility.

**Give champions time, not a second job.** The champion kit budgets about 15 + 20 + 5 = 40 minutes of core tasks each week (our sum), plus optional pairing. It says the role should stay "a multiplier on your existing work rather than an additional support responsibility". Our advice: put that time in the person's plan, and mention it in their yearly review.

**Decide who sees names.** The Claude Code leaderboard has an **Export all users** button. The page says the CSV "includes all users, not just the top 10 displayed". The [OpenAI workspace analytics page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/workspace-analytics) says: "Treat downloaded reports as identifiable organizational data." Apply your company's access, storage and retention rules to them. Our default: managers see team totals; a few named people see names, to help users, not to rank them.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the draft controls

```text
Cost controls (finance draft)
  Workspace default:  a monthly limit for each user
  Group limits:       Sourcing A and Sourcing B, higher
  Override:           team lead (trial), personal limit
  Group "Interns":    no limit set
  Overage limit:      No limit
```

What goes wrong:

- **"No limit" is not a cap.** The guide says "**No limit** isn't a zero-spend cap". If the contract allows overage, use can go on after the credits run out.
- **The interns group is not capped at zero.** The guide says: "A group without a configured limit doesn't impose a zero-credit cap."
- **Nobody knows each person's effective limit:** the one limit that really applies to them.

### Step 2: the fixed controls

```text
Overage limit:      the amount finance agrees
                    (zero, where supported, if no overage is allowed)
Workspace default:  a monthly limit for each user
Group limits:       Sourcing A and B, higher
Interns:            check each intern's effective limit; set a
                    group limit if the result is not what you want
Override:           team lead, personal limit, ends with the trial
Alert at 80%:       kept, as an early warning only
Check:              the effective limit for each pilot participant
```

### Step 3: the hard case

**The hard case is a recruiter in two groups.** Sam is in Sourcing A and Sourcing B. The guide says: "When a person belongs to more than one group, the highest applicable group limit applies." So Sam gets the higher of the two. A personal override would beat both: "An individual user override takes precedence over group and workspace defaults." Our habit: list each pilot person with their groups and any override, and write down the one limit that applies.

### Step 4: why it is better

- **A real stop.** The overage limit, not an alert, ends spending past the agreed amount.
- **No gaps.** Every group and person has a known limit.
- **Alerts still help,** as a warning before the stop.
- **One list to check.** Each person's effective limit is written down.

## Common mistakes

1. **Paying for activity.** Logins, messages and credits show use, not value. Fix: pay for a team outcome with a guard number.
2. **Using a nudge as a budget.** Alerts, defaults and emails don't stop spending. Fix: set user limits and the overage limit.
3. **Exporting names without a plan.** A download with names is personal data. Fix: decide who needs names before you export, and apply your access and retention rules.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

Credits and overage work differently in each situation. What happens in each one?

```text
1. Committed credits (credits already in the contract) remain.
   Does use create a new charge by itself?
2. No credits remain, and the contract doesn't allow overage.
3. No credits remain, the contract allows overage, and the
   overage limit is set to zero.
```

### Answer

1. **No.** The guide says using those credits "doesn't, by itself, create a new charge beyond your existing agreement."
2. **Use stops.** The guide says eligible usage "stops when no available credits remain."
3. **Use stops at the credits.** The guide says: "Where supported, an overage limit of zero prevents the workspace from continuing into overage".

## Recap

- Usage numbers show activity. Reward team outcomes against a baseline, with a guard number for quality.
- Count error reports as a good sign, and limit who sees per-person data.
- Alerts and nudges only warn. Real cost control comes from user usage limits plus the overage limit, checked per person.
