## Why it matters

People do what they are measured and rewarded for. If you reward "using AI", you get a lot of AI use. That is not the same as better work. This chapter helps you design rewards and limits that push in the useful direction.

The idea that people adapt to a measure is our own framing, not a vendor claim. The vendors' documents give you the facts about what their numbers do and do not show.

## A usage score rewards volume

Dashboards make it easy to rank people by use. Anthropic's Claude Code analytics page includes a leaderboard of the top 10 users by contribution volume, and lets an admin export all users as a CSV file. OpenAI's Usage Insights page shows messages, credits and active users per category.

Look at what the vendors say about these numbers:

- OpenAI: a message count doesn't tell you how much work the team finished. Usage and spend should be paired with team records to see whether the work takes less effort, meets quality standards or gives better results.
- OpenAI's usage and cost guide: usage alone doesn't establish savings.
- Anthropic: the analytics page presents the leaderboard as a way to find power users who can share techniques, give feedback and help onboard others.

In our reading, neither vendor presents a usage count, such as messages or sessions, as a score of how well one person does their job. If you pay a bonus for messages sent, people will send messages. Chapter 3 of the previous realm showed why activity numbers are not value.

Example: a rep who sends 400 messages a week and books 3 meetings is not ahead of a rep who sends 95 and books 9.

## Reward what you want

Pick the result first, then reward that. Three kinds of results fit most teams. These are our own suggestions, not vendor rules:

1. **Team outcomes** from your own baseline: time to finish, rework, replies, meetings. Use the outcome numbers from the previous realm.
2. **Sharing**: reusable techniques posted for others, the kind of post the champion kit calls most useful.
3. **Catching errors**: a person who finds and reports a wrong AI output has protected a customer.

Reward at team level where you can. Individual rankings invite one person to copy another's volume, or to hide mistakes.

## Don't punish error reports

Chapter 2 put human review at the points that matter. Reviewers will find errors. If the person who reports an error is blamed for making the program look bad, reports will stop, and the errors will go to customers instead.

Our rule: count error reports as a good sign, and measure what happens next, such as how fast the cause is fixed. This is our guidance, not a vendor requirement.

## Use limits for limits, rewards for behavior

Sometimes the real worry is cost. OpenAI's usage and cost guide separates the tools clearly:

- User usage limits and the workspace overage limit are enforced controls.
- Usage alerts notify, but they don't stop spending.
- A model default or employee guidance can influence consumption, but neither replaces an enforced limit.

Anthropic's admin guide lists spend controls such as spend limits and rate limits in its usage-visibility options.

So don't use a reward or a reminder to control cost. If a hard cap is needed, set one. Use incentives to steer behavior inside the cap.

## Give champions time

The champion kit estimates about 40 minutes a week for the core role and says the role should be a multiplier on existing work, not an extra support responsibility. In our view, a reward that comes with a second job will not last. Two cheap options are ours: put the time in the person's plan, and mention the work when you review their year.

## Protect individual data

The Claude Code CSV export contains all users. OpenAI's analytics page says downloaded reports are identifiable organizational data and should be handled under the organization's access, storage and retention policy.

Decide early who sees per-person usage. A practical default, ours again: show teams an aggregate, and let only a few named people see names, for support rather than scoring.

## An incentive check

This card is a tool for this course. Before you launch a reward, answer:

- What exact behavior does it reward?
- How could someone get the reward without helping the business?
- What guard number (chapter 3 of the previous realm) would show that?
- Does it punish people for reporting a problem?
- Who can see individual data?

## Key points

- Usage numbers show activity. Don't pay for activity alone.
- Reward team outcomes against a baseline, shared techniques and found errors.
- Never penalize people for reporting wrong AI output.
- Use enforced limits for cost control. Alerts and nudges don't stop spending.
- Give champions protected time, and keep person-level data on a need-to-know basis.
