## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Spot a reward that pays for activity, and change it to pay for a team result.
- Pick the right tool for a cost worry: an enforced limit, not an alert or a nudge.
- Decide who may see per-person usage data, and protect people who report AI mistakes.

## The problem

A recruitment agency has 70 recruiters in 7 teams. They use an AI assistant to write job adverts, screen CVs and draft emails to candidates. The managing director wants faster adoption and proposes three things:

1. A "power user" badge and a EUR 200 bonus for anyone who uses the assistant on at least 20 days a month.
2. A monthly league table of the 7 teams by credits used, posted on the office wall.
3. A usage alert at 80% of the yearly credits, "so we never overspend".

Last month one team lead also complained that a recruiter "keeps reporting AI mistakes and makes us look bad".

Every point sounds positive. Each one can push people the wrong way. An **incentive** is a reward or a rule that makes people more likely to do something. This lesson shows how to point incentives at results, and how to control cost with real limits. The facts come from the Claude Code docs and the OpenAI ChatGPT Enterprise docs (October 2026).

## The idea

**People do what is rewarded.** This is our own framing, not a vendor rule: if you pay for "using AI", you get more AI use. That is not the same as better work. The vendors' pages tell you what their numbers can and can't show.

**Usage numbers show activity.** Look at what the dashboards are for:

- The OpenAI [Usage Insights page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/usage-insights) says: "A message count doesn't tell you how much work the team finished." It also says activity counts "don't measure completed work". It asks you to pair usage and spend with team records about effort, quality and results.
- The OpenAI guide [ChatGPT Work: usage and cost](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/chatgpt-work-usage-and-cost) says: "Usage alone doesn't establish savings."
- The [Claude Code analytics page](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/analytics) has a leaderboard of the top 10 users. The page says it helps you spot heavy users who can teach their methods, give feedback and help new users start.

Our reading: neither vendor presents a usage count as a score of how well one person does the job. A recruiter who sends 300 messages is not ahead of one who sends 60 and fills more jobs.

**Reward the result, at team level.** These are our suggestions for this course. They follow the vendors' advice to read usage next to results:

1. **A team result compared with a baseline,** for example time to fill a job, compared with last quarter.
2. **A guard number** next to it, so the outcome can't be bought with lower quality. For example: candidates who leave in the first 90 days.
3. **Sharing,** such as a reusable technique posted for others. The [champion kit](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/champion-kit) says these are the most useful posts.

Rewarding a team, not one person, makes it less useful to copy a colleague's volume or to hide a mistake.

**Protect people who report errors.** Human review finds AI mistakes. That is its job. If the person who reports a mistake gets blamed, reports stop, and the mistakes reach customers instead. Our rule, not a vendor rule: count error reports as a good sign, and measure what happens next, such as how fast the cause is fixed.

**Limits control cost; rewards steer behaviour.** The OpenAI usage and cost guide separates the controls:

- **User usage limits** manage each user's eligible usage. They can be set as workspace defaults, group limits or individual overrides.
- The **workspace overage limit** controls how much use can continue after the shared credits run out.
- **Usage alerts** only warn. The guide says: "Alerts notify; they don't stop spending."
- A model default or staff guidance can lower use, but the guide says "neither replaces an enforced usage limit or workspace overage setting".

The guide also warns that "**No limit** isn't a zero-spend cap". On the Anthropic side, the [Claude Code admin guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/admin-setup) lists spend controls, "Spend limits and rate limits", as part of usage visibility.

**Give champions time, not a second job.** The champion kit budgets about 15 + 20 + 5 = 40 minutes of core tasks each week (our sum), plus optional pairing. It says the role should stay "a multiplier on your existing work rather than an additional support responsibility". Our advice: put that time in the person's plan, and mention it in their yearly review.

**Decide who sees names.** The Claude Code leaderboard has an **Export all users** button. The page says the CSV "includes all users, not just the top 10 displayed". The [OpenAI workspace analytics page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/workspace-analytics) says: "Treat downloaded reports as identifiable organizational data." Apply your company's access, storage and retention rules to them. Our default: managers see team totals; a few named people see names, to help users, not to rank them.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak plan

```text
Adoption incentives (managing director's draft)
  Badge + EUR 200 bonus:  use the assistant on 20+ days a month
  League table:           teams ranked by credits used, on the wall
  Cost control:           alert at 80% of yearly credits
  Error reports:          "they make us look bad"
```

What goes wrong:

- **The bonus pays for opening the tool.** Anyone can log in on 20 days and change nothing.
- **The league table rewards spending.** The team that uses the most credits wins.
- **The alert does not stop spending.** It only sends a warning.
- **Error reports are treated as bad news.** People will stop sending them.

### Step 2: the good plan

```text
Team reward (per quarter, all 7 teams can win)
  Outcome:   days to fill a job, against last quarter
  Guard:     candidates who leave within 90 days must not rise
  Sharing:   one reusable prompt per team in the shared channel

Error reports
  Counted as a good sign; tracked: days until the cause is fixed

Data
  Team leads: team totals only
  2 named support staff: per-person data, for help, not ranking

Champions
  1 per team, about 40 minutes a week, written into their goals
```

Our maths for the reward check: if one team filled jobs in 30 days last quarter and in 27 days now, that is (30 − 27) ÷ 30 = 10% faster. The reward is paid only if the 90-day leaver number did not rise.

### Step 3: the hard case

**The hard case is the 80% alert.** Finance says it is a safety net. It is not. The guide says alerts notify and "don't stop spending". A plan that relies on it can still run past the credits, because use can go on into **overage**: paid use after the credits are gone, if the contract allows it.

The fix uses the enforced controls:

```text
Cost controls
  Workspace overage limit: set to the amount finance agrees
                           (zero, where supported, if no overage is allowed)
  User usage limits:       a monthly default for each user,
                           a higher group limit for the 2 heavy-use teams
  Alert at 80%:            keep it, as an early warning only
```

The guide says that where supported, "an overage limit of zero prevents the workspace from continuing into overage". The alert stays, but now it warns before a real stop, instead of pretending to be one.

### Step 4: why it is better

- **Rewards follow results.** A team gets paid for faster hiring that keeps quality.
- **Cost has a hard stop.** Limits stop spending; the alert only warns.
- **Errors come to the surface.** Reporting is safe, so problems get fixed.
- **Personal data stays small.** Names go only to the people who help.

## Common mistakes

1. **Paying for activity.** Logins, messages and credits show use, not value. Fix: pay for a team outcome with a guard number.
2. **Using a nudge as a budget.** Alerts, defaults and emails don't stop spending. Fix: set user limits and the overage limit.
3. **Showing all usage data to all staff.** It turns use into a contest and exposes personal data. Fix: team totals for managers, names for a few support people.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A workspace has a monthly default limit for each user. One analyst is in two groups: Group A has a higher limit, and Group B has an even higher one. The analyst also has a personal override. Group C has no limit set.

```text
1. Which limit applies to the analyst?
2. Without the personal override, which limit applies?
3. Does Group C's missing limit mean its members can use nothing?
```

### Answer

1. **The personal override.** The guide says: "An individual user override takes precedence over group and workspace defaults."
2. **Group B's limit.** The guide says: "When a person belongs to more than one group, the highest applicable group limit applies."
3. **No.** The guide says: "A group without a configured limit doesn't impose a zero-credit cap." It also says to check the effective limit for each pilot participant.

## Recap

- Usage numbers show activity. Reward team outcomes against a baseline, with a guard number for quality.
- Count error reports as a good sign, and limit who sees per-person data.
- Control cost with enforced user limits and the overage limit. Alerts and nudges only warn.
