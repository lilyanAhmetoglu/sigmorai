## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Say who can see a shared chat, project or helper, and what they see.
- Share a helper with a team that changes every month, without a stale list of names.
- Plan what happens to a helper when the person who owns it leaves.

## The problem

An insurance broker with 120 staff uses Claude on an Enterprise plan. Robin leads the 14-person claims team. Robin built a private Claude project, "Claims letters", with templates and tone rules. Robin invited each team member by name.

The team changes often. Temps join for a busy month and then leave. Robin is tired of adding and removing names. Robin's plan: "I'll make the project Public, so I never touch the list again. And I'll send our outside lawyer a public link to one chat."

The head of compliance asks the AI lead to check the plan first. The product facts below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026).

## The idea

Two questions matter for anything you share: **who can see it**, and **who owns it** when people change.

**A shared chat is a snapshot.** The [Claude docs](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16762496-share-a-chat-with-specific-people) say: "Sharing creates a snapshot of the chat at the moment you share it."

- Invited people do not see "Messages you send after sharing" unless you update the shared chat.
- They also do not see "Raw data from connectors or MCP tool calls, only Claude's final responses". (A **connector** links Claude to another app; **MCP** is an open standard for those links.)
- And: "Shared chats are view only."

The [Gemini docs](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13743730) say a shared chat page "shows the chat as it appeared when you created the public link."

**Who can see a shared chat differs a lot.**

- **Claude Team and Enterprise:**
  - "sharing stays inside your organization"
  - "Emails outside your organization can't be invited."
  - "Public links aren't available."
- **Gemini:**
  - Sharing "creates a public link to that chat. Anyone with the link can read the chat, even if you didn't share the link with them directly."
  - People with the link can also "reshare it with others".
  - Deleting the link later "doesn't delete any posts with the link that you've made on third-party sites."

**Admins** (the people who manage the team's account) hold the switch. The Claude docs: "Admins can turn off chat sharing for the organization, see and revoke shared chats, and view sharing activity in the audit log." An **audit log** is the record of who did what. The Gemini docs: "Your Workspace administrator can turn conversation sharing on or off."

**Sharing a helper.** A helper is a project, a plugin or a page that people use every day.

- **Claude projects** (Team and Enterprise), says the [Claude project sharing page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519189-manage-project-visibility-and-sharing):
  - **Public:** "Everyone in your organization can view and use the project".
  - **Private:** "Only invited members can view and use the project".
  - Each invited person gets **Can view** or **Can edit**. Your own chats in a shared project stay private: "your chats will remain private unless you choose to share them."
- **ChatGPT plugins.** The [ChatGPT migration guide](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/migrate-custom-gpts):
  - "Your new plugin starts private." Even people who used the old custom GPT need a new share.
  - "Sharing access and installation are separate steps."
  - "To share with people or groups, you need Share plugins access."
- **ChatGPT Space pages** are shared "with people or available workspace teams", with view or edit access. A warning from the [ChatGPT Space page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/chatgpt-space): "Content copied or summarized onto a page is visible to its collaborators."

**Turning sharing off does not take it back.**

- The Claude project page: when sharing is turned off, "Projects that are already shared stay shared, and users who already have access keep it." One exception: "Public projects become private, so people who only had access because a project was public lose it."
- The ChatGPT Space page, for Library files and folders: "Disabling sharing blocks new shares and permission changes, but existing shared access remains."

So to remove someone, remove that person in the item itself.

**Share with a group, not a list.** A list of names goes stale. A **group** is a named list of people that IT keeps up to date, such as "Claims team". It follows the team.

- **Claude:** "Sharing a project with a group requires an Enterprise plan and is currently in beta." (**Beta** means an early version that can still change.)
  - "Access follows group membership, so members who join the group later get access automatically, and members who leave lose it."
  - "Access changes can take up to five minutes to apply."
  - A person who leaves a group loses access "unless they have access another way."
- **ChatGPT:** a group helps, but the [groups page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/groups-and-provisioning) says group membership "doesn't replace seat assignments". A **seat** is one paid login on a plan.

**When a person leaves, the vendors differ.**

- **Claude.** The [Claude page on removed users](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12053672-what-happens-to-a-user-s-data-when-they-are-removed-from-a-team-or-enterprise-organization) says:
  - "If the project's visibility was set to private, other members of the organization will not be able to access it once the team member has been removed". The fix has to come first: the person "will need to adjust the project's permissions" so everyone in the organization can edit it, or invite specific users with Can edit.
  - Chats they shared stop working: people see "Conversation not found."
  - If the same email is added back later, "previous chats, projects, and skills will be restored."
- **ChatGPT.** The [user lifecycle page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/user-lifecycle):
  - "When a member leaves, the workspace automatically reassigns their projects and custom GPT ownership to a workspace owner."
  - But: "Reassigning a project or GPT doesn't transfer the former member's private conversations or files".
  - And: "Removing workspace access isn't the same as deleting content."

**Remove leavers at the source.** Many companies add and remove people in an **identity provider**: the central system that holds everyone's work login. The ChatGPT groups page warns: "If you remove the member only from the workspace, a later sync can restore access."

Our comparison: sharing a helper is like giving out office keys. A group is a key card the door reads every day. A name list is a pile of metal keys that nobody collects.

## Worked example

### Step 1: Robin's plan

```text
1. Switch "Claims letters" from Private to Public.
2. Keep the 14 old invites, just in case.
3. Send the outside lawyer a public link to the
   "Smith claim" chat.
```

What goes wrong:

- Step 1 shows the claims templates and tone rules to all 120 staff, not only the claims team.
- Step 2 leaves old access in place. It also hides who really has access.
- Step 3 cannot work: on Enterprise, public links are not available, and outside emails cannot be invited.

### Step 2: the better setup

```text
Project:   "Claims letters" (Private)
Shared with:
  group "Claims team"     Can view
  Robin, Lee (leads)      Can edit
Direct invites: none, except the two leads
Joiners:   IT adds them to "Claims team"
Leavers:   IT removes them from "Claims team"
           (wait up to 5 minutes, then check)
Lawyer:    no Claude share. Robin sends the final
           letter text by the firm's usual email.
```

The Claude docs say an admin must first turn on "Share resources with this group" for that group. Our advice: ask IT before the switch.

### Step 3: the hard case

**The hard case is the 14 old invites.** Robin wants to keep them "just in case". But the Claude docs say a person who leaves a group keeps access to a project if they "have access another way". A direct invite is another way.

So picture a temp who was invited by name in June and then added to the group. In September the temp leaves, and IT removes them from the group. The temp can still open the project through the old invite.

The fix: after the group share works, remove every direct invite except the two leads. Then the group is the only door, and IT controls it.

### Step 4: why it is better

- **Only the claims team sees the project,** not all 120 staff.
- **Joiners and leavers are handled once,** in the group, by IT.
- **No hidden access** stays behind from old invites.
- **The lawyer gets only the final text,** not the chat.

## Common mistakes

1. **Expecting a shared chat to show the data behind it.** Readers see only the final answers, not raw connector data. Fix: send the source documents the reader needs, in your normal way.
2. **Pasting a private summary onto a shared page.** Everyone who collaborates on that page can read it. Fix: check the page's people before you paste.
3. **Thinking a group gives a ChatGPT seat.** Membership does not replace a seat. Fix: assign seats as a separate step.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

Say if each claim is right or wrong, and why.

```text
1. "I posted a Gemini share link in a public forum.
   I deleted the link, so the forum post is gone."
2. "Our Claude Team colleague left last month. The
   chat links that person shared still open for us."
3. "A Claude Team member left and is back, with the
   same work email. Their old projects come back."
```

### Answer

1. **Wrong.** The Gemini docs say deleting the link "doesn't delete any posts with the link that you've made on third-party sites". Delete the forum post too.
2. **Wrong.** The Claude docs say the team then sees "Conversation not found."
3. **Right.** The Claude docs say previous chats, projects and skills are restored when the same email is added back.

## Recap

- A shared chat is a snapshot. Claude Team and Enterprise keep shares inside the company; a Gemini share link is public.
- Turning sharing off stops new shares, not old ones. Share with groups, and remove old direct invites, so one place controls access.
- Plan for leavers: a private Claude project closes unless someone else gets Can edit first; ChatGPT moves ownership to a workspace owner, but not private chats. Remove leavers in the identity provider too.
