## Why it matters

A good helper spreads fast. Someone shares a chat, then a project, then a plugin, and soon ten people depend on it. Two things can go wrong. The wrong people see it, or the one person who owns it leaves and it disappears. This chapter covers both: how sharing works, and who owns what when a person leaves.

Product names and limits below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026). They change often; check the page before you buy.

## Sharing a chat shares a snapshot

When you share a chat, people see a copy of it as it was at that moment.

- **Anthropic:** "Sharing creates a snapshot of the chat at the moment you share it." People you invite don't see "Messages you send after sharing", unless you update the shared chat. They also don't see "Raw data from connectors or MCP tool calls, only Claude's final responses". "Shared chats are view only."
- **Google:** a shared Gemini chat "shows the chat as it appeared when you created the public link."

Who can see it differs a lot:

- **Claude on Team and Enterprise plans:** "sharing stays inside your organization". "Emails outside your organization can't be invited." "Public links aren't available."
- **Gemini:** sharing "creates a public link to that chat. Anyone with the link can read the chat, even if you didn't share the link with them directly." They can also "reshare it with others". Deleting the link later "doesn't delete any posts with the link that you've made on third-party sites."

Admins hold the switch. Anthropic: on Team and Enterprise plans, "Admins can turn off chat sharing for the organization, see and revoke shared chats, and view sharing activity in the audit log." An **audit log** is the record of who did what. Google: "Your Workspace administrator can turn conversation sharing on or off."

Our advice: before you share a chat, read it as if a stranger will read it.

## Sharing a helper

A shared helper is more than a snapshot. People use it every day.

- **Claude projects** on Team and Enterprise plans are **Public** ("Everyone in your organization can view and use the project") or **Private** ("Only invited members can view and use the project"). Each invited person gets **Can view** or **Can edit**. Your own chats inside a shared project stay private: "your chats will remain private unless you choose to share them."
- **OpenAI plugins** (chapter 2): "Your new plugin starts private." Sharing and installing are two steps: "Sharing access and installation are separate steps." Sharing needs its own permission: "To share with people or groups, you need Share plugins access."
- **ChatGPT Space pages** (OpenAI's shared pages that teams draft and edit) are shared "with people or available workspace teams", with view or edit access. One warning: "Content copied or summarized onto a page is visible to its collaborators."

## Turning sharing off is not taking it back

Anthropic and OpenAI describe the same pattern for some admin switches.

- **Anthropic:** when an Owner turns off project sharing, "Projects that are already shared stay shared, and users who already have access keep it." One exception: "Public projects become private, so people who only had access because a project was public lose it."
- **OpenAI** (Enterprise, for sharing Library files and folders): "Disabling sharing blocks new shares and permission changes, but existing shared access remains."

Our example: a team finds a pricing project shared with 30 invited people. Turning off sharing for the company does not fix it. Someone must open that project and remove the people who should not have it.

## Share with a group, not a list

People join and leave teams. A list of names goes stale; a group follows the team.

- **Anthropic** (Enterprise plans; still in beta, so it may change): when you share a project with a group, "Access follows group membership, so members who join the group later get access automatically, and members who leave lose it."
- **OpenAI:** a group is useful, but it is not everything. "Group membership doesn't replace seat assignments". It also does not "grant workspace feature permissions by itself". A **seat** is one paid login on a chat plan (chapter 1).

Later on the map, Swarm Spire, chapter 3 (Roles & skills), covers seats, admin roles and custom roles in depth.

## When a person leaves

This is where helpers can close to everyone. The two vendors work in different ways.

**Anthropic.** "If the project's visibility was set to private, other members of the organization will not be able to access it once the team member has been removed". Chats they shared stop working too: after removal, people see "Conversation not found." The fix must happen before the person leaves: they "will need to adjust the project's permissions so either everyone in the organization can edit it, or invite specific users and grant them 'Can edit' permissions." If the same email is added back later, "previous chats, projects, and skills will be restored."

**OpenAI.** "When a member leaves, the workspace automatically reassigns their projects and custom GPT ownership to a workspace owner." But "Reassigning a project or GPT doesn't transfer the former member's private conversations or files". And "Removing workspace access isn't the same as deleting content."

One more OpenAI warning. Many companies add and remove people from a central **identity provider**: the company system that holds everyone's work login. If that system still lists the person, removing them only in ChatGPT does not stick: "If you remove the member only from the workspace, a later sync can restore access."

## One owner card per shared helper

Our advice: keep one line per shared item, and update it when someone leaves.

```
Item:        "Proposal helper" (Claude project)
Shared with: 6 sales reps, Can view; Ben, Can edit
Owner:       Ana (sales ops)
Backup:      Ben
Leaves?      Owner hands over Can edit first
```

The backup owner matters most on Claude: a private project with no second editor is closed to everyone else when its owner is removed.

## Key points

- A shared chat is a snapshot. Claude Team and Enterprise keep it inside the company; a Gemini share link is public.
- A shared helper needs view or edit rights. An OpenAI plugin starts private, and sharing and installing are separate.
- Turning sharing off stops new shares. Invited people keep access until someone removes them; public projects become private.
- Share with groups where you can. A group does not replace a seat.
- When a person leaves, a Claude private project closes to others unless shared with Can edit first; OpenAI moves ownership to a workspace owner, but not private chats.
- Remove leavers in the identity provider, or a later sync can bring them back.
