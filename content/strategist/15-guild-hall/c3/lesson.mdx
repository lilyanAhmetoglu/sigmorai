## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Explain what a connected app lets an assistant see and do, and whose permissions it uses.
- Plan a rollout: who turns it on, who signs in, and which actions need a person to approve.
- Find the real cause when a connected app shows something it "should not".

## The problem

A logistics firm with 60 staff uses Claude on a Team plan. Last month an **admin** (the person who manages the team's account settings) turned on the Google Drive connector. Last week a junior route planner asked Claude: "What do our drivers earn on average?" Claude answered from a salary spreadsheet.

The head of HR is angry. The message to the AI lead says: "The AI is leaking pay data. Turn off every connected app today. When we turn them back on, write 'never show salaries' in the instructions."

The planners also want Claude to add and move delivery slots in the shared Google Calendar. The AI lead must answer HR and plan the next step. The product facts below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026).

## The idea

A **connected app** is our name for a link between an AI assistant and a tool your team already uses, such as Google Drive, Gmail, Slack or a calendar. Each vendor uses its own word. The Claude docs say **connectors**. The Gemini docs say **Connected Apps**. In ChatGPT, the connection comes inside a **plugin**.

**It can read, and it can act.** The [Claude docs](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11176164-use-connectors-to-extend-claude-s-capabilities) say: "Connectors let Claude access your apps and services, retrieve your data, and take actions within connected services." The [ChatGPT docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/plugins) give examples, such as: "Install the Slack plugin to summarize channels or draft replies." The [Gemini docs](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13695044) say: "With your permission, Gemini can also help you take actions, like editing and managing your content in other apps." So treat **read** actions (search, summarize) and **write** actions (send, create, change, delete) as two different risks.

**It sees what the person can see.** This is the key rule.

- The Claude docs: "Claude inherits each person's permissions from the connected service. If someone can't access a specific file, channel, or record in the source system, the connector can't reach it from Claude either."
- The [ChatGPT migration guide](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/migrate-custom-gpts): "signing in to Google Drive through ChatGPT lets its plugin work with documents your account can already access. It doesn't unlock other files or add Google Drive permissions."

**The exception is a shared login.** The Claude docs: "Custom connectors that use a shared credential reach whatever that credential can access." A **credential** is a login. A shared one is one login used for everyone. Then each person reaches what that login can reach, not only their own records.

**Turned on is not signed in.** On Claude Team and Enterprise plans, an Owner enables a connector first.

- The Claude docs: "Enabling a connector makes it available to your team, but it doesn't automatically grant anyone access. Each person still needs to authenticate individually before they can use it." To **authenticate** is to sign in to the other app and approve the link. (One exception is in **beta**, an early version that can still change: with Enterprise-managed auth, an organization can authorize a connector once, and members get access on first login.)
- The [ChatGPT plugin controls page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/apps-and-connectors) agrees: "Installing a plugin for everyone doesn't, by itself, provide a shared account."
- For Gemini with a work account, "access to apps must be enabled by your Workspace administrator" ([Gemini docs](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14959807)). If one request needs two apps and one of them is not enabled, the same page says "neither of the actions will be completed".

**Admins can limit actions.** In Claude Team and Enterprise:

- Owners "can limit which actions a connected service can take across your organization".
- For each tool, they "select Always allow, Needs approval, or Blocked".
- The choice "applies org-wide to everyone using the connector—individual users can't override it."
- These limits "never" grant "more access than the source system permits".

Some send and share actions already ask first. The [Claude page on Google Workspace](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors) says, for Gmail sending and for sharing or moving Drive files: "By default, Claude asks for your approval before each of these actions."

The ChatGPT plugin controls page gives rollout advice:

- "Whatever the initial set, start with read actions."
- Before write actions, it lists checks: an owner, scopes, data access, and "a recovery path".
- A record to keep: "For each connected service, record the business owner, permitted data, approved read or write actions, authentication method, and a support or removal contact."

**Projects and sharing on Claude.** The Claude docs add two rules for Team and Enterprise: "Connectors are only available in private projects." And: "Chats with synced content can't be shared."

**Check the source.** An answer from a connected app can still be wrong. The Gemini docs: "Gemini Apps can hallucinate responses or provide outdated information, like from an older email when there's a more recent one. To check the information, click and review the sources listed after the response." Claude's answers from Google Workspace include "citations indicating which emails, calendar events, or documents were used as sources".

**Custom connectors start in the cloud.** The Claude docs say custom connectors are reached "from Anthropic's cloud infrastructure, not from your local machine". (The **cloud** means the vendor's own servers, not your office.) So the server must be reachable over the public internet, or your firewall must let in Anthropic's IP ranges.

**The other app has its own rules.** The Claude docs: "Connected services process data on their own infrastructure, under their own terms". The ChatGPT docs: "When ChatGPT sends data through an MCP server, that service's terms and privacy policy apply." (**MCP**, the Model Context Protocol, is an open standard for linking AI apps to tools.)

Our comparison: a connected app is like giving a new assistant your own office key. It opens the doors your key opens, and no others. If a door should be locked, lock the door.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak plan

```text
HR's plan
1. Turn off every connected app for everyone, today.
2. Later, turn Drive back on. Add to the instructions:
   "never show salaries".
3. Turn on Calendar so Claude can add, move and delete
   delivery slots. No approvals, to save time.
```

What goes wrong:

- Step 1 stops all the useful reads (route notes, depot manuals) and does not touch the real cause.
- Step 2 is a request to the model, not a control. The file is still in reach.
- Step 3 turns on write actions on a shared calendar with no person in the loop, and with no way back named.

### Step 2: find the real cause

Claude uses each person's own Drive permissions. So the planner could already open that spreadsheet in Drive. The connector only found it faster. The leak is in Drive sharing: the salary folder is open to "anyone in the company".

### Step 3: the good plan

Our advice: write the card before you turn the app on, and name a way to undo changes before any write action.

```text
Card: Google Drive
  Owner:       Aylin (operations)
  Data:        route notes, depot manuals, ops folders
  Actions:     search and read: Always allow
               share, move, trash: Needs approval
  Sign-in:     each person's own Google account
  Removal:     it-help@example.com
  First:       HR closes the salary folder in Drive

Card: Google Calendar
  Owner:       Aylin (operations)
  Data:        "Delivery slots" calendar
  Actions:     view events: Always allow
               create, update, delete: Needs approval
  Sign-in:     each person's own Google account
  Recovery:    weekly calendar export; slot changes
               are logged by the planner who approves
```

### Step 4: the hard case

**The hard case is HR's demand to keep the salary rule in the instructions.** It sounds safe. But a sentence in the instructions only asks the model. The spreadsheet stays reachable for everyone who can open it in Drive, through Claude or straight in Drive.

The fix is in the source system. HR limits the salary folder to HR staff. Then nobody else can reach it through the connector either, because the connector follows each person's own access. No prompt line is needed.

### Step 5: why it is better

- **The real leak is closed,** in Drive, for every tool, not only for Claude.
- **Useful reads come back** the same day.
- **Calendar changes wait for a person,** so a wrong slot change is caught before it happens.
- **Each app has a card** with an owner and a way to remove it.

## Common mistakes

1. **Forgetting the other service's terms.** The connected app handles your data under its own rules. Fix: read that service's terms before you connect it.
2. **No record of who owns a connection.** Nobody knows whom to ask when it breaks. Fix: keep one card per app, with an owner and a removal contact.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

Say if each claim is right or wrong, and why.

```text
1. "In Claude we set 'create documents' in Drive to
   Always allow. Now members can edit files they can
   only view in Drive."
2. "On my work Gemini account I asked for a calendar
   event plus a Tasks reminder. Our admin has not
   enabled Tasks, but the event will still be made."
3. "Our new custom connector points to a server on our
   private office network. It works, because Claude
   Desktop runs on an office laptop."
```

### Answer

1. **Wrong.** The Claude docs say these settings never grant more access than the source system permits. A person still needs edit rights in Drive.
2. **Wrong.** The Gemini docs say that if one of the needed services is not enabled, "neither of the actions will be completed".
3. **Wrong.** The Claude docs say custom connectors are reached "from Anthropic's cloud infrastructure, not from your local machine". A laptop in the office does not change that.

## Recap

- A connected app reads from and acts in your team's tools, using each person's own permissions. A shared login reaches whatever that login can reach.
- An admin turning an app on is not access: each person signs in. Admins can set each action to allow, approve or block, and start with reads.
- Fix access in the source system, keep one card per app, and check the cited source before you act on an answer.
