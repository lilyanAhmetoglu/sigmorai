## Why it matters

A custom helper knows the files you upload. A **connected app** lets the assistant reach into the tools your team already uses, such as Google Drive, Gmail, Slack or a calendar. Each vendor uses its own word: Anthropic says **connectors**, and Google says **Connected Apps**. OpenAI calls the connection an app, which you install as part of a **plugin**. Product names and limits below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026). They change often; check the page before you buy.

## What a connected app can do

- **Anthropic:** "Connectors let Claude access your apps and services, retrieve your data, and take actions within connected services." Its examples: "connect Claude to Linear to create issues, to Slack to send messages, or to Google Drive to search your files."
- **OpenAI:** you can "Install the Google Drive plugin to work across Drive, Docs, Sheets, and Slides" or "Install the Slack plugin to summarize channels or draft replies."
- **Google:** "Gemini Apps can connect to other apps to complete your request". "With your permission, Gemini can also help you take actions, like editing and managing your content in other apps."

So a connected app can do two kinds of thing: **read** (search, summarize) and **act** (send, create, change). MCP Bridgelands, chapter 1, made the same split: reading is not the same as acting. Behind many of these connections sits MCP (Model Context Protocol), which MCP Bridgelands, chapter 2, explains. This chapter is about what a team turns on, not how the protocol works.

## What the assistant may read

Anthropic and OpenAI state the key rule the same way: the assistant sees only what the signed-in person can already see.

- **Anthropic:** "Claude inherits each person's permissions from the connected service. If someone can't access a specific file, channel, or record in the source system, the connector can't reach it from Claude either."
- **OpenAI:** "signing in to Google Drive through ChatGPT lets its plugin work with documents your account can already access. It doesn't unlock other files or add Google Drive permissions."
- **Google:** the Gemini app also has its own reading limits. With work apps, Google says Gemini can't "Access comments or images in Gmail" or "Access pictures and videos in your Drive."

One exception to watch. Anthropic: "Custom connectors that use a shared credential reach whatever that credential can access." A **shared credential** is one login used for everyone, not each person's own. Then the assistant may see more than the person asking.

Our example: an intern connects Drive. The finance folder is closed to the intern in Drive, so it stays closed in the assistant too.

## Turning an app on for a team

Turning an app on for the team is not the same as giving each person access.

- **Anthropic:** on Team and Enterprise plans, an Owner enables the connector first. "Enabling a connector makes it available to your team, but it doesn't automatically grant anyone access. Each person still needs to authenticate individually before they can use it." To **authenticate** is to sign in to the other app and approve the link.
- **OpenAI:** "Making an MCP server or plugin available in ChatGPT doesn't grant access to files, records, or actions in the connected service." And: "Installing a plugin for everyone doesn't, by itself, provide a shared account."
- **Google:** "For users with a work or school Google Account, access to apps must be enabled by your Workspace administrator."

## Read first, act later

Admins can limit what a connected app may do.

- **Anthropic:** owners on Team and Enterprise plans "can limit which actions a connected service can take across your organization". Its examples include "Allow Claude to search and summarize email, but prevent it from sending messages." Each tool can be set to "Always allow, Needs approval, or Blocked". This "applies org-wide to everyone using the connector—individual users can't override it."
- **Anthropic's default for send and share actions:** for Gmail sending and for sharing or moving Drive files, "By default, Claude asks for your approval before each of these actions."
- **OpenAI's rollout advice:** "Whatever the initial set, start with read actions. Before enabling write actions, identify the plugin owner, review MCP server scopes and service permissions, confirm data access, and document external effects and a recovery path."

Some limits are built in. Google lists actions Gemini can't do with work apps. For example, in Gmail it cannot "Create an email draft or delete emails in Gmail."

## One card per connected app

OpenAI's Enterprise guide gives a simple record to keep: "For each connected service, record the business owner, permitted data, approved read or write actions, authentication method, and a support or removal contact."

Our example card:

```
App:          Google Drive
Owner:        Priya (sales ops)
Data:         Sales and Clients folders only
Actions:      read and search; no share, move or delete
Sign-in:      each person's own Google account
Remove/help:  it-help@example.com
```

MCP Bridgelands, chapter 1, used the same idea for integrations: one card each.

## Check the source

An answer that uses a connected app can still be wrong. Google: "Gemini Apps can hallucinate responses or provide outdated information, like from an older email when there's a more recent one. To check the information, click and review the sources listed after the response." Anthropic says Claude's answer from Google Workspace "includes citations indicating which emails, calendar events, or documents were used as sources". Our advice: open the cited email or file before you act on the answer.

## Where the data goes

The other app has its own rules. Anthropic: "Connected services process data on their own infrastructure, under their own terms". OpenAI: "When ChatGPT sends data through an MCP server, that service's terms and privacy policy apply."

Two more Anthropic rules for Team and Enterprise plans: "Connectors are only available in private projects" and "Chats with synced content can't be shared."

## Key points

- Connected apps (connectors, plugins, Connected Apps) let the assistant read from and act in your team's tools.
- The assistant sees only what the signed-in person can see. A shared credential can see more.
- An admin turning an app on is not access. Each person still signs in.
- Our advice: start with read actions. Block send, share and delete, or require approval for them.
- Keep one card per app: owner, data, actions, sign-in, removal contact.
- Check the cited source before you act on an answer.
