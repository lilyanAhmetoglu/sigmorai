## Why it matters

Prompt Forest, chapter 6 (Team playbooks), turned good prompts into a shared, tested play. This chapter puts a play inside the AI app, so nobody has to paste it. A **custom helper** is a saved setup with standing instructions and files that many chats reuse. The name is ours, not a vendor's. Two of these products are changing right now, and this chapter says how. Product names and limits below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026). They change often; check the page before you buy.

## What a custom helper holds

Every product in this chapter stores the same two things:

1. **Standing instructions.** How the assistant should behave in every chat: tone, role, format.
2. **Files.** The reference material it should use: a style guide, a price list, a policy.

Our example: a support team's "Refund helper" has the instruction "Answer in under 120 words, friendly and direct, and quote the policy section you used", plus one file, the refund policy.

## Claude projects

Anthropic: "Projects allow you to create self-contained workspaces with their own chat histories and knowledge bases."

- **Files.** "Anything you upload to this space will be used across all of your chats within that project."
- **Instructions.** You click "Set project instructions", and "Claude will use these instructions for all the chats within the project." Anthropic's example: "instructing Claude to use a more formal tone or answer questions from the perspective of a specific role or industry."

Two details catch people out:

- **The name and description are only labels.** When you create a project, you "Give your project a name and description (note that Claude will not have access to these details)." So a rule written in the description does nothing. Put it in the instructions.
- **Chats don't share what they learn.** "Context is not shared across chats within a project unless the information is added into the project knowledge base." If one chat learns a new fact, add it to the project's files, or the next chat won't know it.

Limits differ by plan. Anthropic says projects "are available to all users", and "Free users can create a maximum of five projects."

## ChatGPT projects

OpenAI's projects work the same way. "Each project has a **Chats** section that lists project chats and a **Sources** section for uploaded files and connected context. Project instructions apply across its chats."

One limit on the web: "A ChatGPT project doesn't provide direct access to a folder on your computer, so upload or connect the sources you want ChatGPT to use."

OpenAI also says when to skip a project: "Start a chat without a project when the work is self-contained and doesn't need shared project context."

## Custom GPTs are moving to plugins

OpenAI's older custom helper is the **custom GPT**. In OpenAI's table, with a custom GPT "You add instructions to guide the GPT's responses" and "You upload reference files as GPT knowledge." That is changing. OpenAI's guide for ChatGPT Enterprise workspaces says: "We're transitioning custom GPTs to plugins." It adds: "public GPTs in affected Enterprise workspaces are included, but public sharing and timelines for other plans may differ."

A **plugin** is OpenAI's newer home for a saved helper. In OpenAI's words, a plugin is "a reusable package for a workflow that can include skills, apps, or both."

What the move does to a GPT, in OpenAI's words:

- "Migration turns your GPT's instructions into a skill and brings over its knowledge files and connected apps."
- "Rebuild custom actions separately." (OpenAI: "Custom actions connect the GPT to external services.")
- "The selected model does not carry over".
- "Your new plugin starts private." You must share it again.
- "The original GPT becomes read-only after migration."

OpenAI's advice before you switch: "Ask owners to save a few familiar prompts and good results, including one harder case, so they can compare the replacement."

## Gems, and their move to skills

Google's custom helper in the Gemini app is the **Gem**: in Google's words, "customized versions of Gemini" with their own instructions and files.

- **Instructions.** To create one, you "Enter a name and instructions for your Gem."
- **Files.** Under "Knowledge", you can upload files or add them from Google Drive. "If you add a file from your Drive, Gemini will use the most recent version of the file. If you make changes to the file, these changes will be reflected in your Gem."

Gems are changing too. Google's page says: "Starting in November 2026, Gems will go away on personal Google accounts and automatically transition to skills."

## Where this leaves skills

Both moves end in the same place: the instructions become a **skill**. Prompt Forest, chapter 6, and Swarm Spire, chapter 3 (Roles & skills), already cover skills and how to manage them as shared assets. This chapter does not repeat them.

The product that holds your helper can change under you. Our advice: keep the master copy of each helper's instructions and files in a place your team controls. Then a move from GPT to plugin, or from Gem to skill, is a copy and a test, not a rebuild from memory.

## Set one up well

Our checklist for any custom helper:

1. **One job per helper.** "Refund replies", not "Everything support does".
2. **Rules go in the instructions.** Never in the name or description.
3. **Facts go in files.** A price list changes; keep it in a file you can swap.
4. **Name an owner.** Someone updates the files when the policy changes.
5. **Keep test prompts.** Save a few normal requests and one hard one, as OpenAI suggests, and run them after every change.

## Key points

- A custom helper is a saved setup: standing instructions plus files.
- Claude projects and ChatGPT projects apply their instructions and files to every chat inside them.
- Claude can't see a project's name or description. Rules go in the instructions.
- Claude chats in one project don't share what they learn unless you add it to the project files.
- OpenAI's Enterprise guide moves custom GPTs to plugins: instructions become a skill; custom actions must be rebuilt.
- Google says Gems on personal accounts move to skills starting in November 2026.
- Keep your master copy and your test prompts outside the tool.
