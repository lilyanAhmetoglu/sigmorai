## Why it matters

An AI bill has more than one line. You pay for logins, and more and more you also pay for how much people use. If you only count logins, the usage line surprises you. If you set no limits, one busy month can cost more than the whole year's plan.

Product names and limits below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026). They change often; check the page before you buy.

## Seats and usage are two different things

As chapter 1 said, a **seat** is one paid login on a chat plan. **Usage** is how much work the AI does for that person: messages, files, agent tasks. Vendors charge for these in different ways.

- **Claude Team plan:** each seat includes some usage. There are two seat types: **Standard** and **Premium**, which has "Everything in Standard, plus higher usage limits". Anthropic lists a Standard seat at "$25 per member per month, billed monthly" or "$20 per member per month, billed annually" (US prices, before tax). You can mix seat types: give Premium to the few heavy users.
- **Claude Enterprise (usage-based plans):** "a fixed seat fee and separate usage charges". The seat fee "doesn't include any usage."
- **ChatGPT Enterprise (credit-based agreements):** "Work activity draws from a shared pool of workspace credits." OpenAI adds: "Existing subscription or seat fees are separate from an estimate of credits consumed."

The API is a third bill. Chapter 1 showed that Anthropic and OpenAI bill it apart from the chat plans.

## Usage limits: whose and how big

A **usage limit** is the most a person can use in a time window before they must wait or pay more.

- **Anthropic (Team plan):** "Usage limits on Team plans are per-member, rather than applied to the team as a whole." One heavy user does not block the others. Every Claude product counts: "your usage of all different Claude product surfaces (claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Desktop) counts towards the same usage limit."
- **Google (personal Google AI plans):** Gemini Apps have "compute-based usage limits" that "factor in the complexity of your prompt, the models and features you use, and the length of your chat." The limit "refreshes every 5 hours until you reach your weekly limit."

What uses the most? OpenAI's list of the most variable patterns: "workflows that run frequently, retrieve or process large amounts of information, call multiple tools or apps, retry after failures, or produce large artifacts." Anthropic: "Tools and connectors are token-intensive".

Our example: a weekly scheduled agent task that reads 200 files will use far more than a person asking ten quick questions.

## When someone runs out

- **Anthropic:** an Owner can turn on **usage credits**, so members "continue working with Claude, Cowork, and Claude Code after reaching their included usage limits". This extra use "will be billed at standard API pricing rates". If usage credits are off, members "will need to wait for them to reset."
- **OpenAI:** when the credit pool is empty, it depends on your contract. If overage is allowed, use "may incur charges at your contracted overage rate". If not, "Eligible features can pause until credits become available or the applicable limit changes."

## Spend controls: caps, not alerts

A cap stops spending. An alert only tells you about it.

- **Anthropic:** Owners can set "a monthly spend limit on the entire organization's usage credits" and a monthly limit for each member. When a member reaches it, this "will automatically pause their usage credits until the end of the month." One catch: "It's possible to slightly exceed your defined spend limit", because a request is checked before it runs, not after.
- **OpenAI:** admins can set monthly per-user limits, group limits and a workspace overage limit. Read the small print:
  - "Alerts notify; they don't stop spending."
  - "**No limit** isn't a zero-spend cap." An overage limit of zero is what "prevents the workspace from continuing into overage".
  - "An individual user override takes precedence over group and workspace defaults." And "When a person belongs to more than one group, the highest applicable group limit applies."
  - "Users can view their own usage and, if enabled, request more credits, but they can't change assigned limits."

## Unused seats still cost money

Removing a person does not shrink the bill. Anthropic (Team plan): "Removing members does not trigger an immediate credit or refund. The seat becomes available to assign to another member. To reduce your bill, you'll need to reduce your plan's total seat allocation." That reduction "takes effect at your next renewal".

So check who actually uses their seat. Anthropic's admin analytics include a chart of "Active members and assigned seats", and the spend table has a month-to-date column "so you can track members' usage patterns and optimize seat assignments." On Team plans, Owners and Primary Owners see analytics; on Enterprise, "Admins can view all analytics except Spend."

OpenAI warns against reading too much into the dashboard: "Don't treat aggregated analytics as exact per-workflow cost attribution."

## Write the monthly line

Our advice: for each tool, write one line a finance person can check.

```
Tool:     Chat assistant, team plan (made-up prices)
Seats:    16 standard x $30 + 4 premium x $120 = $960
Usage:    extra-usage credits, org cap $300/month
API:      none (separate bill if added)
Total:    $960 fixed, up to $1,260 with the cap
Owner:    Dana (operations), reviews seats each quarter
```

OpenAI says estimates help you plan, but they "aren't fixed per-user prices or guaranteed billing amounts." So compare the estimate with the real invoice after the first month.

MCP Bridgelands, chapter 5 (Total cost), adds the costs a price page can't show. Eval Arena, chapter 3 (KPIs that matter), shows how to judge cost per finished task, not per seat.

## Key points

- A seat is one paid login. Usage is how much work the AI does. Plans charge for them in different ways.
- On Claude Team, usage limits are per member, and all Claude products share one limit.
- When a limit runs out, extra use is billed (usage credits, overage) or it pauses. Know which one your plan does.
- Set a cap. Alerts only notify, and "No limit" is not a zero cap.
- Removing a person frees the seat but does not cut the bill until you reduce seats at renewal.
- Write one monthly line per tool: seats, usage cap, API, total and owner.
