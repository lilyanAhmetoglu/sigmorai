## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Tell seat costs from usage costs, and read how each vendor bills them.
- Choose the right mix of seat types from real usage data.
- Set caps that stop spending, not only alerts that report it.

## The problem

An architecture studio with 18 staff uses Claude on a Team plan, billed annually, with 18 Standard seats. Usage credits are on, so people can keep working after they reach their limit. The plan renews on 1 December.

The extra usage bill keeps growing. Last month it was about $520. The studio owner, Jo, has a plan: "Put all 18 people on Premium seats. Then we never pay for extra usage again."

The finance lead asks the operations manager to check the numbers before renewal. The product facts below come from each vendor's help pages (October 2026).

## The idea

An AI bill has two main lines. A **seat** is one paid login on a plan. **Usage** is how much work the AI does for that person: messages, files, agent tasks. Vendors charge for them in different ways.

**Claude Team: seats include usage.** The [Claude Team plan page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9266767-what-is-the-team-plan) lists two seat types:

- **Standard:** "$25 per member per month, billed monthly" or "$20 per member per month, billed annually".
- **Premium:** "$125 per member per month, billed monthly" or "$100 per member per month, billed annually".

These are US prices, before tax. Standard seats "include 1.25x the Pro plan's per-session usage allowance". Premium seats "include 6.25x the Pro plan's per-session usage allowance". Both also have a weekly limit. The [seats page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12004354-purchase-and-manage-seats-on-team-plans) says: "Assign Premium seats to power users who need more capacity, while keeping other team members on Standard seats."

**Claude Enterprise, usage-based: seats do not include usage.** The [Claude Enterprise billing page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11526368-how-am-i-billed-for-my-enterprise-plan) describes "a fixed seat fee and separate usage charges". The seat fee "doesn't include any usage."

**ChatGPT Enterprise, credit-based.** A **credit** is a prepaid unit of usage. The [ChatGPT usage and cost page](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/chatgpt-work-usage-and-cost) says Work activity "draws from a shared pool" of workspace credits.

**Whose limit is it?** A **usage limit** is the most a person can use before they must wait or pay more.

- On Claude Team, "Usage limits on Team plans are per-member, rather than applied to the team as a whole."
- All products count together: "your usage of all different Claude product surfaces (claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Desktop) counts towards the same usage limit" ([Claude limits page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11647753-how-do-usage-and-length-limits-work)).
- Google's [Gemini limits page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805) describes "compute-based usage limits" that "factor in the complexity of your prompt, the models and features you use, and the length of your chat".
- The Gemini limit "refreshes every 5 hours until you reach your weekly limit."

**What makes cost jump?** The [ChatGPT admin FAQ](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/work-admin-faq): "The highest-variance patterns are often workflows that run frequently, retrieve or process large amounts of information, call multiple tools or apps, retry after failures, or produce large artifacts." The Claude limits page: "Tools and connectors are token-intensive".

**When someone runs out.**

- **Claude usage credits** let members keep working past their limit. The [usage credits page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12005970-manage-usage-credits-for-team-and-seat-based-enterprise-plans): "Your subsequent usage will be billed at standard API pricing rates". (The **API** is the pay-per-use way developers call the model.) If credits are off, members "will need to wait for them to reset."
- **ChatGPT overage** is paid use past the credits you bought. If it is allowed, use may be charged "at your contracted overage rate". If not, "Eligible features can pause until credits become available or the applicable limit changes."

**Caps stop spending. Alerts do not.**

- **Claude:**
  - Owners can set "a monthly spend limit on the entire organization's usage credits", and a limit for each member.
  - A member who hits that limit has their credits paused: it will "automatically pause their usage credits until the end of the month."
  - But: "It's possible to slightly exceed your defined spend limit".
- **ChatGPT:**
  - "Alerts notify; they don't stop spending."
  - "an overage limit of zero prevents the workspace from continuing into overage, while No limit isn't a zero-spend cap."
- **ChatGPT limit order:**
  - "An individual user override takes precedence over group and workspace defaults."
  - "When a person belongs to more than one group, the highest applicable group limit applies."
  - Users "can't change assigned limits."

**Seat changes have timing rules.** On Claude Team:

- "Removing members does not trigger an immediate credit or refund."
- To pay less, "you'll need to reduce your plan's total seat allocation", and that "takes effect at your next renewal".
- Upgrades are quick: a Standard to Premium move is "prorated and charged immediately for the price difference".
- The other way is slow: "Moving a member from Premium to Standard takes effect at your next renewal".

**Use the data you have.**

- The Claude [analytics page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12883420-view-usage-analytics-for-team-and-enterprise-plans) has a chart of "Active members and assigned seats".
- The usage credits page has a per-member **MTD Spend** column (month-to-date spend), "so you can track members' usage patterns and optimize seat assignments."
- But the ChatGPT admin FAQ warns: "Don't treat aggregated analytics as exact per-workflow cost attribution."
- And the ChatGPT cost page says estimates "aren't fixed per-user prices or guaranteed billing amounts."

Our comparison: seats are the rent, usage is the electricity. A bigger flat does not help if one heater runs all night.

## Worked example

### Step 1: Jo's plan, with our maths

```text
Now (per month, US list prices, annual):
  18 Standard x $20           = $360
  usage credits, last month   ~ $520   (made up)
  total                       ~ $880

Jo's plan:
  18 Premium x $100           = $1,800
```

Jo's plan more than doubles the bill, and most staff spend almost nothing past their Standard limit.

### Step 2: read the MTD Spend column

```text
Usage credits per member, last 3 months (made up)
  Kai     $190   $210   $175
  Rosa    $140   $160   $150
  Dev       $5   $240    $10   (one big competition entry)
  others  under $15 each, every month
```

Our maths: a Premium seat costs $80 a month more than Standard on an annual plan. Kai and Rosa each spend more than $80 in credits every month. A Premium seat gives them five times the Standard per-session allowance (6.25 divided by 1.25). So Premium is likely cheaper for them.

### Step 3: the new setup

```text
From renewal on 1 December:
  16 Standard x $20                     = $320
   2 Premium  x $100 (Kai, Rosa)        = $200
Usage credits stay on:
  each member limit                      $60
  organization limit                    $200
Most the plan can cost (our maths)    ~ $720
```

Our advice: after the first month, check the MTD Spend column again. If Kai still spends a lot of credits on Premium, look at the work, not only the seat.

### Step 4: the hard case

**The hard case is Dev.** One month cost $240, more than Kai's worst month. It looks like Dev needs Premium too.

But it was one big project. An upgrade is charged at once. Moving back to Standard waits until the next renewal. So a Premium seat for Dev means paying $80 more each month for a year of mostly light use.

The fix: keep Dev on Standard. Before the next big project, the Owner raises Dev's member limit for that month, and the organization limit too, if needed. Then the Owner sets them back.

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Seats follow real use,** not a guess.
- **Every dollar of extra usage has a cap,** per member and for the organization.
- **Short spikes stay on credits,** so the studio does not pay for a year of Premium.

## Common mistakes

1. **New joiners get the spare Premium seat.** People added automatically from the company's login system (the docs call this **JIT or SCIM provisioning**) are "automatically assigned to the highest-available seat type". Fix: map groups to seat types, or check each new member's tier.
2. **Reading the dashboard as an exact bill.** Analytics and estimates are guides. Fix: compare them with the real invoice each month.
3. **Comparing plans by message count.** Limits depend on the model, features and chat length, not only the number of messages. Fix: test with your real tasks for a month.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

Say if each claim is right or wrong, and why.

```text
1. "On our Claude Team plan we turned usage credits
   off. People at their limit keep working, just
   more slowly."
2. "Our Gemini limit refreshes every 5 hours, so a
   heavy user can never run out for the week."
3. "On Claude Team we upgrade Mia from Standard to
   Premium today. We pay the difference at once."
```

### Answer

1. **Wrong.** The Claude docs say that with credits off, members "will need to wait for them to reset."
2. **Wrong.** The Gemini docs say the limit refreshes every 5 hours "until you reach your weekly limit."
3. **Right.** The Claude seats page says the upgrade is prorated and charged at once for the price difference.

## Recap

- A seat is a login; usage is the work done. Claude Team seats include usage, usage-based Claude Enterprise seats do not, and ChatGPT credit plans draw from a shared pool.
- Set caps, because alerts only report. Know which limit wins, and that spend can go slightly over a cap.
- Match seat types to real use with the MTD Spend data. Upgrades are quick and charged at once; downgrades and seat cuts wait for renewal.
