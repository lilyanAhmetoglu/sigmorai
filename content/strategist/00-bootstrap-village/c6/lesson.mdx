## Your prompt leaves the building

When you use an AI model from a vendor, the text you type and the files you upload are sent to that vendor's systems to be processed. So before you paste anything, ask: **may this data go to this vendor, under these terms?**

Example: a recruiter pastes 30 CVs into an AI chat to compare candidates. Those CVs hold names, phone numbers and work history: personal data. Whether that is allowed depends on which tool and which account the recruiter used, and on company rules.

## Not every account has the same terms

The same vendor can treat data differently depending on the product and plan.

- **Business and developer use (the API).** OpenAI's docs say data sent to the OpenAI API is not used to train or improve its models unless you explicitly opt in. Anthropic's docs say retained API data is never used for model training without your express permission.
- **Personal plans.** Consumer apps have their own terms. Anthropic's docs point consumer plans to a separate consumer data retention policy, and list consumer plans (Free, Pro and Max) as not covered by its zero-data-retention option.

Example: the recruiter used a personal account on their phone instead of the company's approved tool. Even if the AI does the same task, the data terms are different, and the company may not have agreed to them.

## How long data is kept

"Not used for training" is not the same as "not stored". Vendors may keep some data for a time, for example to detect abuse.

- OpenAI's docs say abuse monitoring logs are kept for up to 30 days by default, unless the law requires longer or it is needed to protect against harm.
- Anthropic's docs say that even under special arrangements, it may keep data where the law requires it, or where its automated safety systems flag it. Flagged inputs and outputs may be kept for up to 2 years.

## Options for sensitive work

Both vendors offer stricter options for organizations that need them. These are set up by the company, usually by contract, not by one user.

- **Zero data retention (ZDR).** Under Anthropic's ZDR arrangement, prompts and responses are not stored after the response is returned. OpenAI offers zero data retention and modified abuse monitoring controls. Both need approval from the vendor. Anthropic's docs also list products and features that its ZDR does not cover.
- **Data residency.** Both vendors let eligible customers choose where data is processed or stored, for example in the United States or, at OpenAI, in Europe and other regions. This can cost more: both vendors' docs list a price uplift for it.

Example: a hospital group wants AI to draft letters that include patient details. That is a job for the company's legal and IT teams to set up with the vendor first, with the right agreement in place, before anyone pastes patient data.

## Rules you can use today

These are habits, not legal advice. Your company's policy comes first.

1. **Use the approved tool and account.** Not your personal one.
2. **Share the minimum.** Remove names, account numbers and other personal details when the task doesn't need them. "Customer A" works as well as a real name for most drafting.
3. **Never paste secrets.** Passwords, API keys and access codes don't belong in a prompt.
4. **Know your data's class.** Public, internal, confidential, personal. If you're not sure, ask before you paste.
5. **Ask who decided.** For anything sensitive, check that legal or IT has approved this vendor and plan for this kind of data.

## Key points

- Prompts and files go to the vendor. Treat pasting like sending an email outside the company.
- Terms differ by product and plan. Business API terms are not the same as a personal app's terms.
- "Not used for training" does not mean "never stored".
- Strict options exist (zero retention, data residency), but the company sets them up.
