## Why it matters

A model can write something false in a calm, confident tone. This is called a **hallucination**. It is one of the most important risks to understand before you use AI at work.

## What a hallucination is

Anthropic's docs define it as text that is "factually incorrect or inconsistent with the given context". They add that even the most advanced models can do this.

Two kinds matter at work:

- **Wrong facts.** The model states a date, a number or a rule that is not true.
- **Not in your document.** You give the model a contract and it "quotes" a clause that the contract does not contain.

Example: you ask an assistant to summarize a supplier contract. The summary says "30-day free cancellation". The contract says nothing about cancellation. The sentence sounds normal, which is why it is dangerous.

## Why confident is not the same as correct

Anthropic's glossary says LLMs can generate human-like text. Fluent, human-like text sounds sure. But the tone tells you nothing about whether a fact is true. Treat every specific claim (a number, a name, a date, a quote) as something to check.

## Ways to reduce hallucinations

Anthropic's guide lists simple techniques. You can use all of them in a normal chat, with no code.

**1. Allow "I don't know."** Tell the model it may say it is unsure or lacks the information. The guide calls this a simple technique that can "drastically reduce false information".

Example prompt: "If the report doesn't contain the answer, say: I don't have enough information."

**2. Quotes first.** For long documents, ask the model to pull out word-for-word quotes first, then do the task using only those quotes.

Example: "First copy the exact sentences about payment terms. Then summarize the payment terms from those sentences only."

**3. Cite and check.** Ask the model to back each claim with a quote from your documents. If it can't find a quote, it must remove the claim.

**4. Only your documents.** Tell the model to use only the documents you give it, not its general knowledge.

**5. Ask more than once.** Run the same prompt several times and compare. Anthropic's guide says inconsistent answers can be a sign of hallucination.

OpenAI's accuracy guide adds a warning that fits here: too much irrelevant context can drown out the real information and cause hallucinations. Give the model the documents that matter, not everything you have.

## These techniques reduce the risk. They don't remove it.

Anthropic's guide is clear: the techniques "don't eliminate them entirely". Always validate critical information, especially for high-stakes decisions.

So decide how much checking a task needs by asking what a mistake costs. OpenAI's guide gives a clear contrast: a bad draft a person fixes is a small problem; refunding a customer $1000 instead of $100 is a big one. In one example in that guide, where the cost of failure was very high, the team let the human drive and used the AI as an assistant.

Example plan for a legal team:

- Internal meeting notes → AI drafts, the writer skims.
- Customer-facing contract summary → AI drafts with quotes, a lawyer checks every quote against the contract.
- A final legal decision → a person decides. The AI only helps prepare.

## Key points

- A hallucination is false or unsupported text, often said with confidence.
- Prompts can lower the risk: allow "I don't know", quotes first, cite and check, only your documents.
- No technique makes it zero. Match the amount of human checking to the cost of a mistake.
