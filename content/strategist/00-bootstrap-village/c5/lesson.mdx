## Why it matters

Every vendor offers several models. Some are stronger and slower. Some are faster and cheaper. There is no single best model. There is a best model **for one task**, at your quality bar and your budget.

This lesson gives you a way to choose that still works when the model names change.

## The three questions

Anthropic's guide names three main things to balance (it also lists effort, covered below):

- **Capabilities.** What must the model be able to do? Example: read scanned invoices, or reason through a 40-page contract.
- **Speed.** How fast must it answer? A live chat with customers needs fast replies. A weekly report can wait.
- **Cost.** What is the budget, both for testing and for daily use?

OpenAI's guide asks similar questions: how often does the workflow run, and how quickly do you need the result?

Example: a team summarizes 5,000 short support tickets every day. Speed and cost matter a lot, and the task is simple. A team that drafts one investment memo a month cares most about quality, and cost barely matters.

## Two ways to start

Anthropic's guide describes two starting points.

**Efficiency-first.** Start with a fast, low-cost model. Test it on your task. Move up only if it falls short. The guide says this suits prototypes, tight speed needs, cost-sensitive work and high-volume, simple tasks.

**Capability-first.** Start with a strong model. Get the quality right. Then try to save money with a lighter model or setting over time. The guide says this suits complex reasoning, nuanced understanding, and work where accuracy matters more than cost.

OpenAI's guide lands in the same place: experiment, compare results on the same inputs, and "keep the lightest setting that meets your quality bar".

## Settings matter, not only the model

Many models now have a setting for how hard they think. Anthropic calls it **effort**; OpenAI calls it **reasoning effort**. Anthropic's guide says tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models, because it trades quality for speed and cost inside one model.

Example: a quick email triage can run on low effort. A careful contract review can run on high effort with the same model.

## Test with your own work

Benchmarks on a vendor's page are not your task. Anthropic's guide says having a good evaluation set (a fixed set of test cases) is "the most important step" when choosing or changing models.

A simple test any team can run:

1. Collect 20–30 real examples of the task, with what a good answer looks like.
2. Run them through two or three candidate models or settings.
3. Score each answer: correct? usable? any made-up facts?
4. Compare quality, speed and cost. Pick the lightest option that passes.

## Keep it stable

Vendors update and retire models. OpenAI's text guide recommends pinning production apps to a specific model snapshot (a fixed version) for consistent behaviour, and keeping tests so you can check quality when you upgrade. Anthropic's model table lists the earliest date each model may be retired.

Example: your team's report tool works well today. Note which model version it uses and keep your test set. When the vendor releases a new model, rerun the tests before you switch.

## Key points

- Choose for the task: capability, speed and cost.
- Start cheap and move up, or start strong and move down. Both are valid.
- Try effort or reasoning settings before you change models.
- Decide with your own test examples, not with the newest headline.
