## Why it matters

A lot of work is not typed text. It is a screenshot of an error, a photo of a whiteboard, a chart in a slide deck, a recorded call. Many models can now work with images and audio too. The prompting skills from this realm still apply. But each media type has its own strengths, limits and costs.

## Reading images

Models that can "see" are called vision models. The vendors list similar uses:

- OpenAI's guide: describe images, read visible text, and answer questions about objects, shapes, colors or textures.
- Gemini's guide: image captioning, classification and visual question answering.
- Anthropic's guide: Claude can interpret and analyze images, and in the claude.ai app you upload an image like a file.

Work examples:

- Paste a screenshot of an error message and ask what the user should try first.
- Upload a photo of the whiteboard after a workshop and ask for the action items as a list.
- Share a chart and ask: "What changed between Q1 and Q2? Quote the numbers you read from the chart."

## Brief the model about the image

The brief from earlier in this realm still matters. Say what the image is, what you need from it, and in what format.

Weak: "What's this?"
Strong: "This is a photo of a printed delivery note. Extract the supplier name, the date and each line item as a table. Write 'unclear' for anything you can't read."

Give the model a clear picture. Anthropic's and Gemini's tips both say to use clear, non-blurry images that are the right way up. OpenAI's guide warns that rotated images can be misread. Anthropic's guide adds that important text in the image should be legible and not too small.

**Where to put the image is a vendor detail.** Anthropic's guide says Claude works best when images come before the text. Gemini's guide says that with a single image, the text prompt should come before the image. When a detail differs, follow the docs of the tool you use, and test.

## Where image reading fails

Both Anthropic's and OpenAI's guides list limits. The main ones for office work:

- **Poor images and small text.** Anthropic's guide says the model can make mistakes with low-quality, rotated or very small images. OpenAI's guide names small text, rotated text, and text in non-Latin alphabets such as Japanese or Korean.
- **Counting.** Both say counts of objects can be approximate.
- **Exact positions.** Both say spatial reasoning is weak or approximate.
- **Charts.** OpenAI's guide says the model may struggle with graphs where colors or line styles (solid, dashed, dotted) vary.
- **People.** Anthropic's guide says Claude cannot be used to name people in images and refuses to do so.
- **Fakes.** Anthropic's guide says Claude cannot tell whether an image is AI-generated.

Anthropic's guide says to always review and verify image interpretations, especially for high-stakes use.

Example: never let the model's count of items in a warehouse photo replace a stock count.

## Making images

Not every model can create images. Anthropic's guide says Claude can analyze images but cannot generate or edit them. OpenAI's GPT Image models can create new images from text or edit existing ones.

OpenAI's guide lists the limits that matter at work: text inside the image can still be unclear or misplaced, recurring brand elements may not stay consistent across images, and precise layouts are hard.

Example: an AI-made image is fine for an internal workshop slide. For a product ad with your logo and exact price, a designer should check or finish it.

## Voice: from speech to text

Recorded meetings, calls and interviews can be turned into text.

- OpenAI's file transcription turns a recording into a transcript. Its guide says a prompt can improve how names, product terms and acronyms are written.
- Gemini's audio guide lists summarizing audio, transcription, translation, and speaker diarization (who said what).

Example prompt for a sales call: "Transcribe this call. Our product names are Fluxa and Fluxa Pro. Then list the customer's objections and the agreed next step."

OpenAI's guide also warns that keyword hints should be checked: they should help accuracy without making words appear that nobody said.

## Voice: from text to speech

OpenAI's text-to-speech can narrate a written text, such as a blog post, in several languages. Its usage policies require you to clearly tell listeners that the voice is AI-generated and not a human voice.

Example: an AI-narrated training module should say so at the start.

## Media costs tokens too

Images and audio are turned into tokens, and you pay for them.

- OpenAI's guide says images count as tokens and are billed.
- Anthropic's guide says the token cost depends on image size. Its table shows a 1000×1000 pixel image at 1,296 tokens.
- Gemini's guide says audio costs 32 tokens per second: 1,920 tokens per minute. So a 30-minute call is about 57,600 tokens.

Example: if you only need the numbers from one corner of a big screenshot, crop it first.

## Key points

- Many models can read images; some can also create them, transcribe speech or speak.
- Brief the model about the image: what it is, what you need, in what format.
- Use clear, upright images. Expect errors with small text, counts, positions and some charts.
- Tell people when a voice is AI-generated.
- Media costs tokens. Crop and trim what you send.
