## Why it matters

A prompt is a brief. A short, vague brief gets a generic answer. A clear brief gets work you can use. It is a cheap way to get better results, and it needs no tech skills.

## Brief it like a new colleague

Anthropic's guide gives a simple picture: think of the model as a brilliant but new employee who lacks context on your norms and workflows. It is smart, but it does not know your team, your customers or your goals.

The guide also gives a **golden rule**: show your prompt to a colleague with little context and ask them to follow it. If they would be confused, the model will be too.

Example of a weak brief:

> Write an email about the price change.

A colleague would ask: to whom? Which change? What tone? How long? The model will not ask. It will guess.

## The five parts of a good brief

The three vendors use different words, but their guides cover the same parts. Use them as a checklist.

**1. The task.** Say exactly what you want. Gemini's guide says clear and specific instructions are an effective and efficient way to customize model behavior.

**2. The context and the reason.** Give the facts the model needs, and say why the task matters. Anthropic's guide says that explaining the reason behind an instruction helps the model understand your goal. OpenAI's guide lists context as one part of a prompt, for example private data the model was not trained on.

**3. The audience and the role.** Say who will read the result. You can also give the model a role. Anthropic's guide says even a single sentence of role makes a difference.

**4. The constraints.** Length, tone, what to include, what to leave out. Gemini's guide shows how to set a limit, such as a one-sentence summary.

**5. The format.** Say what the result should look like: a table, three bullets, a short email. A later chapter covers this in depth.

The same email, as a brief:

> You are writing for our customer success team. Task: an email to existing small-business customers about our price change. Context: the Basic plan goes up on 1 March; current customers keep the old price for 6 months; the reason is new support hours. Audience: busy owners who are not technical. Tone: friendly and direct, no sales language. Length: under 150 words. End with one link to the FAQ page.

## Write steps when order matters

If the task has several steps, number them. Anthropic's guide suggests numbered lists or bullets when the order or completeness of the steps matters.

Example: "1. Read the meeting notes. 2. List each decision. 3. Name the owner of each decision. 4. Flag any decision with no owner."

## Put long documents first

When you paste a long document, put it at the top and your question at the end. Anthropic's guide says queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in tests. Gemini's guide gives the same advice for long context: all the context first, the specific question at the very end.

Example: paste the 20-page supplier contract first. Then write: "Based on the contract above, list every date we must act by."

## Say what you mean, including the extra mile

Don't expect the model to read your mind. Anthropic's guide says that if you want "above and beyond" behavior, you should ask for it, not hope the model infers it from a vague prompt.

Gemini's guide adds: explain any term that could mean more than one thing. If "active customer" means "bought in the last 90 days" at your company, write that down.

How much detail? OpenAI's guide compares two kinds of model. A reasoning model is like a senior co-worker: you give it a goal and trust it with the details. A GPT model is like a junior coworker: it does best with explicit instructions. When you are not sure, a clear brief helps both.

## Expect to try again

A first brief is a draft. Gemini's guide says prompt engineering is iterative and suggests trying different phrasing when you don't get the result you want. When an answer misses, ask: what did my brief leave out? Fix the brief, not only the answer.

## Key points

- Brief the model like a smart new colleague with no context.
- Cover the task, the context and reason, the audience, the constraints and the format.
- Number the steps when order matters.
- Long document first, question last.
- Define your own terms. Ask for the extra mile if you want it.
