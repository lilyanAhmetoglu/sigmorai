## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Count how often each step happens and how many staff hours it takes a month.
- Check a repeated step for four signs that it suits AI.
- Spot repeated work that can wait, so it can run as a cheaper batch job.

## The problem

A car leasing company has mapped its back-office work. The team lead now has a list of eight steps and one question: "Where do we start?" Everyone has a favourite. The finance analyst wants the hardest task. The team wants the most annoying one.

A good first target is usually work that repeats. Each minute saved is multiplied by the volume, and a repeated task gives you many real examples to test with. This lesson shows how to count, check and sort repeated work. The guidance below is from each vendor's docs (October 2026).

## The idea

**The vendors point to volume first.** The [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/customer-support-chat) list signs that a model suits support work. The first one is a high volume of questions that repeat. The model can answer "a large number of similar questions", which frees people "for more complex issues". The [legal guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/legal-summarization) starts the same way: "You want to review a high volume of documents efficiently and affordably." An [OpenAI cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/articles/chatgpt-agents-sales-meeting-prep) shows it in sales: teams "often spend time manually pulling account notes" before each customer meeting, so its example agent runs "each day" and writes the briefs.

**Our advice:** check each repeated step for four signs.

```text
1. Same task, many times   a real count, not "lots"
2. Same kind of input      every form has the same fields
3. Same shape of output    a label, a filled form, a fixed template
4. Written rules           a rule you can test, not "Ana just knows"
```

**Work that can wait costs less.** Some repeated work needs an answer now, like a live chat. Other work can wait an hour or a day. Both vendors offer a **batch** option for it: you send many requests at once and collect the results later.

- The [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing) say batches are for "tasks that do not require immediate responses", "cutting costs by 50%", "with most batches completing within 1 hour".
- The [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/batch) say "50% lower costs" and "a clear 24-hour turnaround time". They name jobs like "Classifying large datasets".

**Volume multiplies cost, too.** The [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/model-selection) ask "How often does your workflow run?" and say "A frequent automation makes usage and cost add up faster than an occasional project." Their advice: compare on "the same inputs" and "keep the lightest setting that meets your quality bar". The [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model) agree: beginning with a quicker, lower-cost model is "best for" work that includes "High-volume, straightforward" jobs.

Our example: a bakery saves the most time with a better machine for the bread it bakes every morning, not with a faster way to make the one wedding cake a year.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak list

```text
Ideas: claims, lease-end emails, yearly fleet review, weekly report
Pick: the yearly fleet review, because it is the hardest work
```

What goes wrong: "hardest" is not "most value". Nobody counted anything.

### Step 2: count volume and time

The team lead pulls last month's numbers from the systems.

```text
Step                              Per month  Time each  Hours a month
A. Turn dealer damage reports       1,800     10 min        300
   into the standard claim form
B. Answer "when does my lease       2,200      1 min        ~37
   end?" emails
C. Yearly fleet review                1 a year  2 weeks      ~7 (averaged)
D. Send the weekly sales report         4      5 min        <1
```

Hours = count × minutes ÷ 60. A takes the most staff time by far.

### Step 3: check the four signs

```text
                Many times  Same input  Same output  Written rules
A. Claim form       yes        yes      yes (form)   yes
B. Lease emails     yes        yes      yes          yes
C. Fleet review     no         no       no           no
D. Weekly report    no         yes      yes          yes
```

### Step 4: the hard case

**B looks great on count but not on hours.** No other step on the list comes close on count. But each email takes one minute, so the total is about 37 hours. A saves about eight times more. Always multiply count by time before you compare.

### Step 5: ask "does someone wait?"

```text
A. Claim forms are checked the next morning   -> can wait: batch job
B. Each customer is waiting for a reply       -> needs a fast answer
```

A can run overnight as a batch, at the lower batch price. It is also high volume, so the team tests a lighter model on the same damage reports and keeps the lightest one that meets the bar.

### Step 6: why it is better

- **Counted, not guessed.** Hours a month replace opinions.
- **All four signs.** A repeats, has the same input and output, and has written rules.
- **Cheaper to run.** Nobody waits for A, so it fits a batch job.
- **Cost checked.** High volume is where a lighter model saves the most.

## Common mistakes

1. **Frequent but tiny.** A 15-second task done 60 times a month saves 15 minutes. Fix: always multiply count by time.
2. **Repetitive but risky.** Volume does not remove the need for review. The legal guide says the model can add citations so staff can "review and verify" its summaries. Fix: plan who checks the output.
3. **Repetitive but already solved.** If a simple rule or a tool already does the step well, AI adds little. Fix: drop it from the list.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

An online shop's support team has three repeated steps. For each one, work out the hours a month, and say if it can run as a batch job.

```text
X. Tag product reviews as positive, negative or mixed
   6,000 a month, 30 seconds each, tags used in a monthly report
Y. Answer "is this item in stock?" chat questions
   3,000 a month, 1 minute each, the customer is waiting
Z. Write the yearly supplier review
   1 a year, 3 days, different every time
```

### Answer

- **X: 50 hours a month.** 6,000 × 0.5 min ÷ 60. Nobody waits, so it fits a batch job.
- **Y: 50 hours a month.** 3,000 × 1 min ÷ 60. A customer waits, so it needs fast answers, not a batch.
- **Z: a poor first target.** It is rare and different each time, so it fails the four signs.

X and Y save the same time. X is the easier and cheaper start.

## Recap

- Count first: hours a month = count × time. The highest count is not always the most hours.
- Good repetition means the same task, the same input, the same kind of output, and rules on paper.
- Work nobody waits for can run as a batch job, at a 50% lower price with both vendors. High volume also makes model choice matter more.
