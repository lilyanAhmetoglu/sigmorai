## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Choose the level for a job: a single call, a vendor workflow, or your own chain of steps.
- Read a workflow's defaults (sound, length, version) before you promise a client anything.
- Write a pipeline sheet with a person check at every step a client will see.

## The problem

An online tea shop wants two things:

1. **Every week:** a 10-second vertical video for each new tea, made from 4 product photos. The shop always adds its own music later, so the video must be silent.
2. **Once:** a brand film. The team makes four mood stills. The owner picks one. Then a clip starts on that still and ends on the shop's logo card.

A teammate picks one ready-made video workflow for both jobs. He chooses the one with "more shots", leaves every setting on its default, and tracks the newest version. Week 1 looks great. Week 2 has a soundtrack nobody asked for, and the style has changed. The brand film has no place for the owner's choice at all.

The tools worked. The teammate picked the wrong level, and he did not read the defaults.

## The idea

### Three levels

A real job has many steps: brief, stills, clips, sound, review, delivery. You can run them at three levels. This grouping is ours, not a vendor's.

```text
1. single call       one request -> one asset
2. vendor workflow   one request -> the vendor runs several steps inside
3. your own chain    you run each step, with checks and people between them
```

It is like cooking. A ready meal is fast, but you cannot change the recipe halfway. Cooking from scratch takes longer, but you can taste at each step.

### Level 2: vendor workflows

**Runway Recipes.** The [Runway primer](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/ai-context.md) says Recipes "are prebuilt multi-step workflows that wrap several generations behind a single call, so you do not have to orchestrate the steps yourself". They "return a task and follow the same contract as any other generation": you submit, wait, then pick up the file.

Two recipes, side by side:

| Setting | [Product Ad](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/product-ad) | [Multi-Shot Video](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/multi-shot-video) |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inputs  | "One to ten" product images, "Up to four" style images         | `auto`: one story prompt; `custom`: "3–5 shots"                            |
| Length  | "Output length in seconds (4–15). Defaults to 10."             | "`5`, `10`, or `15`"; in `custom`, shot lengths "must sum to `duration`"   |
| Sound   | `audio` "Defaults to `false`."                                 | `audio` "Defaults to `true`."                                              |

Look at the sound row. The same setting has opposite defaults. Leave it empty and one video is silent, the other has sound.

Style images in Product Ad are for the look: they define "lighting, palette, and mood". They are not a place to hide a bad product photo.

**Versions.** The Multi-Shot page says: "Use a dated version like `2026-06` to pin behavior, or `unsafe-latest` to track the newest stable version." A pinned version keeps the same behaviour from week to week. The newest version can change under you.

**OpenAI's image tool.** This is a workflow inside a chat. The [OpenAI tool docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-image-generation) say that when you include the `image_generation` tool, "the model can decide when and how to generate images as part of the conversation". The result is "a base64-encoded image". The vendor runs the image step for you. You still save the file.

### Level 3: your own chain

You build your own chain when a person must decide something in the middle, or when no workflow has the steps you need. The pieces exist:

- **Still to clip.** The [Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) say you can "Generate a video by specifying the first and last frames", and Veo 3.1 "accepts up to 3 reference images".
- **Tools in a loop.** In the [OpenAI function calling docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling), "your application can continue this flow for as many tool calls as the task requires". In the [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/overview), for client tools, "Your code executes the operation and sends back a `tool_result`." Your app runs each step. The model only asks.

A chain costs more time to build. In return you control every step and every check.

### References decide the quality

The [Runway reference guidelines](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/reference-media) say: "Strong references are the single biggest lever on output quality." For product photos they say: "Avoid heavy reflections, watermarks, or overlaid text on the product." The [task failures page](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/errors/task-failures) also names logos, watermarks and overlaid text in the inputs among the usual reasons a job fails. For people, "only use references of people you have permission to depict."

### Keep a person at the client's door

A workflow hides its inner steps. So the checks must sit around it. Our rule: **every step that reaches the client has a named person who checks it.** And save files in your own place: the Runway primer says "Output URLs are temporary."

Before you go live, the [Runway launch checklist](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/go-live) asks you to test for `429` and `503` errors and to set up autobilling, so the project "doesn't run out of credits unexpectedly". The [OpenAI rate limits docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/rate-limits) add: "Limit both the number of attempts and the total time spent retrying."

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak plan

```text
Weekly tea video:  Multi-Shot Video, auto mode, all defaults,
                   version unsafe-latest
Brand film:        Multi-Shot Video, one story prompt
Checks:            none written down
Delivery:          send the Runway output link to the shop
```

### Step 2: pick the level per job

```text
Weekly tea video   -> Level 2, Product Ad
  why: a plain product video from photos, the same every week
Brand film         -> Level 3, our own chain
  why: the owner picks a still in the middle; no recipe has that step
```

### Step 3: set the defaults on purpose

```text
Product Ad
  version:        2026-07            (pinned, same look every week)
  productImages:  4 clean photos     (no price stickers, no text)
  ratio:          720:1280           (vertical)
  duration:       10
  audio:          false              (written down, even if it is the default)
```

### Step 4: the hard case

**The hard case is the brand film.** It looks like "one video", so a recipe feels right. But the owner must choose a still halfway through. A recipe runs its own steps from start to end. So we chain the steps ourselves:

```text
Step  Input                Tool                Output         Person checks  Saved where
1     brief                image model         4 mood stills  art director   /tea/film/stills/
2     4 stills             -                   1 chosen still owner          /tea/film/chosen/
3     chosen still + logo  Veo 3.1             clip           art director   /tea/film/clips/
      (first + last frame)
4     clip + music         video editor        final film     producer       /tea/film/final/
5     final film           email (our copy)    delivery       producer       -
```

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Each job has the right level.** The weekly video is fast. The brand film keeps the owner's choice.
- **No surprise sound.** `audio: false` is set, not left to a default.
- **The look stays the same.** The version is pinned.
- **Every client-facing step has a person.** And every file lives in the shop's folders, not behind a link that expires.

## Common mistakes

1. **Leaving recipe settings on their defaults.** Two recipes can have opposite defaults. Fix: write every setting that matters on the job sheet, even when it equals the default.
2. **Tracking the newest version on client work.** The look can change between drafts. Fix: pin a dated version.
3. **Using a recipe for a flow with a human choice inside.** The recipe cannot stop for it. Fix: build your own chain and put the person step in it.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A florist asks for a 15-second video that cuts between 4 scenes: shop front, hands tying flowers, a van, a happy customer. They want sound. Your plan: Multi-Shot Video, `custom` mode, shot lengths 4 + 4 + 4 + 4. Is the plan right? What would you change?

### Answer

- **The recipe fits.** 4 shots is inside "3–5 shots", and 15 seconds is an allowed total.
- **The lengths are wrong.** 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16, but the shots together must equal the total length. Use, for example, 4 + 4 + 3 + 4 = 15.
- **Sound is fine.** Multi-Shot Video has audio on by default. Still write `audio: true` on the sheet, so nobody has to guess.
- **Pin the version**, for example `2026-06`.

## Recap

- Pick the level per job: a single call, a vendor workflow, or your own chain. A workflow is fast. A chain lets a person decide in the middle.
- Read and set the defaults: Product Ad has sound off, Multi-Shot Video has sound on. Pin a dated version for client work.
- Clean reference photos matter most for quality. Name a person to check each step the client will see, and save files in your own storage.
