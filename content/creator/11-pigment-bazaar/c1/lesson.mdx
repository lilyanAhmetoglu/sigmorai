## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Turn mood words into things a model can draw.
- Write one brief in the habits of five image models: OpenAI, Gemini, FLUX, Ideogram and Recraft.
- Say where each model takes negatives, settings, vector output and prompt rewrites.

## The problem

Wheelhouse is a small bike repair shop. It wants one hero image for its new website. The owner writes the brief: "Friendly, honest, a bit retro." The designer writes one prompt and pastes it into five image models:

```text
A beautiful, iconic photo of our friendly retro bike shop, masterpiece, 8k.
Use the photo for the look.
```

The designer also attaches two files: a photo of the real shopfront, and an old film photo whose colours the owner loves.

Five models give five different images. Two copy the street from the film photo. Nobody can say which words caused what.

Each model has its own **dialect**: the way its official guide asks you to write. One prompt cannot fit all five.

## The idea

A note on dates: the model names here are the ones on each vendor's page in October 2026. They change often.

### Five guides, five habits

| Model family (October 2026)    | What its guide asks for                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| OpenAI GPT Image 2.5           | The subject and the intended use; labelled parts for complex requests    |
| Google Nano Banana (Gemini)    | Many specific details, and the purpose of the image                      |
| Black Forest Labs FLUX 3 Image | Only what is visible; reference images named by position                 |
| Ideogram 4.5, 4.0 and 3.0      | Full sentences, main idea first, said in positive words                  |
| Recraft V4 and V4.1            | The model name picks raster or vector; vector prompts describe structure |

The [OpenAI prompting guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-prompting) says: "Name the subject and intended use". It adds: "For complex requests, organize the prompt as scene, subject, details, and constraints, using labeled sections."

The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) say: "Be hyper-specific: The more detail you provide, the more control you have." They also say: "Explain the purpose of the image."

### Write only what can be seen

The [FLUX basics guide](https://docs.bfl.ai/guides/prompting_unified_basics) lists what makes a good caption. One point is "Only what's visible". It says: "Every phrase names something you could point to in the frame." It names three words to drop: "beautiful", "iconic" and "masterpiece".

The [Ideogram guide](https://docs.ideogram.ai/prompting/prompting) explains why. Ideogram "reads your words literally and tries to show every one of them". So: "Add a word you don't need and it may show up in the image." Its habit: "Describe what can be seen."

OpenAI's guide says the same for big scenes: "specify scale, atmosphere, and color instead of relying on mood words alone."

### Settings stay out of the prompt

Ideogram says: "There is no hidden syntax." A weight like `::1` or a flag like `--ar` is read as plain words, not as a command. Its advice: "Set aspect ratio, seed and quality in the Image app settings instead."

The [FLUX technical page](https://docs.bfl.ai/guides/prompting_unified_technical) says: "On FLUX 3 Image, the prompt carries everything you want to control." Format goes in request fields, such as `aspect_ratio` (our reading: picture in the prompt, format in the fields).

### Say what you want, not what you don't

Models treat "no" in different ways.

- **FLUX 3 Image** "has no negative prompt field". Its guide says to name what you would see there instead: an empty promenade, not a promenade with no crowds. Its swap table turns "no people" into words like "empty" or "solitary", and "no text" into words like "unmarked".
- **Ideogram** says: "Say what you want, not what you don't." Its example: an empty street works better than a street with no people.
- **Ideogram 3.0** is the only Ideogram model with a **Negative prompt** field, by the [Ideogram settings page](https://docs.ideogram.ai/create/image-settings). Even there, "Your main prompt always wins."
- **Recraft** lists `negative_prompt` in its [API reference](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/endpoints) for "V2 / V3 models" only.
- **OpenAI** shows a short exclusion line in one example: "No extra text, no watermarks, no unrelated logos."

Our reading: start with positive words on all five. Use a negative field only on a model that has one.

### Raster or vector

Most image models make **raster** images: a grid of pixels, like a PNG or a JPEG. A **vector** image (SVG) stores shapes and lines instead, so it stays sharp at any size (our plain gloss).

Recraft makes both. Its [model list](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/models/overview) says names "ending in `_vector` produce vector (SVG) images; all other models produce raster images". Its API also has a `/generations/vector` **endpoint** (the web address a request goes to). It accepts only vector models.

The prompt changes too. For logo and vector work, the [Recraft V4 guide](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/prompt-engineering-guide/prompting-with-recraft-v4) asks you to define the graphic type, shape logic, colour system, line discipline, layout structure and constraints. It adds: "Avoid texture or material-focused language." In plain words: describe shapes, colours and lines, not shine or metal.

### Who rewrites your prompt?

Some models change your words before they draw.

- **Ideogram Magic Prompt** "Enhances your prompt to improve image variety and richness." The prompting guide says it is a toggle "On every Ideogram model", and the settings page says it "starts on". Its advice: "Turn it off when you want the model to follow your exact words."
- **FLUX 3 Image** expands a short prompt "into a detailed description before it generates the image". It has no `prompt_upsampling` field, so there is no switch for this (our reading).
- **Recraft Enhance prompt** is a separate request. It returns an `enhanced_prompt`, and you decide whether to use it. The [page](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/tools/enhance-prompt) lists "$0.01 per request".
- **OpenAI**: in the image generation tool, the mainline model "will automatically revise your prompt for improved performance", says the [OpenAI image docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation). You can read the new version in `revised_prompt`.

Our advice: when exact words matter, turn the rewriter off or read what it wrote.

### Reference images by position

FLUX 3 Image takes reference images in an `images` list. The guide says to "refer to references by position: "image 1" is the first entry in `images`, "image 2" the second." It also says: "Clarify the purpose of each image."

## Worked example

Our example. The shop, the files and the prompts are made up.

### Step 1: find the mood words

```text
A beautiful, iconic photo of our friendly retro bike shop, masterpiece, 8k.
Use the photo for the look.
```

"Beautiful", "iconic" and "masterpiece" are the three words FLUX names. "Friendly" and "retro" are moods too. You cannot point to any of them in the frame.

### Step 2: turn each mood into things you can see

```text
friendly  -> open door, a hand-painted OPEN sign, a bike on a stand by the door
honest    -> tools hanging on a pegboard, a work bench you can see from the street
retro     -> faded mint-green paint, a 1970s racing bike in the window
```

### Step 3: the OpenAI version, in labelled parts

```text
Use: hero image for a bike repair shop's website, wide 16:9 banner.
Scene: a narrow street on a sunny morning.
Subject: a small bike repair shop with faded mint-green paint and an open door.
Details: a 1970s racing bike in the window; tools on a pegboard inside;
a bike on a work stand by the door; warm, low sunlight from the left.
Constraints: photorealistic; no people; keep the left third calm for a headline.
```

The use comes first, as OpenAI's guide asks. Each part can change on its own later.

### Step 4: the hard case, two reference images

**The hard case is the two attached photos.** "Use the photo" does not say which one. It does not say what to take from it, either. The model took the street from the film photo.

Name each image by its place in the `images` list, and give each one a job:

```text
images: [shopfront.jpg, film-sample.jpg]

Prompt (FLUX 3 Image):
Street-level photograph of the bike repair shop in image 1, same mint-green
front and same window. Colour and grain like image 2: warm faded film tones,
soft yellow highlights. A 1970s racing bike in the window. A bare, sunny
pavement in front of the open door.
```

Image 1 gives the subject. Image 2 gives only the colour.

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Every word can be drawn.** No model guesses what "iconic" looks like.
- **Each model gets its own shape**, and **each image has one job**.

## Common mistakes

1. **Praise words in the prompt.** "Stunning" and "masterpiece" name nothing in the frame. Fix: swap each one for an object, a light or a colour.
2. **One prompt, pasted into every model.** The habits differ in the details. Fix: read the guide for the model you use, and reshape the prompt for it.
3. **Leaving out the use.** "A photo of a bike shop" could be a poster or a tiny icon. Fix: start with what the image is for, as the OpenAI and Gemini guides ask.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A café asks for a menu-board photo. For each phrase, say whether you could point to it in the frame. Rewrite the ones you can't.

1. "cosy autumn vibe"
2. "a ceramic cup of hot chocolate on a dark wooden counter"
3. "award-winning, trending, professional"

### Answer

1. **Not visible.** "Cosy" and "vibe" are moods. Our rewrite: "a knitted blanket on a window seat, orange maple leaves outside the glass, low warm lamp light".
2. **Visible.** A cup, a drink, a material and a surface. Keep it as it is.
3. **Not visible.** These are praise words, like the three FLUX tells you to drop. Delete them. If you want a quality look, describe it: "sharp focus on the cup, soft blurred background" (our example).

## Recap

- Write only what you could point to in the frame. Mood and praise words give the model nothing to draw.
- Each model has a dialect: the use and labelled parts for OpenAI, detail and purpose for Gemini, visible captions and image positions for FLUX, positive sentences for Ideogram, and the model name for Recraft's raster or vector.
- Keep settings in settings, say what you want instead of "no", and know which models rewrite your prompt.
