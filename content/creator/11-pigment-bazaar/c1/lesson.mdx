## Why it matters

A tea shop sends one brief: "A launch poster for our new jasmine tea. Calm, green, premium." You try it on five image models. You get five different readings.

Prompt Forest, chapter 1, gave you the parts of a shot prompt. Chapter 9 put a text prompt next to an image prompt. This chapter goes one step further. Each image model has its own **dialect**: the way its guide asks you to write. Same brief, different wording.

A note on dates. The model names below are the ones on each vendor's page in October 2026. They change often.

## Five guides, five habits

| Model (October 2026)           | What its guide asks for                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| OpenAI GPT Image 2.5           | Name the subject and the use. "For complex requests, organize the prompt as scene, subject, details, and constraints, using labeled sections." |
| Google Nano Banana (Gemini)    | "Be hyper-specific: The more detail you provide, the more control you have." Say the purpose of the image.                                     |
| Black Forest Labs FLUX 3 Image | Describe what appears. Refer to input images by position: "image 1", "image 2".                                                                |
| Ideogram 4.5 and 4.0           | Full sentences, main idea first. Plain language or a JSON prompt.                                                                              |
| Recraft V4 and V4.1            | The model name decides raster or vector. Short prompts to explore, structured prompts for a set art direction.                                 |

The habits overlap. All five want things you can see. The differences are in the details below.

## Write only what can be seen

FLUX's basics page shows a long caption and lists what makes it work. One point is "Only what's visible: no "beautiful", "iconic", or "masterpiece"." Every phrase names something "you could point to in the frame".

Ideogram says the same in its own words. It "reads your words literally and tries to show every one of them". So "Add a word you don't need and it may show up in the image."

For the tea poster, "premium" is a mood word. Turn it into things you can see (our example): "a matte white tin with a thin gold rim, on pale stone".

## No hidden syntax

Some designers bring habits from other tools: weights like `::1`, or flags like `--ar 16:9` at the end of a prompt. Ideogram says it doesn't read these as commands; "it treats them as words." Its advice: "Set aspect ratio, seed and quality in the Image app settings instead."

FLUX works the same way. On FLUX 3 Image, "the prompt carries everything you want to control", and the request has fields such as `aspect_ratio` and `resolution`. So keep settings in the settings and the picture in the prompt (our advice). Tool Forge, chapter 1, showed the OpenAI and Gemini settings.

## Negatives, again

Chapter 9 of Prompt Forest showed that OpenAI, Gemini and older Imagen treat "don't" differently. The three newer vendors add more variety.

- **FLUX 3 Image** "has no negative prompt field". Its guide says: describe "an empty promenade" instead of "no crowds".
- **Ideogram** says "An empty street" works better than "a street with no people." Only Ideogram 3.0 has a separate **Negative prompt** field, and "Your main prompt always wins."
- **Recraft** lists `negative_prompt` in its API for V2 and V3 models only.

So start with positive words on all five (our reading). OpenAI's guide also shows a short exclusion line: "No extra text, no watermarks, no unrelated logos." Use a negative field only where the model has one.

## Raster or vector

Most image models make **raster** images: a grid of pixels, like a PNG or JPEG. A **vector** image (SVG) stores shapes and lines instead of pixels (our plain gloss).

Recraft makes both. Its model list says names "ending in `_vector` produce vector (SVG) images; all other models produce raster images". It also has a `/generations/vector` **endpoint** (the API address you send the request to) that accepts only vector models.

The prompt changes too. For logo and vector work, Recraft's V4 guide says to define the graphic type, shape logic, colour system, line discipline, layout and constraints. In plain words: what shapes it is built from, and how thick and even the lines are. It adds: "Avoid texture or material-focused language." So "matte white tin with a gold rim" suits a raster photo. For an SVG icon of the same tin, write "flat outline icon, two colours, even stroke, centred" (our example).

## Who rewrites your prompt?

Some models change your words before they draw. Know which ones do.

- **Ideogram Magic Prompt** "Enhances your prompt to improve image variety and richness." It starts on. "Turn it off when you want the model to follow your exact words."
- **FLUX 3 Image** expands a short prompt "into a detailed description before it generates the image". There is no switch for it (our reading): the guide says FLUX 3 Image has no `prompt_upsampling` field.
- **Recraft Enhance prompt** is a separate request. It returns one `enhanced_prompt` string, and you choose whether to use it. Price on the page in October 2026: $0.01 per request.
- **OpenAI** says the mainline model in its image generation tool "will automatically revise your prompt". You can read the new version in the `revised_prompt` field (Prompt Forest, chapter 9).

Our advice: when exact wording matters, such as a product name or a legal line, turn the rewriter off or read what it wrote.

## One brief, written five ways

Here is the start of the tea brief in each dialect (our examples):

- **OpenAI:** labelled parts. "Scene: … Subject: … Details: … Constraints: …"
- **Gemini:** one rich scene paragraph, with the purpose: "A launch poster for a premium jasmine tea brand…"
- **FLUX:** medium first, then subject and positions: "Studio product photograph. In the centre, a matte white tin…"
- **Ideogram:** the main idea in the first sentence. Magic Prompt off if the wording is final.
- **Recraft:** pick `recraftv4_1` for a raster photo or a `_vector` model for the SVG icon, then write for that format.

## What this means for your work

- **Read the guide for the model you use.** The habits overlap, but the details differ.
- **Only write what can be seen.** Swap mood words for objects, light and colour.
- **Keep settings out of the prompt.** Flags like `--ar` are just words to Ideogram.
- **Positive words first.** OpenAI's guide also shows a short exclusion line.
- **Pick raster or vector first** when the vendor offers both.
- **Know your rewriter.** Turn it off, or read its version, when words must stay exact.
