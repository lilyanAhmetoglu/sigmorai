## Why it matters

It is launch morning, 09:00. The whole studio presses "generate". Half the requests fail. Nobody changed the prompts. What happened?

You hit a limit. A limit is not a bug, and it does not always say "wait". Tool Forge, chapter 5, taught you to read a `429` after it happens. This lesson is about planning before launch, so that day one does not stop.

## Limits are meters

Every vendor counts your use with several meters. Hit one, and you get an error, even if the others are low.

| Meter         | Means                       | Where the docs name it |
| ------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| RPM           | requests per minute         | OpenAI, Claude, Gemini |
| TPM           | tokens per minute           | OpenAI, Gemini         |
| ITPM and OTPM | input and output tokens/min | Claude                 |
| RPD           | requests per day            | OpenAI, Gemini         |
| IPM           | images per minute           | OpenAI, Gemini         |

OpenAI says limits "can be hit across any of the options depending on what occurs first". Its example: 20 requests with only 100 tokens can fill a limit of 20 requests per minute, even if the token limit is far from used. Gemini gives the same kind of example. Gemini adds that IPM is counted only for models that can generate images.

Limits also differ by model. A fast image model and a premium one can have different limits. Look up the limit for the exact model you will use.

## Who shares a limit

Read where the meter lives.

- **OpenAI:** limits are "defined at the organization level and at the project level, not user level".
- **Claude:** the service sets limits at the organization level. You can also set limits for your workspaces.
- **Gemini:** limits "are applied per project, not per API key". Daily request quotas reset at midnight Pacific time.

Example: a designer creates a second API key for a test, inside the same Gemini project. The test and the live campaign still share one limit. A new key is not a new limit. A separate project is.

OpenAI suggests separate projects for staging and production. You can then "set custom rate and spend limits per project", so a rehearsal cannot use up the live campaign's limit.

## Pace the work, do not burst it

Claude's limits use a token bucket. Capacity "is continuously replenished up to your maximum limit, rather than being reset at fixed intervals". Claude also says a rate of 60 requests per minute might be enforced as 1 request per second, so short bursts can trigger errors.

So 60 requests sent in the first second can fail, even though 60 per minute is allowed. Spread the requests out. OpenAI returns headers such as the remaining requests and tokens and the time until reset, so a tool can pace itself.

A **queue** helps. Requests wait in line and leave at a steady pace. Ask your tool's owner whether one exists.

## Ramp up, do not jump

Limits are not only about size. They are also about speed of growth.

- **OpenAI:** a `slow_down` error "can occur even when your traffic is within its requests-per-minute and tokens-per-minute limits". It reflects how quickly traffic increased. As a rule of thumb for token traffic, once you reach 1 million input tokens per minute, increase by no more than 50% every 15 minutes. OpenAI says the exact point varies by model and conditions.
- **Claude:** a sharp rise in usage can cause `429` errors from "acceleration limits". Claude says to "ramp up your traffic gradually and maintain consistent usage patterns".

Both vendors say the same thing: grow gradually. Going from zero to full speed at 09:00 is a risk.

## Money is a limit too

Spend controls stop traffic as well as rate limits do.

- **OpenAI** has two controls. A spend alert "sends a notification; API traffic continues". A hard spend limit means affected requests "return a `429` error".
- **Claude** has a monthly spend cap per tier. At the cap, usage pauses until the next month, and the `429` has no `retry-after` header.
- **Gemini** has spend-based limits in a rolling 10-minute window. Some tiers do not apply it.

Tiers also matter. Limits depend on a tier that rises with usage and spending. A new account may start low. Claude says new organizations "may start in the Evaluation tier". Check your tier, and ask for higher limits before the launch, not on the day. Vendors also say limits are maximums, not guarantees. Gemini says "actual capacity may vary".

## A worked plan

All numbers are made up for this example. A studio needs 2,400 images for launch. The image limit is 60 per minute.

- Minimum time: 2,400 divided by 60 is 40 minutes. It cannot be done in 10.
- So generate before the launch, not at 09:00.
- Ramp: start well below 60 per minute and raise the pace in steps.
- Rehearse in a staging project.
- Set a spend alert below the hard limit, so people hear before traffic stops.

## What this means for your work

This checklist is ours, not a vendor rule.

- **Find the meters for your model.** Name them: requests, tokens, images, days.
- **Find where the limit lives.** Organization, project or key.
- **Do the sum.** Items divided by the per-minute limit gives the minimum time.
- **Pace and ramp.** A queue and a gradual start beat a burst.
- **Rehearse in staging.** Never test in the live project.
- **Set an alert before the cap.** Know what happens at the cap.
