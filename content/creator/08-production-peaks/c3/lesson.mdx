## Why it matters

A brand wants to post a realistic AI video. Does it need a label? The answer comes from three places: the vendor's policy, the platform's rules, and the law. They are different, and they change.

This lesson sorts them. It is not legal advice. It reports what the official pages say. For a real contract or a legal question, ask a lawyer.

## Layer 1: the vendor's policy

Bootstrap Village covered the policy lines on impersonation and on passing AI work off as human. One new line matters here, and one is worth a reminder.

- **Anthropic** requires that "all consumer-facing chatbots", including any "external-facing or interactive AI agent", must tell users they are talking to AI, "at a minimum at the beginning of each chat session". Anthropic's policy also lists "Media or professional journalistic content" among its high-risk use cases, which carry extra safety measures. Read that section if you publish AI output without a person in the loop.
- **Google** (a reminder from Bootstrap Village) forbids "misrepresenting the provenance of generated content by claiming it was created solely by a human, in order to deceive".

Both lines are about honesty toward the audience. Notice the aim: to deceive.

## Layer 2: the platform's rules

Each platform sets its own rules for what you post there.

**YouTube** requires creators to disclose when they use AI "to meaningfully alter or generate photorealistic content". Disclosure is needed for content that:

- makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not do
- alters footage of a real event or place
- generates a realistic scene that did not actually occur
- creates music that is the main focus of the video

It is not needed for non-realistic content (an animated rider on a unicorn) or for minor edits. YouTube lists examples that need no disclosure: colour adjustment, captions, upscaling, and "production assistance" such as using AI to improve a script, a thumbnail or a title. Creators answer an "AI use" question in YouTube Studio during upload. YouTube also says disclosing "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money".

YouTube may add a label itself for content that contains C2PA metadata or that its systems detect as AI. A creator can usually correct a mistaken label, but not for "content containing C2PA metadata". Creators who "consistently choose not to disclose" may face a manual label, removal of content, or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program.

**Meta** described its approach in April 2024. The post adds updates from July and September 2024, and shows a last update of October 23, 2025. Its "AI info" label can appear when Meta detects industry-standard AI indicators or when people disclose. In July 2024 Meta renamed the label from "Made with AI". It said content with minor AI changes, such as retouching, had carried indicators and was labelled. In September 2024 it moved the label into the post's menu for content only edited by AI tools. It kept the visible label for content it detects was generated by an AI tool.

**TikTok** said in May 2024 that it labels content made with its own AI effects, that it has required creators to label realistic AI content, and that it can read Content Credentials to label content from other platforms.

These pages are dated and platforms change them. Read the current page in the week you launch.

## Layer 3: the law, with the EU as one example

The European Commission's page says the transparency duties in Article 50 of the AI Act apply from 2 August 2026. They cover marking and detecting AI content and labelling deepfakes.

- **Providers** of generative AI systems must make sure outputs are marked in a machine-readable way and are detectable as AI.
- **Deployers** must disclose a **deepfake**. The page defines it as image, audio or video content that "resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful".

The Commission's Code of Practice is a voluntary tool. But the page says: "Even though adherence to the code is voluntary, the transparency requirements under article 50 of the AI Act are legal obligations." The EU has also made a set of icons that deployers may use.

Whether one of your assets counts as a deepfake is a legal question. This page covers the EU only. Other countries have other rules.

## Putting it together

This table is our own planning tool, not a rule.

| Asset                                   | Ask before export                                          |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Realistic scene that never happened     | Platform disclosure on? Does the law need a label? Lawyer. |
| Clearly stylised art                    | Does the platform say no disclosure is needed? Keep a note |
| AI help on a script, title or thumbnail | Check the platform's list of minor edits                   |
| Chat helper on your own site            | Tell visitors at the start of each session                 |

## What this means for your work

- **Decide the label per asset, before export.** Do not leave it to the person who clicks upload.
- **Do not strip credentials to avoid a label.** Platforms can also detect AI by other means, and some labels cannot be changed.
- **Never hide AI use in order to deceive.** The vendors' policies name that aim.
- **Re-read the platform page at launch.** Rules move.
- **Send legal questions to a lawyer.** Give them the asset and the market.
