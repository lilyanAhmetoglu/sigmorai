## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Choose how to make a clip longer: extend it, chain it by a frame, or cut to a new shot.
- Plan an extension on Veo or Luma inside the vendor's rules, and count the total length.
- Check every seam, and keep the ids and files you need for later.

## The problem

A travel agency wants a 24-second clip of a ferry trip. The ferry leaves the harbour, crosses the bay, and arrives at an island. The agency already approved one 5-second clip of the island pier.

The first try asks one model for "a 24-second ferry trip". It does not work. The [Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) describe Veo 3.1 as "a model for generating 8-second videos". Shorter lengths work too: `durationSeconds`, the "Length of the generated video", takes "4", "6" or "8". Most video models we cite make short clips (our reading). So a long clip is made of parts, and every join between two parts is a risk.

## The idea

### Seams

Every join between two parts is a **seam**. A seam is where things break: a jacket changes colour, the sun jumps to the other side, a voice stops.

There are three ways to go longer (our grouping):

| Way    | What happens                          | Result              |
| ------ | ------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Extend | The model continues a clip it made    | one continuous take |
| Chain  | A saved frame of clip A starts clip B | two clips, joined   |
| Cut    | Separate shots, joined on purpose     | a visible cut       |

### Extend on Veo

The Gemini API docs describe Veo extension in three parts:

- How much: you can "extend videos that you previously generated with Veo by 7 seconds and up to 20 times".
- Where it starts: "Extend finalizes the final second or 24 frames of your video and continues the action."
- What you get: "a single video combining the user input video and the generated extended video for up to 148 seconds of video".

The rules, as of October 2026:

- **Models.** Extension is for "Veo 3.1 & Veo 3.1 Fast models only, not Veo 3.1 Lite".
- **Source.** "Gemini API only supports video extensions for Veo-generated videos."
- **Resolution.** "Video extension is also limited to 720p videos."
- **Age.** "You can only extend videos that were generated or referenced in the last two days." If a video "is referenced for extension, its 2-day storage timer resets".

The extension prompt says only what happens next. The example in the Gemini API docs is short: "Extend this video with the paraglider slowly descending."

Watch the sound. The docs note: "voice is not able to be effectively extended if it's not present in the last 1 second of video". Our reading: if a voice must go on after the seam, it must still be speaking in the last second of the clip you extend.

### Extend on Luma and Runway

On Luma, you pass the old clip's id as a frame. The [Luma docs](https://docs.agents.lumalabs.ai/guides/videos/generation) give two shapes:

- `video.start_frame` with the id: "Forward extend — the prior clip becomes the start, and the new generation continues after it".
- `video.end_frame` with the id: "Backward extend — the prior clip becomes the end, and the new generation is prepended before it". (Prepended means added before.)

Luma's extend is "standard dynamic range only". (HDR, high dynamic range, is the format with a wider range of bright and dark.) And `video.loop: true` "is only supported on forward extend".

On Runway, some video-to-video models take a `mode` of `extend`. For Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 there, the [API reference](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/api) says `duration` "is the amount of footage added to the input video". Rules change per model, so read each one.

### Chain by a frame

This method is ours. It works with any model that takes a first frame. Save the last frame of clip A as an image. Use it as the first frame of clip B. Then join A and B in your editor.

The Gemini API docs point to this habit for first and last frames: "Upload an image or use a frame from a previous video generation to make sure your scene begins and concludes exactly as you envision it."

Our advice: chain when you change model between parts, or when extension is not offered for your clip.

### Cut on purpose

Not every seam must hide. The [Runway Multi-Shot recipe](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/multi-shot-video) makes videos "composed of multiple shots that cut between scenes". Its docs give two tips:

- "Write one clear idea per shot".
- "Reuse the same product or style language across shots for visual consistency."

The [Google prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/video/video-gen-prompt-guide) lists editing words for a prompt, such as "match cut", "jump cut" and "montage".

A small comparison: an extension is one long sentence, and a cut is a new sentence. Both are fine. A seam that tries to hide and fails is the problem.

### Check every seam

Our checklist. Watch each seam at full speed, then frame by frame:

| Check     | Question                                           |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Subject   | Same clothes, same product, same logo?             |
| Light     | Sun on the same side? Same time of day?            |
| Direction | Still moving the same way across the frame?        |
| Camera    | Does the move keep its speed and direction?        |
| Sound     | Does the background noise jump? Does a voice stop? |

Our advice: when a seam fails, repair the later part, not the earlier one. Remake it from the final good frame, and name what broke in the new prompt.

### Keep the parts

Long clips have many parts to lose.

- Luma ids: "The generation id does not expire — keep it if you plan to edit, extend, or reframe the video later."
- Luma downloads: links "expire after 1 hour".
- Veo: "Generated videos are stored on the server for 2 days".

So keep a parts log (our habit): part name, tool, id, prompt, last frame file, and where the download lives.

## Worked example

The ferry clip again: about 24 seconds, ending on the approved 5-second island clip. That island clip was filmed by the agency's own camera crew.

### Step 1: the weak plan

```text
One request: "A 24-second clip of a ferry leaving the harbour, crossing
the bay and arriving at the island pier."
```

No model here makes 24 seconds in one go, and nothing ties the end to the approved island clip.

### Step 2: the crossing as one take

The harbour and the crossing should feel like one take, so we extend. We pick Veo 3.1 Fast at 720p.

```text
Part 1  Veo 3.1 Fast, 720p, 8 s
        "Wide shot. A white ferry leaves a small stone harbour at
        morning. Slow truck right, following the ferry. Gulls, engine hum."
Part 2  extend Part 1 by 7 s
        "The ferry moves out into open water. The camera keeps the
        same slow truck right."
```

The extension prompt says only what happens next, and repeats the camera move. Total so far: 8 + 7 = 15 seconds, as one video.

### Step 3: the hard case

**The hard case is the end.** The island clip is camera footage, not a generated clip. Veo extends only Veo-made videos, and Luma extends by generation id, so neither can extend the crossing into it.

So we build a short bridge with first and last frames (chapter 2):

```text
Part 3  Veo 3.1, 720p, 4 s
        first frame: the last frame of Part 2
        last frame:  the first frame of the approved island clip
        "The ferry slows and turns toward the island pier."
Part 4  the approved island clip, 5 s
```

The bridge starts where the crossing ends, and it ends where the island clip starts. Total: 15 + 4 + 5 = 24 seconds.

### Step 4: check and log

Check two seams: Part 2 to Part 3, and Part 3 to Part 4. Use the seam checklist above. Then log every part's id and file.

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Every part is a length the model can make.**
- **The approved clip stays as it is.** The bridge leads into it.
- **Each seam has a plan:** an extension, or a bridge with fixed frames.

## Common mistakes

1. **Describing the whole scene again in an extension prompt.** The model may restart the action. Fix: say only what happens next.
2. **Changing the camera move at a seam.** A slow truck in one part and a fast one in the next shows the join. Fix: write the same move into both prompts.
3. **Trying to hide every seam.** Some changes of place or time look better as cuts. Fix: cut on purpose, and keep the product and style words the same in every shot.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

Pick extend, chain or cut for each job, and give one reason.

1. A 36-second continuous Veo take of a hot-air balloon rising. How many 7-second extensions does an 8-second Veo clip need?
2. A recipe video jumps from the kitchen to the garden table.
3. A Veo clip must continue on another vendor's model, which takes a first frame.

### Answer

1. **Extend.** It is one continuous take. 8 + 7 × 4 = 36, so **four** extensions. That is within "up to 20 times".
2. **Cut.** The place changes, so a visible cut is honest. Keep the style words the same in both shots.
3. **Chain.** Veo extension works only on Veo videos, and the other model is not Veo. Save the last frame and use it as the first frame of the next clip.

## Recap

- A long clip is short parts joined at seams. Extend for one continuous take, chain by a frame across tools, and cut on purpose when the place or time changes.
- Plan inside the rules: on Veo, extension adds 7 seconds at a time, at 720p, on Veo 3.1 or 3.1 Fast, for recent Veo videos. On Luma, an id in the start frame extends forward and an id in the end frame extends backward.
- Check every seam for subject, light, direction, camera and sound, and keep every id and file.
