## Why it matters

The client approved a 16:9 master: a perfume bottle on a marble shelf, 10 seconds. Now the media plan asks for more. A 9:16 version for stories. A 1:1 version for the feed. A 4K file for a shop screen. A ProRes file for the editor.

The master is what the client approved. Every new file should stay true to it.

Bootstrap Village, chapter 4, listed each model's sizes. Tool Forge, chapter 4, covered file types, auto-cropping and expiry. This chapter plans the deliverables from one master.

## Three ways to a new shape

This grouping is ours.

| Way        | What happens                               | Risk                   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Crop       | cut the master down to the new shape       | the edges are lost     |
| Outpaint   | keep the master, add new picture around it | the new area can drift |
| Regenerate | make a new take in the new shape           | everything is new      |

**Crop** works when the subject sits in the middle with space around it. If the bottle sits on the right, a 9:16 crop can cut it.

**Outpaint** means the model adds new picture beyond the edges.

- **Luma** `video_reframe` "outpaints an existing video to a new target aspect ratio". Luma says: "The source video content is preserved frame-for-frame". Use it for "the same clip in a different shape". Use `video_edit` (chapter 4) to restyle.
- **Runway Aleph 2.0** has `targetAspectRatio`, a "Target aspect ratio for expand/outpaint". It "Letterboxes the input video and keyframes before generation".

**Regenerate.** On Veo, the shape is a setting of a new clip: `aspectRatio` is "16:9" (the default) or "9:16". Gemini Omni Flash offers the same two. A new 9:16 take has new motion and new framing, so it needs a new approval (our advice).

Choose on purpose (our advice). Outpaint when the client approved the master's motion. Regenerate when the new shape needs a new layout, such as a tall shot that a wide frame cannot hold.

## Luma reframe rules (October 2026)

- **Six ratios:** 9:16, 3:4, 1:1, 4:3, 16:9 and 21:9.
- **Length:** the source must be 10 seconds or shorter. Output is 5 seconds by default and 10 at most. For a longer clip, Luma says to "split it into ≤10s segments and reframe each one".
- **Vertical 1080p is not ready.** At 1080p, 9:16 and 3:4 return a "coming soon" 400 error. Use 720p for vertical.
- **Position:** by default Luma fits the source "with a centered crop". `video.source_position` sets where the source sits in the new frame instead.
- **Prompt:** it describes "how the new canvas area should be filled".
- **No HDR:** reframe is standard dynamic range only.

Example (ours). The bottle sits on the right of the master. For 9:16, set `source_position` so the bottle stays in the frame. Prompt: "Extend the marble wall and the soft window light above and below the shelf."

## Upscale: bigger, not newer

To **upscale** is to make a clip bigger in pixels.

- **Runway Magnific Video Upscaler** (`video_upscale`) raises a clip to 4K. Inputs can be "at most 30 seconds". Its `creativity` setting runs "from 0 (faithful) to 100" and sets "How much AI-generated detail to add". For an approved master, keep it low (our advice). High creativity adds detail the client never saw.
- **Runway Enhance Frame Rate** changes a clip to a set frame rate, such as 25 or 29.97. Inputs can be at most 300 seconds.
- **Google.** Omni marks its 1080p and 4K outputs as "upscaled". Veo 3.1 "can also directly generate 720p, 1080p or 4k videos".

## Delivery formats

An MP4 is fine for social media. An editor or a colourist often wants more. Runway says film work often needs "a container it can open directly, such as ProRes or a PNG sequence".

On Gen-4.5 and Aleph 2.0, Runway's `outputFormat` sets the file:

| Value              | What you get                                                 |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `mp4` (default)    | an H.264 .mp4                                                |
| `prores`           | a ProRes .mov                                                |
| `png_sequence`     | a .zip of PNG frames, plus a separate .wav if there is sound |
| `sdr_rec709_10bit` | a 10-bit Rec.709 .mp4, for grading                           |

Two traps:

- **ProRes 4444 is not a matte.** It is the default ProRes profile. But Runway says its alpha channel "is present but fully opaque": "choose these tiers for 12-bit 4:4:4 color fidelity, not for mattes". (A matte is a file with see-through parts.)
- **These files are standard range.** Runway: "HDR is a separate delivery."

**HDR** (high dynamic range) "stores a wider span of brightness than standard dynamic range (SDR)".

- **Runway.** Gen-4.5 can make HDR directly. Ruby (`video_to_hdr`) converts an SDR clip, "including an Aleph edit", to HDR. Inputs are at most 30 seconds. Runway says the output "keeps the source's own pixels".
- **Luma.** `hdr: true` makes an HDR MP4 (720p or 1080p, not 10 seconds, no loop). `exr_export` adds an EXR file "for professional colour-grading workflows". Reframe and extend stay standard range.

## Order the steps

Our advice: edit first, then reframe, then upscale, then convert the format. Each step works on the approved result of the step before.

Two facts from the docs back this order (our reading). Luma's reframe rejects HDR, so HDR comes after it. And Ruby can turn an SDR result into HDR at the end.

Send a delivery sheet with the files. This format is ours:

Keep every source id. Luma says to keep the generation id "if you plan to edit, extend, or reframe the video later". Download every file the same day (Tool Forge, chapter 4).

## What this means for your work

- **One master, many shapes.** Crop, outpaint or regenerate on purpose.
- **Outpaint to keep approved motion.** Regenerate for a new layout.
- **Check reframe limits:** 10 seconds, six ratios, no vertical 1080p yet.
- **Upscale faithfully:** low creativity on approved work.
- **Pick the format for the next person:** MP4 for social, ProRes or PNG frames for the editor.
- **HDR is its own delivery**, and ProRes 4444 is not a matte.
- **Send a delivery sheet** with every source id.
