## Why it matters

A client asks: "Show the new grinder, with some camera movement." One take spins the grinder. Another is a shaky close-up. Neither is wrong: the brief named no move.

Prompt Forest, chapter 5, gave the camera a slot in a video prompt. Chapter 8 of the same realm gave you distance, position and lens words for still images. This chapter fills in what a still image does not have: **how the camera moves during the shot**.

A note on the main source. In October 2026, Google's old Vertex AI link for its video prompt guide opens the same guide in its Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform docs, written for Veo and Gemini Omni Flash. We use its lists as a word bank, not as a promise that every model obeys every word.

## Turn or travel

Google's guide defines each camera move. We sort them into two groups (our grouping): the camera **turns** on one spot, or the camera **travels** through the space.

| Group   | Move          | Google's definition (short)                                                    |
| ------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| None    | Static        | "the camera remains completely still"                                          |
| Turns   | Pan           | "rotates horizontally left or right from a fixed position"                     |
| Turns   | Tilt          | "rotates vertically up or down from a fixed position"                          |
| Turns   | Whip pan      | "an extremely fast pan that blurs the image"                                   |
| Travels | Dolly         | "physically moves closer to the subject or further away"                       |
| Travels | Truck         | "physically moves horizontally (sideways) left or right"                       |
| Travels | Pedestal      | "physically moves vertically up or down while maintaining a level perspective" |
| Travels | Crane         | moves "vertically (up or down) or in sweeping arcs"                            |
| Travels | Arc           | "moves in a circular or semi-circular path around the subject"                 |
| Travels | Aerial, drone | "taken from a high altitude", "often involving smooth, flying movements"       |

**Handheld** is its own kind. Google says the camera "is held by the operator, resulting in less stable, often jerky movements that can convey realism, immediacy, or unease".

## A zoom is not a dolly

Google defines a **zoom** as the lens changing "its focal length to magnify or de-magnify the subject". It adds: "This is different from a dolly, as the camera itself doesn't move."

So pick the word for what you mean. "Slow zoom in on the grinder" asks for a bigger picture from the same spot. "Slow dolly in toward the grinder" asks for the camera itself to come closer.

Google also lists a move that uses both at once: the **dolly zoom** (or vertigo effect). The camera dollies one way "while simultaneously zooming the lens in the opposite direction". The subject stays "roughly the same size", but "the background perspective changes dramatically, often conveying disorientation or unease".

## Moves inside the lens

Some moves happen in the focus, not the camera body. A **rack focus** is "shifting the focus of the lens from one subject or plane of depth to another within a single, continuous shot".

Google adds a warning to its lens list: "Some advanced camera lenses are not officially supported. The results and reliability may vary depending on the overall prompt and your specific use case." Its camera-angle list has the same note. So test a rare move on a short draft first (our advice).

## Write the move as a sentence

All three video vendors here show the move written into the prompt.

- **Google Veo.** The Veo page lists "Camera positioning and motion" as an optional prompt part, with terms like "aerial view, eye-level, top-down shot, dolly shot, or worms eye".
- **Google's guide** writes a whole move with a start and an end: "The camera starts wide, showing the map and a flickering candle, then smoothly zooms in until the intricate, glowing symbols on the compass face fill the entire frame."
- **Luma.** Its video page says: "Be specific about subject, motion, camera movement, lighting, and pacing." Its example prompt is "A slow dolly shot through a misty greenhouse at sunrise".
- **Runway.** The Product Ad recipe has a `userConcept` field for "brand voice, scene specifics, lighting, camera motion, and narrative". Its example: "Bright, refreshing summer campaign with slow dolly moves".

From these, we use one pattern (ours): **speed, move, direction, from what, to what.**

> Slow dolly in from a wide view of the counter to a close-up of the grinder's dial.

## Who moves: the camera or the subject?

Runway's Multi-Shot page has this example shot: "Product hero shot rotating against a clean background". Does the product spin, or does the camera go around it? The words allow both.

Google's guide keeps them apart. An **arc shot** is the camera moving around the subject. "Spinning" is in its list of things a subject does. So say which one moves (our advice):

- "The grinder turns slowly on a turntable. Static shot."
- "Slow arc shot around the grinder, which stays still."

## One main move, with a reason

Prompt Forest, chapter 5, said one camera setup per shot. We add our own rule: **one main move per shot.** A tilt, a truck and a zoom in one short clip are really three shots. Google's tilt and compass examples each use one move with a clear start and end.

Pick the move for its feeling. Google ties moves to moods: a dolly out "to emphasize their isolation", a crane "for dramatic reveals", handheld for "realism, immediacy, or unease". A calm premium brand may not want unease in its product shot.

## Check every take

This checklist is ours:

1. **Did the camera move at all?** Or did only the subject move?
2. **Is it the right move?** A zoom when you asked for a dolly is a miss.
3. **Right direction and speed?** "Slow pan left" should not be a fast pan right.
4. **Did anything move that should stay still?** The product, the label, the horizon.
5. **Where does the move end?** Chapter 3 joins clips at that point.

## What this means for your work

- **Name the move.** Pan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal, crane, arc, aerial, handheld, static.
- **Zoom is the lens, dolly is the camera.** Choose the one you mean.
- **Write speed, move, direction, start and end** in one sentence.
- **Say who moves:** the camera, the subject, or both.
- **One main move per shot**, chosen for the feeling it gives.
- **Test rare moves first.** Google says some are not officially supported.
