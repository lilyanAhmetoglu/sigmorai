## Why it matters

A coffee roaster has one approved still of its new bag. It wants three short clips from it: a slow loop for the web header, a product turn for social, and a logo card for the end of an ad.

The still is approved. The motion is not. This chapter is about adding motion to one approved still without losing what the client approved.

Prompt Forest, chapter 5, showed that a video model can start from an image. Tool Forge, chapter 6, showed how Runway's Product Ad recipe runs. Here we choose which motion fits a still, and how to keep its look.

## When a still beats text

Our rule: start from a still when the look is already approved. A text prompt alone draws a new bag every time.

The vendors point the same way:

- **Google Veo** uses "the input image as the initial frame". Google says to pick the image "closest to what you envision as the first scene".
- **Runway's Product Ad tips** say to "let the product images define what the product looks like".
- **Google's Omni page** lists product shots, illustrations and photographs as image-to-video inputs.

Text is still the better start when nothing is approved yet and you want ideas (our advice).

## Say how the image is used

Models use your image in different ways (October 2026):

| Model                         | How the image is used                    |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Veo 3.1                       | the first frame                          |
| Runway Gen-4.5 image to video | the first frame (`position: first` only) |
| Luma `ray-3.2`                | `start_frame`, `end_frame`, or both      |
| Gemini Omni Flash             | the model decides, based on your prompt  |

Google says that for Omni, "Depending on your prompt, the model will decide how to use the image." So say it. Google's own line is: "Use this image as the starting frame."

Then describe the motion. Google warns that vague prompts like "make it move" "produce less compelling results than detailed descriptions of the camera movement, subject motion, and environmental effects".

## Loops that don't jump

A **seamless loop** is a clip whose end flows back into its start. It can repeat with no visible jump, which suits a web header.

**Luma.** Set `video.loop: true`. Luma says it "generates a seamlessly looping video". The rules:

- `loop` is for new videos only (`type: "video"`). An edit rejects it.
- It is rejected with `duration: "10s"`, `hdr`, `end_frame` or `keyframes`.
- So a loop from your still is: `start_frame` set to the approved still, `loop: true`, 5 seconds, no HDR.

**Google Omni.** Use the same image as the first and the last frame: `[# Sources <FIRST_FRAME>@Image1 <LAST_FRAME>@Image1]`. Google says this creates "a video that loops". Its guiding line is: "Use this image as the first frame and the last frame."

For any other model, check its reference for a loop setting before you promise a loop (our advice).

## Motion that can come back

A loop must end where it began. So choose motion that returns (our advice):

- **Loops well:** steam rising from a cup, a slow light sweep, leaves moving in a light wind.
- **Loops badly:** a dolly in. The camera ends closer than it started, so the repeat jumps back.

Chapter 1 sorted moves into "turn" and "travel". A camera that travels rarely loops. Write it into the prompt: "Static shot. The bag does not move. Steam rises from the cup beside it."

Check a loop by playing it three times in a row. Watch the join. Does the steam jump? Does a shadow snap back?

## Product turns

A **product turn** shows a product from more than one side. Chapter 1 asked: who moves? Decide first. Is the bag on a turntable, or does the camera arc around it?

A front-only still does not show the back of the bag. In our reading, the model must invent it. Runway's Product Ad tips ask for reference images "from multiple angles — front, back, and sides". Runway adds: "The first image is used as the primary product reference".

So approve a side and a back view as stills first (Context Library, chapter 5, built views this way). On Luma, you can pin the approved front at frame 0 and the approved side at frame 120 of a 5-second clip (chapter 2).

If the client approved only the front, keep the turn small: a slow quarter arc shows depth without the back (our advice).

## Logos and brand cards

A logo needs exact letters. Prompt Forest, chapter 4, said to finish exact things by hand.

Our advice for a logo card:

- **Animate around the logo.** Use a light sweep, or a slow push in on the background. Keep the logo itself still.
- **Or leave it out.** Animate a clean card with no logo, then place the real logo file in your editor.
- **Name the text.** If the model will draw text, Google says "it can help to define what it should say".
- **Check every frame.** Letters can bend in the middle of a clip, not only at the ends.

## What holds in a still

Chapter 2's beat sheet works here too. List what holds (logo, label, colours, the bag's shape) and what may move (steam, light, background). Then write the holds into the prompt: "The bag and its label do not move or change."

Compare the clip with the approved still at the first frame, the middle and the end (our advice).

## What this means for your work

- **Start from the approved still** when the look is set.
- **Say how the image is used:** first frame, last frame or reference.
- **Loop with a tool that supports it:** Luma `loop`, or the same first and last frame on Omni.
- **Pick motion that can come back** to where it began.
- **Plan turns** with approved side and back views.
- **Keep logos exact:** animate around them, or add them in the editor.
