## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Find the billing unit on a pricing page and turn it into a cost for one image, clip or voice track.
- Budget for every try, not only the results you keep.
- Cut a bill in half with batch for work that can wait, and spot prices that change on a date.

## The problem

An indie game studio asks for a quote:

- 120 character portraits, square, 1K, for a wiki.
- 5 short gameplay teaser clips, 6 seconds each, 720p.
- A 3-minute voice-over for a trailer, recorded in January next year.

A designer reads "$30" on the pricing page and quotes $30 for the images. Another says the clips cost "a few cents". Both are wrong. The first read the price per million tokens as the price per image. The second forgot that most clips are thrown away.

## The idea

Every pricing page bills a **unit**. Find the unit first. It is like a taxi: the meter shows a price per kilometre, not per trip. You still need the distance.

```text
units per asset  x  price per unit  =  cost of one try
cost of one try  x  tries per keeper =  cost per kept asset
```

This method is Sigmorai's, not a vendor rule. The prices below were checked on the live pages on 2026-10-08. They change, so open the live page before you quote.

**The unit differs by model.**

| What you make                | Unit on the pricing page          | Example                                         |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Image (Gemini Nano Banana)   | image output tokens               | Nano Banana 2.1: $30 per 1M tokens              |
| Image (OpenAI GPT Image)     | image output tokens               | GPT Image 2.5 models: $30 per 1M tokens         |
| Video (Google Veo 3.1)       | seconds of video                  | Veo 3.1 Fast: $0.10 (720p), $0.12 (1080p) per s |
| Voice (Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS) | audio tokens, also shown per 10 s | $0.00225 per 10 s of audio (until 2026 ends)    |
| Transcript (OpenAI)          | minutes of audio (estimated cost) | gpt-transcribe: $0.0045 per minute              |

**Images: tokens per image.** The [Gemini pricing page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) says Nano Banana 2.1 images "at 1K (1024x1024px) consume 1120 tokens and are equivalent to $0.0336 per image". At 2K it is 1680 tokens ($0.0504). At 4K it is "3780 tokens" ($0.113). The size step changes the price.

**Same rate does not mean same cost.** OpenAI's two GPT Image 2.5 models, `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` and `gpt-image-2.5-flare`, have the same token rates on the [OpenAI pricing page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing). But the [OpenAI image docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation) warn: "Equal token rates don't mean equal cost per image: token consumption can differ by model and quality setting." The guide has a calculator. For an earlier model, `gpt-image-2` at 1024×1024, its table shows $0.006 (low), $0.053 (medium) and $0.211 (high) per image. Quality moves the price a lot.

Other things add to an OpenAI image bill. "The final cost is the sum of" input text tokens, input image tokens when you edit, and image output tokens. If you stream previews, "each partial image will incur an additional 100 image output tokens". The guide says to "Use the response's usage to measure token consumption".

**Video: seconds.** The Gemini pricing page lists Veo 3.1 Standard at $0.40 per second (720p and 1080p) and $0.60 (4k). Veo 3.1 Fast is $0.10 (720p), $0.12 (1080p) and $0.30 (4k). The [Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) add: "4k videos are also more expensive". A blocked video is free: "You will not be charged if your video is blocked from generating."

**Batch: wait a day, pay half.** You send many requests at once and collect the results later.

- [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/batch): "50% cost discount", and "Each batch completes within 24 hours". Image generations and edits are on its list of batch endpoints.
- [Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/batch-api): "priced at 50% of the standard interactive API cost", and jobs "are designed to complete within a 24-hour turnaround time". The docs suggest batch when you "need to generate a lot of images".
- [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing): the Message Batches API is also "cutting costs by 50%". Claude makes text, not media, so use it for batch reviews of images, not for making them.

**Prices have dates.** The Gemini pricing page lists Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS audio at "$0.00225 per 10s audio" "through December 31, 2026", and "$0.0045 per 10s audio" "starting January 1, 2027". The rate doubles on that date.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the quote before

```text
Portraits: "$30"          (read from the page)
Clips:     "a few cents"  (a guess)
Voice:     not priced
```

### Step 2: price one try of each asset

```text
Portrait (1K, Nano Banana 2.1, standard)
  1,120 tokens x $30 / 1,000,000 = $0.0336

Clip (6 s, 720p, Veo 3.1 Fast)
  6 s x $0.10 = $0.60

Voice (3 min = 180 s = 18 blocks of 10 s, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS)
  recorded in 2027: 18 x $0.0045 = $0.081
```

### Step 3: add the tries

Run a small test first and count how many tries you need for one result you keep. Say the team keeps 1 portrait in 2, and 1 clip in 3.

```text
Portraits: 120 kept x 2 tries x $0.0336 = $8.064
Clips:       5 kept x 3 tries x $0.60   = $9.00
Voice:     one take                     = $0.081
```

### Step 4: use batch where you can wait

The wiki portraits are not urgent, so run them as a batch at half price:

```text
Portraits on batch: $8.064 / 2 = $4.032
```

The teaser clips are needed for a review tomorrow, so they stay on the normal (interactive) rate.

**The hard case is the voice-over.** It is recorded in January, so the 2027 rate applies. A quote with today's rate ($0.0405) would cover only half the cost.

### Step 5: the quote after

```text
Portraits (batch, 2 tries each)   $4.03
Clips (Veo 3.1 Fast, 3 tries)     $9.00
Voice-over (2027 rate)            $0.08
Prices checked on 2026-10-08. Model costs only.
```

### Step 6: why it is better

- **Every line has a unit.** Tokens, seconds or 10-second blocks, then the price.
- **Tries are in the budget.** The quote covers the drafts you throw away.
- **Batch saves money where time allows.** Urgent work stays on the normal rate.
- **The date is on the quote.** The client knows when the prices were read.

## Common mistakes

1. **Reading the rate as the price per image.** "$30 per 1M tokens" is not $30 per image. Fix: multiply tokens per image by the rate.
2. **Budgeting only for the keepers.** Most tries are thrown away. Fix: measure your own tries per keeper on a small test, then multiply.
3. **Ignoring dates on the page.** A rate can change on a fixed day. Fix: check the date the work happens, and write the check date on the quote.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A florist wants 30 product photos at 2K on Nano Banana 2.1, for next month. Your test shows about 1 kept image in 2 tries. Use the 2K numbers above. What is the cost on the standard rate, and on batch?

### Answer

- One try at 2K: 1,680 tokens × $30 / 1,000,000 = $0.0504.
- Tries: 30 kept × 2 = 60 tries.
- Standard: 60 × $0.0504 = $3.024.
- Batch (half price): $1.512. The photos are for next month, so batch fits.

## Recap

- Find the unit (tokens, seconds, minutes), then multiply: units × price = cost of one try.
- Budget for every try. A kept asset costs the price of one try, times the number of tries you need for one good result.
- Batch halves the price at OpenAI and Gemini when you can wait up to 24 hours. Note the date beside each price.
