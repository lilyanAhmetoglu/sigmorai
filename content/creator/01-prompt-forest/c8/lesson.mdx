## Why it matters

Chapter 1 put "camera" and "format" in the five-part checklist. This chapter fills them in. Where is the camera? Which lens? How wide is the picture? The answer to each one changes what the model draws.

Example: one sneaker, three framings. A close-up shows the stitching. A wide shot shows the street. A tall 9:16 frame fits a phone story; a 16:9 frame fits a website header. Same subject, three different jobs.

## Distance and position

Google's older Imagen guide has a table of "photography modifiers". Two rows are about the camera:

- **Camera proximity:** "Close up, taken from far away". Its examples are "A close-up photo of coffee beans" and "A zoomed out photo of a small bag of coffee beans in a messy kitchen".
- **Camera position:** "aerial, from below". Example: "A photo of a forest canopy with blue skies from below".

This guide is Google's Vertex AI image prompt guide (October 2026). Google marks the Vertex AI docs as "no longer being updated", and the Gemini API page says Imagen models are shut down there. We use its tables as a word list, not as rules for today's models.

The current guides use the same kind of words:

- **Google Gemini.** The photo template ends: "Shot from a [camera angle] with a [lens type]". Its example: "Shot from a low perspective with a wide-angle lens". The product template has "a slightly elevated 45-degree shot". The best-practices list names "wide-angle shot, macro shot, low-angle perspective".
- **OpenAI.** Its prompting guide's photo example says "medium close-up at eye level, using a 50mm lens". For people, it says to describe "body framing", such as "full body visible, feet included".

## Lens and focal length

Imagen's guide lists lens types: "35mm, 50mm, fisheye, wide angle, macro". It also has a table of lenses for photorealistic images:

| Use case                                    | Lens type      | Focal length |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------ |
| People (portraits)                          | Prime, zoom    | 24-35mm      |
| Food, insects, plants (objects, still life) | Macro          | 60-105mm     |
| Sports, wildlife (motion)                   | Telephoto zoom | 100-400mm    |
| Astronomical, landscape (wide-angle)        | Wide-angle     | 10-24mm      |

Google adds: "Take these keywords as general guidance". They "aren't required to achieve your goal".

OpenAI gives the same warning in other words: "Treat camera specifications as cues for appearance, not a guarantee of exact physical simulation." So "100mm macro" asks for the look of a macro shot. It does not make the model work like a real lens.

Example, for a bakery's menu: "A plate of cinnamon buns, macro lens, 100mm, shallow depth of field, soft side light."

## Aspect ratio: pick it for the use

Imagen's guide gives a use for each of its five ratios:

| Ratio | Imagen's suggested use                                           |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1:1   | The default. Social media posts.                                 |
| 4:3   | Media, film and photography; more of the scene side to side.     |
| 3:4   | More of the scene top to bottom.                                 |
| 16:9  | TVs, monitors, landscape phone screens; wide scenic backgrounds. |
| 9:16  | Short-form video apps; tall subjects like buildings or trees.    |

Each current API sets the ratio in its own way:

- **Gemini.** You set `aspect_ratio` (and `image_size`) under `response_format`, when its `type` is `"image"`. By default the model "matches the output image size to that of your input image, or otherwise generates 1:1 squares". Each model has its own list. Nano Banana 2.1 lists 14 ratios, from 1:8 to 8:1, including 4:1 and 21:9.
- **OpenAI.** You set `size` in pixels. Common sizes are 1024x1024 (square), 1536x1024 (landscape) and 1024x1536 (portrait). Custom sizes are allowed within rules: both edges multiples of 16, no edge over 3,840 pixels, and the long edge no more than 3 times the short edge. For slides, its guide says to "use a landscape size for deck-style outputs".

The 3:1 rule matters for banners. A 4:1 web banner fits Gemini's list but not OpenAI's rule. On OpenAI, generate at 3:1 or less, then crop or extend in your design tool (our advice).

Choose the ratio before you generate (our advice). Cropping a square image down to 16:9 cuts off the top and bottom.

## Room for copy

Empty space for a headline is chapter 1's topic: Google's "Minimalist & negative space design" template.

## What the model may not hold

Framing words help, but they are not a layout tool. OpenAI's limits list says the model "may have difficulty placing elements precisely in structured or layout-sensitive compositions". Imagen's guide says the same about text: "you should expect occasional variations" in placement.

So check the framing of every result. Is the product where you asked? Is the horizon where the layout needs it? If a part must be exact, OpenAI's prompting guide says to "composite the approved edit into the original image instead of relying on prompting alone".

## What this means for your work

- **Name the distance.** Close-up, medium close-up, wide shot, zoomed out.
- **Name the position.** Eye level, low angle, from below, aerial, 45-degree.
- **Use lens words as looks.** Macro for detail, wide-angle for space. They are cues, not physics.
- **Set the ratio in the API, for the real use.** Check the model's list or size rules first.
- **Check the framing, then finish exact layouts in your design tool.**
