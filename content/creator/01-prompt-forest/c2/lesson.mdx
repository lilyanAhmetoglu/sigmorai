## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Sort your reference images into jobs (object, character, style) and pick a model whose limits fit.
- Write a prompt that gives every reference image a clear role.
- Protect the details that must not change, look at the result next to the original.

## The problem

A yoga teacher, Ana, sells a steel water bottle with her studio's sun logo. She wants photos for her shop: Ana herself, holding the bottle, in her bright studio.

She has the files: a portrait photo of herself and a clean packshot of the bottle. She uploads both and types "make a nice yoga photo with these". Two things go wrong:

1. The woman in the result looks a bit like Ana, but not enough. Her students will notice.
2. The sun logo on the bottle has gained extra rays.

Words cannot describe a face or a logo well enough. A **reference image** is a picture you give the model with the prompt, so it can use what it sees. But the model still needs to know what each picture is for.

## The idea

A reference image is like a photo you hand to a set designer: "this exact bottle, this person". Without a note, they don't know if you want a copy or just the mood.

The [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation) say the edits endpoint can "Generate new images using other images as a reference". The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) list what a reference can be used for. We group it into three jobs (our grouping, based on the Gemini docs' table):

```text
object     a product or logo that must look exact
character  a person or mascot that must stay the same
style      a picture that shows the look you want
```

**Limits depend on the model.** The Gemini docs' table, shortened:

| Reference job               | Nano Banana 2 Lite | Nano Banana 2.1 and Nano Banana 2 | Nano Banana Pro |
| --------------------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Objects, high fidelity      | up to 14           | up to 10                          | up to 6         |
| Characters, kept consistent | N/A                | up to 4                           | up to 5         |
| Style references            | N/A                | N/A                               | up to 3         |

The limits count images. The docs also say Nano Banana 2 Lite is "Not optimized for multiple reference inputs". So count your jobs before you pick a model.

**Give every image a role.** Three habits from the docs:

- The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) give a template for a composite scene: "Take the [element from image 1] and place it with/on the [element from image 2]."
- To protect a face or a logo, the Gemini docs say to "describe them in great detail along with your edit request".
- The [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision) label each image ("Image 1:", "Image 2:"), so you can refer to it by name.

**A reference helps. It does not promise a copy.** The OpenAI docs warn that the model "may occasionally struggle to maintain visual consistency for recurring characters or brand elements across multiple generations".

Three more facts:

- **Clear images.** The [Gemini image-understanding docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding) say: "Use clear, non-blurry images." The Claude docs say the same.
- **Rights.** Before its editing examples, the Gemini docs say: "Make sure you have the necessary rights to any images you upload."
- **Cost.** Every reference picture counts as an image input. The OpenAI docs say that on `gpt-image-2`, "edit requests that include reference images can use more input tokens", and to use the response's `usage` to measure what a request used.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak prompt

```text
Make a nice yoga photo with these.
```

What goes wrong:

- No image has a role. The model guesses which one is the person and which is the product.
- Nothing says what must stay exact.

### Step 2: list the jobs, then pick the model

```text
ana-portrait.jpg   character  (Ana, her own photo)
bottle-pack.png    object     (the product with the sun logo)
```

One character and one object. Nano Banana 2.1 covers this (up to 4 characters, up to 10 objects). The Lite model is not a good fit: it is not optimized for several references.

### Step 3: give each image a role

```text
Image 1: a portrait photo of Ana.
Image 2: a product photo of a steel water bottle with a sun logo.

Create a lifestyle photo for an online shop. Take the woman from image 1
and let her hold the bottle from image 2 in her right hand, label facing
the camera. She stands in a bright yoga studio with pale wooden floors
and large windows. Soft daylight from the left. Medium shot, eye level.
Portrait 4:5.
```

### Step 4: protect the details

Add the details that must not drift, in words:

```text
Ensure that Ana's face, her short curly dark hair and her features remain
completely unchanged. The sun logo on the bottle has exactly eight straight
rays around a plain circle. Keep its shape and colour as in image 2.
```

### Step 5: the hard case, a group photo with a mood board

Ana now wants a class photo: Ana plus four students (all have signed a photo release), the bottle, and her mood-board photo for the look.

```text
characters  5   (Ana + 4 students)
objects     1   (bottle)
style       1   (mood board)
```

Nano Banana 2.1 allows only 4 characters and shows N/A for style. Nano Banana Pro allows up to 5 characters, up to 6 objects and up to 3 style references. So this job needs Pro.

**Why this is the hard case:** the model that was fine in Step 2 fails here, and the reason is in one table row. Count first, then pick.

### Step 6: check against the original

Put the result next to `ana-portrait.jpg` and `bottle-pack.png`. Count the logo rays. If the logo still drifts, place the real logo file in your design tool.

### Why it is better

- **Every image has a role**, so the model does not guess.
- **The critical details are in words**: face, hair, the logo's shape.
- **The model fits the jobs**, checked against the vendor's table.
- **You compare with the source files**, because the docs say drift can happen.

## Common mistakes

1. **References with no roles.** The model mixes up which image is the person and which is the product. Fix: label each image and say what to take from it.
2. **Ignoring the model's limits.** Too many characters, or a style reference on a model that shows N/A. Fix: count the jobs and read the table for that exact model.
3. **Trusting the copy.** A logo can lose or gain a detail. Fix: compare with the original file, and place the exact logo by hand if needed.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A children's author wants a book-launch image. She has: 2 drawings of her mascot (a hedgehog), 1 photo of the printed book, and 2 paintings that show the style she wants. Answer:

1. How many images does each job (object, character, style) use?
2. Which model in the table fits all of them in one request?
3. Write the first two lines of the prompt.

### Answer

1. Character: 2 images (the mascot drawings). Object: 1 image (the book). Style: 2 images (the paintings).
2. Nano Banana Pro. It is the only column with style references (up to 3). Its character and object limits also fit.
3. For example:

```text
Image 1 and Image 2: the hedgehog mascot. Image 3: the printed book.
Image 4 and Image 5: style references for the colours and brush texture.
```

## Recap

- Sort references into jobs: object, character, style. Then check the table for the model you use.
- Give every image a label and a role, and describe in words what must not change.
- A reference is guidance, not a copy. Compare with the original file before you send.
