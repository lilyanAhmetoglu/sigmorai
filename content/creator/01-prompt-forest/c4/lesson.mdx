## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Edit one part of an image with words, and say what must stay the same.
- Prepare a correct mask file for a mask edit, and know what a mask does not promise.
- Plan edits one change at a time, look at the result next to the original.

## The problem

A bakery has an approved photo of a chocolate birthday cake. It has fresh strawberries on top, and the words "Happy 30th, Mia" in white icing on the side. The cake stands on a slate board in warm café light.

The owner says: "Perfect. But we sell this one with blueberries. Swap the strawberries."

Her nephew types a new prompt: "a chocolate cake with blueberries on a slate board". The new picture has blueberries. It also has a different cake shape, different light, and "Happy 30th, Mia" now reads "Hapy 30th Mai".

He regenerated the whole picture. He needed an **edit**: keep the image and change only one named part. An edit of one area is called **inpainting**.

## The idea

An edit is like asking a retoucher to fix one detail in a finished photo, not asking for a new shoot.

Not every model can do it. The [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision) say Claude "is an image understanding model only" and "cannot generate, produce, edit, manipulate, or create images". So this lesson uses the OpenAI and Gemini docs.

There are two ways to say where to edit:

```text
by words   "change only the X, keep everything else"
by mask    a file that marks the area to replace
```

**By words.** The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) call this "Inpainting (semantic masking)". Their template has three parts: the provided image, the one element to change and what it becomes, and an order to keep the rest, "preserving the original style, lighting, and composition".

For adding or removing things, the Gemini docs say the model "will match the original image's style, lighting, and perspective". The template there ends with "Ensure the change is [description of how the change should integrate]."

**By mask.** The [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation) accept an image plus a mask. The rules:

- The image and the mask "must be of the same format and size (less than 50MB in size)".
- "The mask image must also contain an alpha channel."
- If you send more than one input image, the docs say the mask is used on the first image only.

And one warning: masking is "entirely prompt-based". The mask is a guide, and the model may not keep to its outline exactly. So check the edges.

**One change per turn.** The Gemini docs call multi-turn conversation "the recommended way to iterate on images". Their own example asks for one change and adds: "Do not change any other elements of the image." In OpenAI's Responses API, setting `action: "edit"` forces an edit "when an image is in context".

**Size.** The Gemini docs say: "By default, the model matches the output image size to that of your input image". That helps when the shop needs the same size back.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak plan

```text
A chocolate birthday cake with blueberries on a slate board.
```

What goes wrong:

- It is a new image, not an edit. Cake, light and icing all change.
- Nothing says what must stay the same.

### Step 2: edit by words

Send the approved photo with this prompt:

```text
Using the provided photo of the cake, change only the strawberries on top
to a generous pile of fresh blueberries with a light dusting of icing sugar.
Keep everything else in the image exactly the same, preserving the original
style, lighting, and composition. Keep the cake shape, the slate board and
the white icing text on the side unchanged.
```

The first two sentences follow the Gemini docs' template. The last sentence names the parts that matter most to the owner.

### Step 3: or edit with a mask

On an OpenAI model you can paint the area instead. Paint only the top of the cake in your image editor. Then save the mask:

```text
photo:  cake.png   2048x1365  PNG
mask:   mask.png   2048x1365  PNG, with an alpha channel
```

Same format, same size, alpha channel. Send `cake.png` as the first input image, because the mask goes with the first input. Then, as in OpenAI's mask example, describe the finished picture, not only the change: "A chocolate birthday cake topped with fresh blueberries, on a slate board, warm café light."

### Step 4: the hard case, text next to the edit

The icing words sit just below the berries. Even with a good prompt or mask, an edit can touch the area next to it. The mask is only guidance, so the edit can spill over its edge.

Check the result letter by letter: "Happy 30th, Mia". If one letter changed, don't regenerate everything. Use the approved original for that area: copy the side of the cake back from it in your design tool.

**Why this is the hard case:** you asked for berries, so you look at the berries. The damage is in the part you did not ask about.

### Step 5: more changes, one per turn

The owner adds: "Also make the board white marble, and add one gold candle." Do them as two turns:

```text
Turn 2: Change only the slate board to white marble. Do not change any
        other elements of the image.
(check)
Turn 3: Add one thin gold candle in the centre of the cake, unlit.
        Ensure the change matches the warm light of the photo.
(check)
```

If you ask for both at once and the icing changes, you can't tell which request caused it.

### Step 6: compare before and after

Put the approved photo and the result side by side. Check:

- **Text:** every letter of the icing.
- **Light and shadow:** does the new part match?
- **Edges:** a halo or soft seam around the edit?
- **Everything else:** cake shape, board, background.

### Why it is better

- **An edit, not a new image.** The approved parts stay approved.
- **The prompt names what stays**, not only what changes.
- **The mask is correct**, and the photo is the first input.
- **One change per turn**, with a check after each.

## Common mistakes

1. **Regenerating instead of editing.** A new prompt changes the parts the client already liked. Fix: send the image and edit one element.
2. **A bad mask file.** A different size, a different format, or no alpha channel. Fix: export the mask from the same image, same size and format, with an alpha channel.
3. **Checking only the edited area.** Text, faces and edges next to it can change. Fix: compare the whole image with the original.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A travel blogger has a beach photo she loves, but a red rubbish bin stands on the sand on the left. She also wants the sky a bit warmer. Write the edit plan: how many turns, and the prompt for turn 1.

### Answer

Two turns: remove the bin first, check, then warm the sky.

Turn 1, for example:

```text
Using the provided image of the beach, please remove the red bin from the
sand on the left. Ensure the sand there matches the texture and light of
the sand around it. Do not change any other elements of the image.
```

Then compare with the original, and only then ask for the warmer sky.

## Recap

- Edit, don't regenerate, when most of the image is approved. Name one element and say what stays the same.
- A mask must match the image in size and format, have an alpha channel, and the image must be the first input. A mask is guidance, not a pixel-exact cut.
- One change per turn. After each edit, compare the whole image with the original, especially text and edges.
