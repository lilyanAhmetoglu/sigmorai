## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Write a video shot prompt that covers the seven elements in the Veo docs.
- Add sound to the prompt: speech, sound effects and background noise.
- Split a busy idea into a shot list, and check the length rules of your video model.

## The problem

Rosa runs a flower shop. For Mother's Day she wants a vertical video for social media: her hands wrapping a bouquet of pink peonies, a ribbon, the bouquet handed over, and Rosa saying the shop's line, "Flowers by noon."

Her first prompt: "Lovely flower shop video, Rosa wraps peonies, ties a ribbon, gives them to a customer and says our slogan, cinematic." The clip that comes back jumps between moments. The peonies turn into roses halfway. Rosa says something, but not the slogan. The sound is random.

An image prompt describes one moment. A video prompt also describes **time**: what moves, how the camera moves and what we hear. Rosa's prompt crammed four moments into one request, with no camera, light or sound for any of them.

## The idea

Write a video prompt like one line of a film crew's shot list: one shot, one action, one camera setup.

The [Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) list the elements a prompt should include:

```text
Subject      who or what is in the shot
Action       what the subject is doing
Style        creative direction: film noir, cartoon
Camera       position and motion: eye-level, dolly shot   (optional)
Composition  wide shot, close-up                         (optional)
Focus/lens   shallow focus, macro lens                   (optional)
Ambiance     how colour and light set the mood           (optional)
```

**Sound.** Veo 3.1 makes "natively generated audio". The Veo docs give three kinds of cues. **Dialogue:** put the exact words in quotation marks. **Sound effects:** "Explicitly describe sounds." **Ambient noise:** "Describe the environment's soundscape."

**One idea per shot.** An [OpenAI Cookbook guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide) for Sora 2 says that when one prompt covers several shots, each shot block should stay distinct, with "one lighting recipe at a time" and just one camera setup and one action. The guide is now archived ("may reference outdated models or APIs"), so we use only its general prompting ideas. The [Runway docs](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/multi-shot-video) give the same tip: one clear idea for each shot. They also say to reuse the same product words in every shot "for visual consistency".

**Detail versus freedom.** The OpenAI guide says "detailed prompts give you control and consistency, while lighter prompts open space for creative outcomes". It also says the same prompt run again "will lead to different results". So write the details you need, and plan to run a shot more than once.

**Start from a picture.** If you give Veo an image, the shot starts on it: the docs say to pick the image "closest to what you envision as the first scene". Veo 3.1 also takes up to three "asset images" of one person, character or product to keep how it looks, or a first and a last frame. Runway's multi-shot recipe has an optional `firstFrame` image too.

**Length rules.** Read them for your model. On Veo 3.1, `durationSeconds` is "4", "6" or "8", and it "Must be "8" when using extension, reference images or with 1080p and 4k resolutions". Runway's multi-shot recipe takes a total of 5, 10 or 15 seconds, and in custom mode a list of 3–5 shots.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the weak prompt

```text
Lovely flower shop video, Rosa wraps peonies, ties a ribbon, gives them
to a customer and says our slogan, cinematic.
```

What goes wrong:

- Four actions in one request, so the model cuts between them at random.
- No camera, composition or light for any moment.
- "Our slogan" is not the words. The model can't guess them.
- No sound cues.

### Step 2: split it into a shot list

| Shot | Action                            | Camera                    | Sound                       |
| ---- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 1    | Hands wrap peonies in kraft paper | Top-down close-up, static | Paper rustles, quiet shop   |
| 2    | Hands tie a pink silk ribbon      | Macro lens, slow push-in  | Ribbon slides, soft ticking |
| 3    | Rosa holds the bouquet, speaks    | Eye-level medium shot     | Her line, doorbell far away |

Each row has one action and one camera setup. This table format is ours.

### Step 3: write shot 1 in full

```text
Top-down close-up of a florist's hands wrapping a bouquet of soft pink
peonies in brown kraft paper on a pale wooden workbench. The hands fold
the paper slowly around the stems. Natural, realistic style. Static
camera, shallow focus on the petals. Warm morning light from a window
on the left. Paper rustles; a quiet flower shop in the background.
Vertical 9:16.
```

Every element from the Veo list has a line, and so does the sound.

### Step 4: keep the bouquet the same in every shot

Copy one fixed bouquet description into all three prompts: "a bouquet of soft pink peonies in brown kraft paper". This follows Runway's tip to reuse the same product words. On Veo 3.1 you can also give up to three photos of the real bouquet as reference images.

### Step 5: the hard case, the speaking shot

Shot 3 has Rosa's line, and Rosa wants it short: 4 seconds, in 1080p, with her bouquet photos as references.

Check the length rule first. On Veo 3.1, 1080p and reference images both need "8". So you choose: an 8-second shot with references, or a shorter shot in 720p with no references. Rosa takes 8 seconds. Then write the line in quotes:

```text
Eye-level medium shot of a smiling florist in a green apron holding a
bouquet of soft pink peonies in brown kraft paper, in a bright flower
shop. She looks at the camera and says, "Flowers by noon." A doorbell
chimes far away. Warm natural light. Vertical 9:16.
```

**Why this is the hard case:** the prompt can be perfect and the request can still fail, because the settings break a rule. Check the model's limits before you write.

### Step 6: render, compare, pick

Run each shot two or three times. Pick the take where the bouquet matches the other shots. Cut the three shots together in your editor.

### Why it is better

- **One action and one camera setup per shot**, so the model has one job.
- **Every Veo element and the sound are in the prompt**, not left to chance.
- **The bouquet has one fixed description**, plus references.
- **The settings fit the model's rules** before you spend a render.

## Common mistakes

1. **Many moments in one prompt.** The model cuts between them, or merges them. Fix: one action and one camera setup per shot, then edit the shots together.
2. **Sound left out, or the words left vague.** "She says something nice" gets random words. Fix: put the exact words in quotes, and describe effects and background noise.
3. **A new product description in every shot.** "Peonies", then "pink flowers", then "a bouquet" give three bouquets. Fix: copy one fixed description into each shot, and add reference images if your model takes them.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A bike shop wants a short clip with this prompt:

```text
A mechanic fixes a bike wheel, then rides it down a hill, then the shop
sign appears, with a cool sound. Make it 4 seconds and 4k.
```

1. How many shots are hidden in this prompt?
2. Name one problem with "4 seconds and 4k" on Veo 3.1.
3. Write a better prompt for the first shot.

### Answer

1. Three: fixing the wheel, riding down the hill, the sign.
2. On Veo 3.1, 4k clips must be 8 seconds. Choose 8 seconds, or a lower resolution.
3. For example:

```text
Close-up of a mechanic's hands tightening the spokes of a bicycle wheel
on a repair stand, in a small workshop. Realistic style. Slow dolly-in,
shallow focus on the spokes. Warm light from a work lamp. Metal clicks
softly; a radio plays quietly in the background.
```

## Recap

- One shot, one action, one camera setup. Cover the seven elements from the Veo docs.
- Prompt the sound: exact speech in quotes, sound effects and background noise in plain words.
- Plan a shot list, reuse one product description, and check your model's length and resolution rules before you render.
