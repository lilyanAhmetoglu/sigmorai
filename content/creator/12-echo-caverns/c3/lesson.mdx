## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Choose between a designed voice (a new voice from words) and a cloned voice (a copy of a real person).
- Write a short voice design description, and plan a clean recording for a clone.
- Name each vendor's consent and policy rules before you clone anyone.

## The problem

A cooking school is building an app with 60 short spoken tips. The team wants two voices. One is "Chef Pip", a cartoon helper that is nobody in real life. The other is the founder, Ana, who wants the welcome lines in Ana's own voice, without a studio day for each update.

These are two different jobs with very different rules. This lesson shows both.

The rules and limits below are from each vendor's page in October 2026.

## The idea

### Two ways to make a new voice

- **Design:** you describe a voice in words, and the model invents it. It belongs to no real person.
- **Clone:** the model copies a real person's voice from recordings. Google calls this **voice replication**, OpenAI calls it **custom voices**, and ElevenLabs calls it **voice cloning**.

**Timbre** is the sound of a voice itself: deep, bright, breathy (our plain gloss).

### Design a voice from words

The [Gemini voice design docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/voice-design) say you can make "a brand-new, persistent vocal persona" from a description. You get back a reusable `voice_...` ID and a short preview clip. Their advice for the description:

- Describe "a character's age, vocal timbre, accent, and baseline delivery".
- Keep it to "A clear 1–2 sentence description".

Put lasting traits in the design. For the mood of one line, the [Gemini speech docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation) use a short `style` field on each turn.

ElevenLabs Voice Design gives three voice options per try. The [ElevenLabs Voice Design docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-design) say: "we create three voice options based on your prompt". They list three pitfalls:

- Don't write "accent" when you mean intonation. It "can trigger unwanted dialect shifts".
- "Avoid FX words" such as "reverb" or "echo". The docs say they "can negatively affect output quality".
- "always specify the language and regional variant in the first sentence".

The **preview text** is the line the new voice reads for you. It "should complement the voice description, not contradict it". A calm voice with an angry preview line leads to "unnatural or inconsistent results".

### Clone a real voice: consent first

A **consent recording** is audio of the speaker agreeing, out loud, to the clone (our plain gloss). Each vendor asks for proof, in a different way.

**Google.** The [Gemini replication docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/voice-replication) say each request "requires two real human audio recordings from the same adult speaker":

- the reference audio: "A 10–30 second clip of clean, natural speech"
- the consent audio: the same speaker "clearly reciting the mandatory consent statement" in a supported language

In English the statement is: "I am the owner of this voice and I consent to Google using this voice to create a synthetic voice model." The docs also say to record both "on the same microphone in the same acoustic setting so the speaker verification check succeeds reliably."

**OpenAI.** The [OpenAI custom voices docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-voices) say custom voices "are limited to eligible customers". Their rules for the recordings:

- Consent: "The consent audio recording must only include one of the following phrases. Any divergence from the script will lead to a failure."
- Sample: it "must match the consent recording".
- "At most 20 voices can be created per organization."
- "The audio samples must be 30 seconds or less."

**ElevenLabs.** The [cloning concepts page](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/concepts/voice-cloning) says: "Both IVC and PVC include a voice verification step." IVC is Instant Voice Cloning; PVC is Professional Voice Cloning. The check uses "voice-captcha technology": a live test that you are the person giving the samples (our plain gloss).

- For an instant clone, the [IVC docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-cloning/instant-voice-cloning) say you also "confirm that you have the right and consent to clone the voice".
- A professional clone is stricter. The [PVC docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-cloning/professional-voice-cloning) say: "You can only create a Professional Voice Clone of your own voice. Even with their consent, you cannot clone someone else's voice."
- The way to share one: the voice owner can "create and verify a Professional Voice Clone on their own account, then share it with you privately using a sharing link".
- The concepts page says PVC fits when "you are on a Creator plan or above".

### A passed check is not permission

The ElevenLabs concepts page explains the limit of the check. It "cannot guarantee that the provided recording truly belongs to the requester, only that the requester was present and actively participating." Its terms "place the responsibility for authorised use with the creator."

### The policy rules

The [ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy) (last updated 09 October 2026) has voice rules:

- No "Cloning, synthesizing, or impersonating the voice, identity, or likeness of any living individual without their explicit and informed consent or other valid authorization".
- No "Circumventing our security, usage limits, or safety guardrails, including any voice verification systems such as Voice CAPTCHA".

Its elections section adds two more lines:

- No "Impersonating political candidates or elected officials without authorization".
- No "Political campaigning without our prior written consent". So the consent must come from ElevenLabs itself, not only from the candidate.

The [OpenAI speech guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text-to-speech) also requires "a clear disclosure to end users that the TTS voice they are hearing is AI-generated and not a human voice."

### A clone copies everything

- The OpenAI docs: "The model copies exactly what you give it—tone, cadence, energy, pauses, habits—so record the exact voice you want." Record "in a quiet space with minimal echo".
- The ElevenLabs PVC docs: a clone will copy "any artifacts and unwanted audio present in the samples", such as noise, echo or music.
- The Gemini docs: "Minimize room echo, background noise, music, and overlapping voices."

### How long a voice lasts

On Gemini, stored voices (designed or replicated) share a limit of "200 voices per project". The voice design docs give them "a 1-year TTL (time-to-live)": they keep for 1 year. A **stateless** key (`voicekey_...`) is one Google does not store (our plain gloss). The table in the replication docs lists it as "Client-managed", with a life of 7 days.

## Worked example

The cooking school: Chef Pip (designed) and Ana (cloned).

### Step 1: design or clone?

```text
Chef Pip   nobody's real voice      -> design
Ana        a real person's voice    -> clone, with Ana's own consent
```

### Step 2: Chef Pip's design (Gemini)

The first draft (before):

```text
A really fun chef voice who is excited when talking about cakes but sad
when a recipe fails and angry about burnt toast, a bit like a cartoon,
with lots of energy, sometimes whispering secrets about spices...
```

This mixes lasting traits with moods for single lines. The voice would have no stable base.

The rewrite (after), 2 sentences of lasting traits only:

```text
A bright, bouncy cartoon chef in their 20s with a light, friendly tone.
Quick, playful pacing and a warm smile in every line.
```

Moods like "sad" or "whispering" now stay out of the design. You set them line by line.

### Step 3: the hard case: Ana's clone (OpenAI)

**This is the hard case.** Ana has an old 25-second clip from a cooking video. You can hear a fan, pans and soft music. Ana asks: "Can we just use this?"

No. The OpenAI docs say the model "copies exactly what you give it", so the fan, the pans and the music come with it.

The plan (after):

```text
Sample   25 seconds, Ana reads app-style tips
Room     quiet, soft furniture, fan off, no music
Voice    the same calm, friendly energy Ana wants in the app
Mic      one microphone, same distance for the whole take
Record   keep Ana's consent file, the date and the agreed use (our advice)
```

### Step 4: why it is better

- **Each voice uses the right method**: no real person behind Pip, Ana's own consent behind Ana.
- **Pip's design holds only lasting traits**, so mood can change line by line.
- **Ana's sample is short, clean and in the target energy**, because the clone copies what it hears.

## Common mistakes

1. **Putting every mood into the design.** The voice gets no stable base. Fix: 1–2 sentences of lasting traits; moods go on single lines.
2. **Cloning from an old noisy video.** Noise, echo and music get copied too. Fix: record a fresh, clean sample in a quiet room.
3. **Losing track of stored voices.** Gemini stored voices keep for 1 year, and stateless keys for 7 days. Fix: keep a list of voice IDs, owners and dates.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A children's science museum wants three voices for its app:

1. "Professor Bolt", a made-up robot guide.
2. The museum director, Sol, reading the yearly welcome in Sol's own voice.
3. "Spark", a made-up talking spark of electricity.

For each one, say design or clone. Then write a 1–2 sentence design for Professor Bolt with lasting traits only. Last, give Sol three tips for the clone sample.

### Answer

1. **Professor Bolt: design.** It is nobody's real voice. For example:

   ```text
   A calm, kind robot guide with a slightly metallic, mid-pitch voice.
   Clear, steady pacing, like a patient teacher.
   ```

2. **Sol: clone**, with Sol's own consent. Tips for the sample: a quiet room with little echo; no music or other voices; the same energy Sol wants in the app.
3. **Spark: design.** Also nobody's real voice.

Your words can differ. Check that the design has no moods for single lines.

## Recap

- Design invents a voice from 1–2 clear sentences. Clone copies a real person and needs that person's recorded consent.
- Each vendor checks consent its own way, and a passed check is still not permission. You stay responsible.
- Record clone samples clean and short, follow each vendor's policy, and tell listeners the voice is AI.
