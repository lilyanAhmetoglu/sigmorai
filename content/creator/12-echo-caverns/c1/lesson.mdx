## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Put voice direction in the place each vendor gives for it, so the model does not read it aloud.
- Control pace and pauses with the settings, tags and punctuation the docs describe.
- Write an audio tag that clearly describes a voice, so the model does not turn it into a sound.

## The problem

A planetarium plays one short voice line before each show: "Welcome. The lights will go down in a moment. Please stay in your seat." The team made it with a speech model. The take is fast and bright, like a radio advert. They want it slow, quiet and calm, with a clear pause before "Please".

The words are right. The delivery is wrong. This lesson shows where to put delivery notes, and how to set pace and pauses.

The model names below are from each vendor's page in October 2026. They change often.

## The idea

### Text, direction and voice

A **TTS** model (text to speech) turns text into a voice track. Three inputs shape the result (our split):

- **Text:** the words to say.
- **Direction:** how to say them: tone, pace, emotion.
- **Voice:** who says them. **Timbre** is the sound of the voice itself: deep, bright, breathy (our plain gloss).

The key fact: the model speaks the text. So direction must not sit inside the words.

- The [ElevenLabs docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech) say: "Descriptive text will be spoken out by the model and must be manually trimmed or removed from the audio if desired." Their example of descriptive text is "she said excitedly".
- The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation) say: "Gemini 3.8 TTS treats the `text` field strictly as a verbatim transcript." **Verbatim** means word for word.

So each vendor gives direction its own place. There are two kinds (our grouping): a **field** next to the text for a whole line, and an **inline tag** inside the text for one moment.

| Vendor (October 2026)         | Whole line or turn                    | One moment                          |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts`      | the `instructions` field              | none on the page                    |
| Google Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS   | `style` in `speech_metadata`          | angle-bracket tags: `<sigh>`        |
| ElevenLabs Eleven v4 (and v3) | an audio tag at the start: `[warmly]` | an audio tag in the line: `[sighs]` |

### OpenAI: the instructions field

The [OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text-to-speech) says you can prompt `gpt-4o-mini-tts` to control things like accent, intonation, tone, speed of speech and whispering. Its example is "Speak in a cheerful and positive tone."

The [API reference](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/audio/subresources/speech/methods/create) adds one limit for `instructions`: "Does not work with `tts-1` or `tts-1-hd`." Those are OpenAI's other two speech models.

### Gemini: style and angle brackets

A **turn** is one block of text for one speaker (our plain gloss). In Gemini, delivery for a whole turn goes in `speech_metadata.style`, for example `"warm and enthusiastic"`. The voice name picks who speaks.

Short events go inside the text, in angle brackets: `<sigh>`, `<cough>`, `<short pause>`. The Gemini docs say to "stick to human vocalizations rather than non-vocal sound effects". If the script is not in English, they say to "continue to use English inline tags for best results."

### ElevenLabs: audio tags

Eleven v4 and v3 use **audio tags**: short notes in square brackets, like `[whispers]` or `[sighs]`. A tag can set a whole delivery. The [ElevenLabs best practices](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices) show `[Low, steady voice, restrained urgency]`.

One trap: Eleven v4 can also make sound effects, so "a tag can occasionally be interpreted as a request for a sound effect rather than a delivery instruction (or vice versa)." The docs suggest tags "that clearly describe the voice quality you want", such as `[low, gravelly voice]`.

The tag must also suit the voice: "A meditative voice shouldn't shout; a hyped voice won't whisper convincingly."

### Pace and pauses

- **OpenAI:** the `speed` setting. The reference says: "Select a value from `0.25` to `4.0`. `1.0` is the default."
- **Gemini:** `"speaking slowly"` or `"speaking rapidly"` in `style` sets pace for a turn. `<short pause>` and `<long pause>` go at exact points. Commas, dashes and ellipses ("...") add "natural conversational hesitation".
- **ElevenLabs:** a speed setting with a default of 1.0, "to a minimum of 0.7" and "to a maximum of 1.2". The docs warn: "Extreme values may affect the quality of the generated speech."

ElevenLabs adds that Eleven v4 and v3 "do not support SSML break tags". **SSML** is an older tag language for speech (our plain gloss). For pauses there, use tags, ellipses and text structure. The docs say "Ellipses (...) add pauses and weight".

### Stress on one word

Both Google and ElevenLabs use capital letters. The Gemini docs say: "Capitalize specific words in the transcript" to put stress on them. The ElevenLabs docs say: "Capitalization increases emphasis." Both show nearly the same line: "It was a VERY long day".

### Less direction, more voice

The Gemini docs warn that long direction can hurt the voice:

- Long "Director's Notes" blocks "are the most common cause of voice drift". **Drift** means the voice slowly stops sounding like itself (our plain gloss).
- Don't ask it to "maintain identical timbre". The docs say "extra prompt text increases drift."
- Don't put "age, gender, names, or permanent accent changes" in `style`. Pick another voice, or design one.
- Test with an empty `style` first: "most requests need no `style` instruction at all."

So pick the voice before you direct it:

- **OpenAI** lists 13 built-in voices. "For best quality, we recommend using `marin` or `cedar`." Its "Voices are currently optimized for English."
- **Gemini** prebuilt voices each have a one-word label: Kore is "Firm", Achernar is "Soft", Sulafat is "Warm".
- **ElevenLabs** has a Voice Library. Check dates: the [Voices page](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/voices) says its Default voices "will expire on December 31, 2026".

### Tell people it is AI

The OpenAI guide says its usage policies require "a clear disclosure to end users that the TTS voice they are hearing is AI-generated and not a human voice." Plan that label with the script.

## Worked example

The planetarium line again. Our goal: slow, quiet and calm, with a pause before "Please".

### Step 1: the first take (before)

```text
model:  gpt-4o-mini-tts
voice:  coral
speed:  1.0
input:  Welcome. The lights will go down in a moment. Please stay in your seat.
```

There is no direction, so the model picks its own delivery.

### Step 2: voice, direction and speed (OpenAI)

```text
model:         gpt-4o-mini-tts
voice:         marin
speed:         0.9
instructions:  Calm, quiet and slow, like a guide in a dark room.
               Soft tone, close to a whisper.
input:         Welcome. The lights will go down in a moment. Please stay in your seat.
```

- `marin` is one of the two voices the OpenAI guide recommends for quality.
- `0.9` is our pick: a little slower, well inside the range.
- The instructions name tone, pace and whispering, all things the guide says you can prompt.

### Step 3: the same line on ElevenLabs Eleven v4

```text
[soft, slow, hushed voice] Welcome. The lights will go down in a moment... Please stay in your seat.
```

The tag describes only the voice. The ellipsis gives the pause before "Please".

### Step 4: the hard case

**This is the hard case.** A teammate wants "a space feeling" and changes the tag:

```text
[echo in a huge dome] Welcome. The lights will go down in a moment... Please stay in your seat.
```

This tag describes a room and a sound, not a voice. Eleven v4 can make sound effects, so this tag can be read as a sound request. Write a tag that only describes the voice:

```text
[low, slow, hushed voice] Welcome. The lights will go down in a moment... Please stay in your seat.
```

If the team still wants a room sound, our advice: add it in the audio edit.

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Each direction sits where the vendor reads it**: the OpenAI field, or a voice tag.
- **Every tag describes a voice and the pause is marked**, so there are fewer surprises. Listen to check.

## Common mistakes

1. **A tag that sounds like a sound cue.** Words like "thunder" or "dome echo" describe sounds, not a voice. Fix: describe pitch, pace and loudness, like `[low, gravelly voice]`.
2. **A tag that fights the voice.** A calm, meditative voice will not shout well. Fix: choose tags that suit the voice, or choose a different voice.
3. **Fixing timing with a big speed jump.** The ElevenLabs docs say extreme values can hurt quality. Fix: cut some words first, then change speed a little and listen.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A museum audio tour has one Gemini 3.8 line and one ElevenLabs line.

1. Gemini text: `Look up. That is the oldest star map in the city.` The director wants a small breath before "Look up", and a long pause after it. Write the new text.
2. ElevenLabs Eleven v4: `[howling wind voice] Look up. That is the oldest star map in the city.` What could go wrong? Write a better tag.
3. OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts`: the take is at speed 1.0. The director wants it about 20% slower. Which value do you set?

### Answer

1. Put human-sound and pause tags inline, at the exact points:

   ```text
   <breath> Look up. <long pause> That is the oldest star map in the city.
   ```

2. "Howling wind" can be read as a sound effect, so you may get wind instead of a voice. Describe the voice only, for example `[slow, quiet, amazed voice]`.
3. `0.8` (our maths: 1.0 minus 20%). It is inside OpenAI's range of 0.25 to 4.0. Listen after the change.

Your words can differ. Check that each tag describes a voice or a human sound.

## Recap

- The model speaks the text. Put direction in the vendor's place: OpenAI `instructions`, Gemini `style`, or ElevenLabs audio tags.
- Set pace with speed settings, pause tags and punctuation. Keep speed changes small.
- Keep direction short, pick the right voice first, write tags that describe a voice, and tell listeners the voice is AI.
