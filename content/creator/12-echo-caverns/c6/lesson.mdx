## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Choose between a one-step dubbing tool and a chain of steps you control.
- Know what a dubbing tool keeps, what it changes, and how you can edit it.
- Fit a translated line into the time slot of the original, and keep names right.

## The problem

A bike shop makes a 2-minute repair video in English. The host shows how to fit the shop's own brake pad, the "GripLock". The shop now wants a German version.

The first German try sounds smooth. But two lines run past the end of their shot, and in one line the product name comes out as a German word.

A **dub** replaces the spoken voice with a new language. To **localise** means to adapt it for one place: the language, the region and the names people know (our plain glosses).

The models and limits below are from each vendor's page in October 2026. ElevenLabs Dubbing v2 is in alpha (an early test version), so expect changes.

## The idea

### Two ways to dub

- **One step:** a dubbing tool takes the video and gives back the new language.
- **A chain:** transcript, then translation, then a new voice track. You see and fix the text at every step.

### One step: ElevenLabs dubbing

The [ElevenLabs dubbing docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/dubbing) say dubbing works "across 90+ languages while preserving the emotion, timing, tone and unique characteristics of each speaker." Also: "The original background audio is retained".

**Language tags.** The API takes a **BCP-47** tag, a short standard language code (our plain gloss), "for example `fr` or `es-MX`". On Dubbing v2, a tag with a region "must be one of the supported dialects listed below; all other languages use the base language tag." The list shows:

- Spanish: `es-AR`, `es-CL`, `es-ES`, `es-MX`
- Portuguese: `pt-BR` (Brazil), `pt-PT` (Portugal)

German has no dialects on the list, so it is plain `de`.

**Cloning strength.** Dubbing keeps each speaker's own voice. A setting called **Speaker similarity** sets how close, "on a scale of 0 to 10". "The default value of 7 works well for most content." Higher values "can sound less natural across languages with very different phonetic characteristics", and "can also carry over more of the original accent". **Phonetic** means about the sounds of a language (our plain gloss). Lower values give "more freedom for natural delivery in the target language at the cost of resemblance to the original voice."

**Editing.** In the app, "Dubbing v2 is automatic and has no in-app editor." "For Dubbing v2, transcript editing and audio regeneration via the API are available on Enterprise plans only." The older v1 model has **Dubbing Studio**, with "transcript editing, speaker reassignment, and per-clip regeneration". But "Dubbing Studio is in maintenance mode and receives critical bug fixes only". And the [product guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/products/dubbing) says: "It's not possible to convert an existing automatic dub to a Dubbing project."

**Output.** "Dubbed video is a high-quality preview, not a production master. It is most commonly output at 1080p and may not match the source resolution." The audio:

- "Audio is mono for Dubbing v1 and at most stereo for Dubbing v2, so 5.1 and other multichannel audio is not preserved." **5.1** is surround sound with six channels (our plain gloss).
- The advice: "download the dubbed audio and use it to replace the audio track on your original video."

**Lip-sync** means moving the mouth to match the new words (our plain gloss). The guide says: "At the moment, ElevenLabs does not offer lip syncing as part of Dubbing."

**Watermark.** "Free-tier dubs are watermarked automatically; paid-tier dubs are not."

### The chain: transcript, translation, voice

**1. Transcript.** A speech-to-text model writes down the English.

**2. Translation.** Read the limits. OpenAI's `/v1/audio/translations` endpoint "supports translation into English only", says the [OpenAI speech to text guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/speech-to-text). So it can't turn English into German. For that step, use a translator or a text model, then check the result (our note).

**3. New voice.** Send the translated text to a speech model:

- **OpenAI:** you can make speech in other languages "by providing input text in the language of your choice". But its "Voices are currently optimized for English", says the [OpenAI speech guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text-to-speech).
- **Google:** in the [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation), "The TTS models detect the input language automatically." Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS "supports over 130 languages". Their tip: "select a regional voice from the Extended Voice Library that matches your target language and persona."

The OpenAI guide also requires "a clear disclosure to end users that the TTS voice they are hearing is AI-generated and not a human voice."

### Timing and names

Each line has a slot in the video: the time from when it starts until the next line or shot. A translated line can be longer than the English one (our note). The dubbing tool tries to keep timing. In the chain, you check it yourself.

Product names are easy to lose. A name can come back translated, or said in a strange way (our note). Our advice: write a short list of names that never change, before you translate.

## Worked example

The bike shop video, using the chain into German, with a Gemini voice.

### Step 1: the "do not translate" list

Before translation, the team writes:

```text
Keep as is in every language:
  GripLock      (product name)
  Rider Hub     (shop name)
Say as numbers:
  4 mm, 2 newton metres
```

The translator gets this list with the script.

### Step 2: translate, then measure

One English line and its first German translation (our example):

```text
EN  "Now slide the GripLock pad into the slot until it clicks."
    slot in the video: 3.2 seconds
DE  "Schieben Sie jetzt das GripLock-Pad in die Halterung, bis es hörbar einrastet."
    first voice take: 4.6 seconds
```

The German take is 1.4 seconds too long. That is about 44% over the slot (our maths: 1.4 ÷ 3.2 ≈ 0.44).

### Step 3: the hard case

**This is the hard case.** The team's first idea is to make the voice faster. In our view, a 44% speed-up would sound rushed. Our advice: shorten the text first, and keep the meaning.

```text
DE before  "Schieben Sie jetzt das GripLock-Pad in die Halterung, bis es hörbar einrastet."
DE after   "Jetzt das GripLock-Pad einschieben, bis es klickt."
           new take: 3.0 seconds
```

The new line drops "hörbar" (audibly) and the long verb phrase. The meaning stays: push the pad in until it clicks. "GripLock" stays as it is, as the list says.

### Step 4: voice and checks

- **Voice:** a German regional voice from Gemini's Extended Voice Library, as the docs suggest.
- **Disclosure:** a line in the description says the German voice is AI-generated.
- **Native-speaker check** (our advice): a German speaker watches the whole video and checks meaning, names, timing and the sound mix.

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Every line fits its slot**, so the voice never runs into the next shot.
- **Names are fixed before translation**, so nobody has to find them later.
- **The text is short and natural**, not a fast, squeezed voice.

## Common mistakes

1. **Speeding up the voice to fit.** A rushed voice sounds wrong. Fix: shorten the translated text first, then change speed only a little.
2. **Translating product names.** "GripLock" can come back as a new word. Fix: give the translator a "keep as is" list before translation.
3. **No human check.** A smooth dub can still say the wrong thing. Fix: ask a native speaker to watch the whole video.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A yoga studio dubs a short video into French with the chain. One line:

```text
EN  "Breathe in slowly and lift your arms above your head."
    slot: 3.0 seconds
FR  "Inspirez lentement et levez doucement les deux bras au-dessus de votre tête."
    first take: 4.2 seconds
```

The studio's class name, "FlowNest", appears in the next line.

1. How much too long is the French take, in seconds and in percent?
2. What do you change first: the speed or the text? Why?
3. What goes on the "keep as is" list?

### Answer

1. 4.2 − 3.0 = **1.2 seconds**. 1.2 ÷ 3.0 = **40%** over (our maths).
2. **The text first.** A 40% faster voice would sound rushed. A shorter line, like "Inspirez et levez les bras", keeps the meaning and fits better. Then measure again.
3. **"FlowNest"**, the class name, so it is not translated.

Your French can differ. Check that the meaning stays, the line fits, and the name is on the list.

## Recap

- One-step dubbing is fast; the chain gives you control of the text at every step. Know the edit path and the output limits before you start.
- Dubbing keeps the background audio and the speaker's voice, with a cloning strength from 0 to 10 (default 7). Use exact language tags from the vendor's list.
- Fit each translated line into its slot by shortening the text, fix names before translation, disclose the AI voice, and get a native speaker to check.
