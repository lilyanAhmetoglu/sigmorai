## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Pick a music model by the length you need, and write a music prompt with genre, mood, instruments, tempo and era.
- Plan when things happen in a track, in seconds and in bars.
- Write sound effect prompts, and know the length and loop settings.

## The problem

A two-person team is making a small cosy game about a lantern shop. They need a calm menu loop, a 30-second title theme that grows when the logo appears, and a few short sound effects. Their first music prompt was "chill music for my game". The result was fine, but it sounded like every other chill track, and the change came at a random point.

This lesson shows how to ask for music and sound effects that fit the moment.

The models and limits below are from each vendor's page in October 2026.

## The idea

### Pick the model by length

| Vendor and model (October 2026)             | Length                                            |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Google Lyria 3 Clip, `lyria-3-clip-preview` | always 30 seconds                                 |
| Google Lyria 3.5, `lyria-3.5`               | "A couple of minutes (controllable using prompt)" |
| ElevenLabs Eleven Music                     | 3 seconds to 5 minutes                            |

The [Gemini music docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/music-generation) say: "The Lyria 3 Clip model always generates a **30-second** clip." For Lyria 3.5, you set the length in the prompt: their example is "create a 2-minute song". Their tip: "Iterate with Clip first", then make the full version with `lyria-3.5`.

The [Eleven Music docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/music) say: "Generated music has a minimum duration of 3 seconds and a maximum duration of 5 minutes." You can also give a length like "60 seconds".

### Five things every music prompt decides

The [ElevenLabs best practices](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/music/best-practices) say a prompt answers five questions "whether you intend it to or not: genre, mood, instrumentation, tempo, and production era." For any open question, the model picks "the most statistically likely choice — which is to say, the most average one."

Google's [Lyria prompt guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/lyria-prompt-guide) agrees: "Lead your prompt with the primary genre." It lists musical settings:

- **Tempo (BPM):** BPM means beats per minute, the speed of the music (our plain gloss). Examples: `120 BPM`, `slow tempo around 72 BPM`.
- **Key:** the set of notes a piece uses (our plain gloss). Examples: `in G major`, `in D minor`.

The ElevenLabs docs say the model "accurately follows BPM and often captures the intended musical key."

### Vocals are on by default

- The Lyria guide: "Lyria 3.5 generates vocal tracks with lyrics by default." The Gemini docs show "Instrumental only, no vocals." in a prompt.
- ElevenLabs: "By default, most music prompts will include lyrics. To generate music without vocals, add "instrumental only" to your prompt."

### Your own lyrics

On Lyria, put lyrics "beneath a `Lyrics:` header" and tag the parts: `[Verse 1]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`. The Gemini docs say: "Separate lyrics from instructions." Our reading: then the model does not sing your directions.

Lyria writes lyrics in the language of your prompt. Its safety filters block "prompts that request specific artist voices or the generation of copyrighted lyrics."

### Shape the music over time

- **Google:** time ranges like `[0:00 - 0:10] Intro: ...`, or a cue like "The chorus kicks in at 22 seconds".
- **ElevenLabs:** "Narrate the arrangement in order, the way you'd brief a band". Their example: "start with just a shuffled drum loop, add a warm sub bassline after four bars".

The small words matter. The docs say: "Without the _just_, the model fills the silence".

A **bar** is a small group of beats (our plain gloss). In this lesson we count 4 beats per bar (our assumption). A loop prompt names what is not allowed, as in "no melody — just drums".

### Fixing a take

- **Google:** "Music generation is a single-turn process." You can't refine a clip with more prompts. Change the prompt and generate again. "Results may vary between calls, even with the same prompt."
- **ElevenLabs:** in the app you can "select a specific section of a generated song and regenerate just that section". This is called **inpainting**.

The Gemini music docs say: "All generated audio includes a SynthID audio watermark for identification." You cannot hear it.

### Sound effects

The [ElevenLabs sound effects docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/sound-effects) take a text description and some settings:

- **Duration:** "Range: 0.1 to 30 seconds". By default, the length is set from the prompt.
- **Looping:** makes sound "that can be played on repeat without perceptible start/end points". Their example: "Generate 30s of 'soft rain' then loop it endlessly".
- **Prompt influence:** "High: More literal interpretation of the prompt". "Low: More creative interpretation with added variations".

The key facts list a maximum of "30 seconds per generation".

Prompt tips from the same page:

- A simple effect: a short, clear line, like "Heavy wooden door creaking open".
- A sequence: say the order, like "Footsteps on gravel, then a metallic door opens".

Three words from their list:

- A **one-shot** is a "Single, non-repeating sound".
- A **loop** is a "Repeating audio segment".
- A **stem** is an "Isolated audio component", like the drums alone.

### Sound inside a video

The [Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) say Veo 3.1 makes "8-second videos" with "natively generated audio". You can ask for dialogue, sound effects and ambient noise in the video prompt. Our reading: that sound is part of the clip. A separate file from a music or effects model can be moved, looped or reused in the edit.

## Worked example

The lantern shop game.

### Step 1: the menu loop (before → after)

Before:

```text
chill music for my game
```

It names no genre, instruments, tempo or era.

After, on Eleven Music with a short length:

```text
Cosy lo-fi, 80 BPM, in F major. Warm and sleepy mood.
Just soft Rhodes piano chords and gentle brushed drums, four bars,
dusty 1990s sampler sound.
```

Genre, mood, instruments, tempo and era each have an answer. "Just" stops the model filling the space with extra parts (the ElevenLabs tip).

### Step 2: the hard case: the title theme

**This is the hard case.** The logo appears at 0:12 in the 30-second title screen. The team wants the music to grow right there.

The ElevenLabs docs talk about bars, so we turn seconds into bars (our maths, with 4 beats per bar):

```text
80 BPM   -> one beat = 60 / 80 = 0.75 s
one bar  = 4 beats  = 3 s
0:12     = 12 / 3   = 4 bars
```

Now the prompt can brief it like a band:

```text
Cosy lo-fi, 80 BPM, in F major, gentle and hopeful, 1990s sampler sound.
Start with just soft Rhodes piano chords. After four bars, bring in
brushed drums and a warm bass, then a light glockenspiel melody.
```

Length: 30 seconds, set in the request.

Listen to the take. If the change lands a little early or late, a short trim in the edit is fine (our advice).

### Step 3: sound effects

One sequence and one simple effect:

```text
Old wooden shop door creaking open, then a small brass bell ringing
Match striking, then a lantern flame catching softly
```

Each line says the order of the sounds with "then".

### Step 4: why it is better

- **Each music prompt names genre, mood, instruments, tempo and era**, all chosen for this game.
- **The change is planned in bars**, so it lands near the logo.
- **Each effect says its order**, so the sounds come in the right sequence.

## Common mistakes

1. **Forgetting "just".** The model fills empty space with extra parts. Fix: say "start with just..." and name what is not allowed.
2. **Planning in seconds only.** A change at a random second can land in the middle of a bar. Fix: work out beats and bars from the BPM.
3. **Many sounds with no order.** "Door, bell, steps" gives no sequence. Fix: write "then" or "followed by" between the sounds.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A short cooking video wants a happy loop at 100 BPM, four bars long.

1. How long is four bars at 100 BPM, with 4 beats per bar?
2. Write the loop prompt. Use only ukulele and hand claps.
3. Write one sound effect prompt for an egg cracking into a hot pan, in order.

### Answer

1. One beat is 60 / 100 = 0.6 seconds. One bar is 4 × 0.6 = 2.4 seconds. Four bars are **9.6 seconds** (our maths).
2. For example:

   ```text
   Happy acoustic pop, 100 BPM, in C major, bright and playful,
   modern clean production. Just ukulele and hand claps, four bars.
   ```

3. For example: `Egg cracking on the edge of a pan, then sizzling in hot oil`

Your words can differ. Check that the loop names its tempo, says "just", and that the effect gives an order.

## Recap

- Pick the model by length: Lyria 3 Clip is always 30 seconds; Lyria 3.5 and Eleven Music let you set it.
- Answer genre, mood, instruments, tempo and era. Vocals are on by default, so say what you want.
- Plan changes in bars, give sound effects an order, and keep each effect within 0.1 to 30 seconds.
