## Why it matters

A small coffee roaster makes a 45-second video about its new bean. The voice-over is done (chapters 1 to 3). Now it needs two more things:

- a calm **music bed**: music that plays quietly under the voice (our plain gloss)
- two **sound effects** (SFX): a grinder whirring, and a cup set down on a table

The first try at music came back with a singer, on top of the voice-over. This chapter shows how to ask for what the video needs.

A note on dates: the models and limits below are from each vendor's page in October 2026.

## Two vendors for music

| Vendor (October 2026)    | Model                         | Length                                            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Google, Lyria 3 Clip     | `lyria-3-clip-preview`        | always 30 seconds                                 |
| Google, Lyria 3.5        | `lyria-3.5`                   | "A couple of minutes (controllable using prompt)" |
| ElevenLabs, Eleven Music | Music v2.5 (`music_v2_5`), v2 | from 3 seconds to 5 minutes                       |

Google's table says Lyria 3 Clip is for "Short clips, loops, previews". Lyria 3.5 is for "Full-length songs with verses, choruses, bridges". Its tip: "Iterate with Clip first", then make the full version with `lyria-3.5`.

So a 30-second clip is good for testing a sound, but it cannot be a 45-second bed. For that, use Lyria 3.5 and say the length in the prompt ("create a 2-minute song" is Google's example), or set the length on ElevenLabs ("60 seconds" is its example).

## Write a music prompt

Both vendors want the same basic facts. ElevenLabs says a prompt answers five questions "whether you intend it to or not: genre, mood, instrumentation, tempo, and production era." If you leave one open, the model picks "the most average one."

Google's Lyria guide says: "Lead your prompt with the primary genre." Then name instruments, and add "standard musical properties":

- **Tempo (BPM):** BPM means beats per minute, the speed of the music (our plain gloss). Google's examples: "120 BPM", "slow tempo around 72 BPM".
- **Key:** the set of notes the music uses (our plain gloss). Google's examples: "in G major", "in D minor".
- **Mood:** words like "Chill", "Dreamy", "Upbeat".

ElevenLabs says its model "accurately follows BPM and often captures the intended musical key."

For the roaster (our example): "Warm acoustic folk, slow tempo around 72 BPM, in G major, soft fingerpicked guitar and light brushed drums, calm morning mood. Instrumental only, no vocals."

## Instrumental, or with words

Here is why the first try had a singer. Google: "Lyria 3.5 generates vocal tracks with lyrics by default." ElevenLabs: "By default, most music prompts will include lyrics." Under a voice-over, that is a problem.

- **Google** shows "Instrumental only, no vocals." in its prompt.
- **ElevenLabs** says to add "instrumental only" to your prompt.

When you do want a song, you can write the words yourself:

- **Google:** put your lyrics "beneath a `Lyrics:` header". Tag each part: `[Verse 1]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`. Use parentheses for backing vocals, like "(moving on)". And "Separate lyrics from instructions". Our reading: then the model does not sing your directions.
- **Language:** Lyria "generates lyrics in the language of your prompt". For French lyrics, write the prompt in French.
- **Real artists:** Google's safety filters block "prompts that request specific artist voices or the generation of copyrighted lyrics."

## Shape the music over time

You can tell the model what happens when.

- **Google:** time ranges like `[0:00 - 0:10] Intro: ...` and `[0:10 - 0:30] Verse 1: ...`, or cues like "The chorus kicks in at 22 seconds".
- **ElevenLabs:** narrate the order "the way you'd brief a band": "start with just a shuffled drum loop, add a warm sub bassline after four bars". It says the small words matter: "Without the _just_, the model fills the silence".

For the roaster (our example), a quiet start helps the first spoken line: "Start with just guitar. Add soft drums at 10 seconds."

## Fixing a take

The two vendors differ here.

- **Google:** "Music generation is a single-turn process." You cannot refine a clip with more prompts. Change the prompt and generate again. "Results may vary between calls, even with the same prompt."
- **ElevenLabs:** in its app you can select one section of a song "and regenerate just that section". This is called **inpainting**.

Google also says every Lyria track has a SynthID audio watermark, which you cannot hear.

## Sound effects

ElevenLabs has a separate sound effects model. You give a text description and two optional settings:

- **Duration:** "Range: 0.1 to 30 seconds". If you don't set it, the length is chosen from the prompt.
- **Looping:** makes an effect that "can be played on repeat without perceptible start/end points". Its example: "Generate 30s of 'soft rain' then loop it endlessly".
- **Prompt influence:** "High: More literal interpretation of the prompt". "Low: More creative interpretation with added variations".

The maximum is "30 seconds per generation". So a 40-second grinder hum is one 30-second looping effect, played longer in the edit (our reading).

Prompt tips from the same page:

- Simple effect: a short, clear description, like "Heavy wooden door creaking open".
- A sequence: say the order, like "Footsteps on gravel, then a metallic door opens".

ElevenLabs gives a small word list. A **one-shot** is a "Single, non-repeating sound". A **loop** is a "Repeating audio segment". A **stem** is an "Isolated audio component", for example the drums alone.

For the roaster (our examples): "Coffee grinder whirring, steady, close-up" with looping on. "Ceramic cup set down on a wooden table" as a one-shot.

## Sound inside the video

Prompt Forest, chapter 5, showed another way: Veo makes sound with the picture. Google's Veo page says Veo 3.1 makes "8-second videos" with "natively generated audio". You can ask for dialogue, sound effects and "Ambient Noise" in the video prompt.

The difference (our reading): Veo's sound comes inside the clip, tied to that picture. A sound effect file from ElevenLabs is separate, so you can move it, loop it, or use it again. Reel Hollow, chapter 3, showed how sound can jump at a seam between clips. A separate music bed under the whole video avoids that jump.

## What this means for your work

- **Pick the model by length.** Lyria 3 Clip is always 30 seconds. Use Lyria 3.5 or ElevenLabs for a longer bed.
- **Answer the five questions:** genre, mood, instruments, tempo (BPM), era. Add a key if it matters.
- **Say "instrumental only"** for music under a voice. Both vendors add vocals by default.
- **Keep lyrics apart** from directions, under a `Lyrics:` header on Lyria.
- **Effects: 0.1 to 30 seconds** on ElevenLabs. Loop for longer background sounds.
- **Separate files are easier to edit** than sound baked into a video clip.
