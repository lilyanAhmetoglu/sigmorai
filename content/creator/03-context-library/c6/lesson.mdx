## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Keep a rights record for every asset, and keep an asset out of sets while a field is empty.
- Store the origin of a file in a place that survives edits, including for AI-made images.
- Plan how vendor copies are deleted when a licence ends.

## The problem

A bookshop chain, Pageturn, asks a studio to build a reference library for its posters. On the first day, these arrive:

- Cover photos from publishers, sent by email.
- A stock photo of a reading room, with a licence for "print only".
- Drawings from an illustrator, with no contract attached.
- A photo of a shop worker, Sam, reading. Nobody wrote down if Sam agreed.
- A poster the studio made last year with an image model.
- A picture "from the internet".

A designer wants to use all of them today. Some may be fine. Some may not. Nobody can tell, because nothing is written down.

**This is not legal advice.** Laws differ by country. This lesson is about records: what the vendor docs say, and what a studio can keep on file. For a contract question, ask your producer or a lawyer.

## The idea

**A rights record** is a short note kept with each asset. It is our method. A reference card says how an image is used. A rights record says whether you may use it.

```text
Source        where it came from, and when
Owner         the client, a stock site, a freelancer, the studio
Allowed use   what for, including use as an AI reference: yes or no
Valid until   the end date of the licence
People shown  names, and where the written permission is
Made by AI    model, date, prompt log, every reference used
Proof         where the contract or email is stored
```

Our rule: **if any field is empty, the asset is not used in any set** until someone fills it. A licence for one use does not cover another use unless it says so. A file sent by email is not a contract.

**The vendors put the duty on you.**

- The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation), before the editing examples: "Make sure you have the necessary rights to any images you upload."
- The [Runway docs](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/reference-media), for talent: "only use references of people you have permission to depict. You are responsible for the rights to any likeness you submit."

These are reminders, not checks. The tool does not find the answer for you.

**Keep the origin outside the file.** The [OpenAI provenance docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/content-provenance) say: "C2PA metadata provides more context about a file's origin. Editing, converting, or sharing a file can remove its metadata." The [C2PA site](https://c2pa.org) describes a standard "to establish the origin and edits of digital content". So a resized copy may lose its label. Two habits of ours:

- Keep the original file **untouched** in an archive, with any credentials it came with.
- Write the source in the **catalogue**, which no export can change.

Two tools do not prove where any file came from:

- The OpenAI provenance check "checks for supported OpenAI signals. It isn't a general-purpose AI detector".
- The Gemini docs say: "All generated images include a SynthID watermark." That covers images made by those models, not a client's own photo.

**AI-made assets need a record too.** As we read it, a watermark tells you an image is AI-made. It cannot hold your prompt, the model version or the references that went in. Those inputs can matter: if a licensed photo went in, its terms may still apply. Our rule: log which model, which day, which prompt log, and each reference.

**Make rights searchable.** Rights are hard rules, so they belong in search filters. The [OpenAI retrieval docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/retrieval) let you set `attributes` on files. Store fields such as `valid_until` and `ai_reference_ok` there, so an expired asset never reaches the model.

**When a licence ends, delete the vendor copies.**

- **Anthropic.** The [Claude Files API docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/files) say:
  - "Files persist until you delete them" or reach their `expires_at`.
  - `expires_in_seconds` is set at upload, "between 3,600 (1 hour) and 7,776,000 (90 days)".
  - "Expiration is set once at upload and cannot be changed."
  - Expiry is "a lifecycle feature", not a deletion promise. Content "may be retained for a limited period thereafter for safety review".
  - To remove a file sooner, use `DELETE /v1/files/{file_id}`. "Deleted files cannot be recovered."
- **OpenAI.** The [data controls docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data) have a table of endpoints. For `/v1/files`, it shows "Until deleted" in the application state column, and 30 days of abuse monitoring retention. You can delete files by hand (API or dashboard), or let them go on their own with `expires_after`. The retrieval docs add that a vector store can have `expires_after` too. When it expires, "all associated `vector_store.file` objects will be deleted".

## Worked example

### Step 1: the intake before

```text
P-01  publisher cover        "they sent it"
P-02  reading-room stock     "licensed"
P-03  illustrator drawing    "came by email"
P-04  photo of Sam           (empty)
P-05  last year's AI poster  "we made it"
P-06  internet picture       "looks free"
```

Every line is a guess. None says what use is allowed, until when, or where the proof is.

### Step 2: fill the records

```text
P-01  Owner: publisher. Use: posters + AI reference, OK in writing
      (email saved). Ends: 2027-06-30.                              -> ready
P-02  Owner: stock site. Licence: print only.
      AI reference: not stated.                             -> out, ask producer
P-03  Owner: illustrator. No contract on file.              -> out, ask producer
P-04  Sam: no written permission.                           -> out
P-05  Made by AI: model, date, prompt log #41,
      references: P-01 only. Use and end date as P-01.      -> ready
P-06  Source unknown.                                       -> out
```

Two assets are ready today. Four stay out until a person fills the empty field.

### Step 3: the hard case, a licence longer than 90 days

P-01 is valid until 2027-06-30, far more than 90 days away. A copy is in the Claude Files API. The longest expiry is 90 days, so `expires_in_seconds` cannot reach the end date. And even a 90-day expiry is not a deletion guarantee. So:

```text
1. Write 2027-06-30 in the catalogue.
2. Add a task: DELETE the Claude copy and the OpenAI copy by 2027-06-30.
3. On that date, mark P-01 retired.
```

### Step 4: the resized banner

A designer resizes P-01 for a web banner. The banner has lost its Content Credentials. That is fine, because the origin is in the catalogue, and the untouched original is in the archive.

### Step 5: make rights a filter

```text
attributes:  valid_until = 2027-06-30,  ai_reference_ok = true
filter:      ai_reference_ok = true AND valid_until >= today
```

Now an expired or blocked asset is filtered out before the model sees it.

### Why it is better

- **Every asset has a record**, and empty fields keep it out.
- **The origin survives edits**, because it lives in the catalogue.
- **AI-made work lists its inputs.**
- **Deletion is planned**, not left to expiry.

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating a spoken yes or an email as a licence.** Nothing says what is allowed. Fix: written terms in the record, or the asset stays out.
2. **Trusting the file's label.** One export can remove it. Fix: the catalogue and the untouched original.
3. **Relying on expiry to delete.** Expiry has a 90-day maximum at Anthropic and is not a deletion guarantee. Fix: a delete task on the end date.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A studio uploads a client logo to the Claude Files API. The client's permission ends in 45 days. A designer says: "Set the expiry to 45 days and we are done." What is right in this idea, and what is missing?

### Answer

- **Right:** 45 days is 3,888,000 seconds. That is inside the allowed range (3,600 to 7,776,000), so the expiry can be set at upload.
- **Missing:** expiry does not promise deletion, and it cannot be changed later. So also log the end date in your catalogue. Plan a task to `DELETE` the file by that date, and mark the logo retired when the date comes.

## Recap

- Keep a rights record for every asset. If a field is empty, the asset waits. The vendors say the rights are your job.
- File labels can disappear when a file is edited. Keep the source in the catalogue and the original in an archive. AI-made assets list model, date, prompt log and references.
- Plan deletion on the licence end date. Anthropic's expiry is at most 90 days and is not a guarantee. OpenAI files stay until deleted or until `expires_after`.
