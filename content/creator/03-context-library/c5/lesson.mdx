## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Build a cast sheet with a clean anchor image and approved extra views.
- Plan shots around a model's reference limit.
- Stop a character from drifting over many images.

## The problem

A toothpaste brand, Brightbite, has a mascot: Zip, a small blue robot. Zip appears in 40 posts and six short videos. Each designer makes Zip from the last image they have.

After two months:

- Zip's antenna has moved from the left side to the middle.
- The blue is now closer to purple.
- One video shows Zip next to a real dentist, but the photo of the dentist was taken at a party, laughing, with a hat.

A model does not remember Zip between requests. Each request starts new. The [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation) list this as a known limit: the model "may occasionally struggle to maintain visual consistency for recurring characters or brand elements across multiple generations". So consistency is something you build and check.

## The idea

**A cast sheet** is one page per recurring character. It is our method:

```text
Name          Zip, the robot mascot
Anchor        one clear front view, neutral, even light
Views         side, three-quarter, back, each approved
In words      a fixed description, used in every prompt
Never change  the details a stranger could check at a glance
Permission    who made it or who it shows, and where the proof is
```

**Start with a clean anchor.** The [Runway docs](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/recipes/reference-media) give advice for talent references in its Recipes. Using it for any character is our method:

- "Use a clear, well-lit reference with the face visible and unobstructed."
- "Choose a neutral expression and framing unless you want a specific look carried through."

A laugh, a hat or a side light in the anchor can carry into every later image. Writing "neutral face" in the prompt does not undo what the picture shows. Fix the picture. For a real person, that means a new photo, taken with their permission. The Runway docs say: "only use references of people you have permission to depict." Also: "One subject per reference". So the anchor shows one character, not a group.

**Build the other views from the anchor.** The [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) describe a 360-degree method:

- Ask for each angle in turn: "iteratively prompting for different angles".
- "include previously generated images in subsequent prompts to maintain consistency".
- "For complex poses, include a reference image of the selected pose."

Our rule adds a check: approve each new view against the anchor before you use it.

**Say what must not change, in detail.** The Gemini docs advise: "Be hyper-specific". Their example: not "fantasy armor" but "ornate elven plate armor, etched with silver leaf patterns, with a high collar and pauldrons shaped like falcon wings." Write the never-change line the same way, so anyone can check it. For a small edit, the Gemini docs suggest a follow-up that keeps the rest of the image the same and changes only one thing, such as the expression.

**Count the reference slots.**

- **Images:** the Gemini docs say Nano Banana 2.1 supports "character resemblance of up to 4 characters". Nano Banana Pro takes "Up to 5 images of characters".
- **Video:** the [Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) say "Veo 3.1 now accepts up to 3 reference images", and to "Provide images of a person, character, or product". The docs' own example uses three images: a dress, a woman and a pair of glasses. So a person, a mascot and a product can each take one of the same 3 slots. Another line on the same page says "up to three asset images of a single person, character, or product"; its examples show this means one subject per image. Veo 3.1 Lite does not take reference images. The image-model limits do not apply to Veo.

A collage of four characters in one image is not a trick around the limit. It is a reference with several competing subjects, which the Runway docs advise against.

**Go back to the sheet.** Our habit: for each new asset, give the approved sheet image, not the latest result. If each result feeds the next request, tiny mistakes add up, like a photocopy of a photocopy. The Gemini advice to include earlier images is for building the views. A higher quality setting is not the fix. The OpenAI docs list consistency as a limit and do not tie it to quality.

A good sheet lowers the risk, but the limit still applies. Compare each new image with the anchor before it ships.

## Worked example

### Step 1: the setup before

```text
Zip         made from "the last good image"
Dentist     one party photo: laughing, hat, side light
Shot list   Shot A: Zip, the dentist, the toothbrush and the tube, together
```

What goes wrong:

- No anchor, so every image copies the drift of the one before.
- The dentist photo will carry the laugh and the hat.
- Shot A needs four references. Veo 3.1 takes up to 3.

### Step 2: choose the anchor and write the never-change line

```text
Anchor:       zip-front-v1.png, front view, even light, plain background
Never change: sky-blue body, one antenna on the LEFT side, two round
              white eyes, orange heart on the chest, three-finger hands
```

A reviewer can tick off each detail in seconds. "Keep Zip cute" cannot be ticked off.

### Step 3: build the side view

```text
Image 1: zip-front-v1.png (the anchor)

Create a side view of the robot in image 1, facing right, same pose,
same plain background and light. Keep the sky-blue body, the antenna
on the left side of the head, the round white eyes and the orange
heart exactly as in image 1.
```

Put the result next to the anchor. Approve it, add it to the sheet, then ask for the three-quarter view with both images.

### Step 4: fix the dentist reference

First get the dentist's written permission, and record it on the sheet. Then take a new photo: even light, the whole face in view, a calm face, no hat.

### Step 5: the hard case, too many things in one shot

Shot A needs four references: Zip, the dentist, the toothbrush and the tube. Veo 3.1 has 3 slots, and they are shared. Plan around them:

```text
Shot A1  Zip, the dentist, the tube         3 references
Shot A2  Zip, the toothbrush, the tube      3 references
```

Another option: if the toothbrush is a plain white brush, describe it in words and keep it out of the slots.

### Step 6: protect the next 30 posts

For every new post, give `zip-front-v1.png` and the approved views, never last week's result. Before each post ships, put it next to the anchor and check the never-change line.

### Why it is better

- **One clean anchor** is the source for everything.
- **Each view is approved** before use.
- **Shots fit the slots**, so no reference is dropped or mixed.
- **Drift is stopped at the source**, because every request starts from the sheet.

## Common mistakes

1. **Chaining results.** Each new image is made from the one before, and errors add up. Fix: always start from the approved sheet image.
2. **A vague never-change line.** "Same as usual" cannot be checked. Fix: name colours, positions and shapes.
3. **A lively anchor.** A laugh or a hard shadow in the anchor shows up everywhere. Fix: a neutral, evenly lit anchor; add expression in the prompt later.

## Try it

Not graded. Write your answer down before you read the answer.

A pet-food brand plans a Veo 3.1 shot with its parrot mascot, a real child actor, a dog and the food bag. All four need a reference image. Plan the shots, and say what you need before the child's photo can be used.

### Answer

- **Four references do not fit** in Veo 3.1's 3 shared slots. Split the shot, for example: parrot, child, food bag; then parrot, dog, food bag. Or describe a simple element in words instead of using a slot.
- **The child's photo:** written permission on file first (for a child, ask your producer what form is needed). Then a clear, evenly lit photo with the face visible, a neutral expression and one subject.

## Recap

- One cast sheet per character: a clean, neutral anchor, approved views, and a never-change line anyone can check.
- Count the reference slots before you plan a shot. Veo 3.1 takes up to 3 images, and any mix of person, mascot or product shares them.
- For every new asset, start from the approved sheet, not the last result, and compare with the anchor.
