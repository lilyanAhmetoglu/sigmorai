## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Write a first message that says what you want, the details that matter and the shape of the answer.
- Read the answer as a draft, check it, and fix it with short follow-ups in the same chat.
- See when a chat has gone wrong, and start a new one with a clean summary.

## The problem

You open a chat app for the first time. It can be Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or another one. There is an empty box. You type three words and get a long, polite answer. It is not quite what you wanted, and you don't know what to do next.

Most people stop here. They think "AI is not for me", or they copy the answer without reading it. Both are mistakes. A chat is a conversation, not a search box. This lesson shows the four moves that work in any chat app: **ask, read, follow up, start again**. The quotes below are from each vendor's help pages (October 2026).

## The idea

**1. Ask like you talk to a colleague.** The [ChatGPT docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/use-chatgpt) say: "Write as if you were explaining the request to a helpful colleague." The [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114491-get-started-with-claude) gives the same advice: speak "like you would a coworker or friend". A good first message has three parts:

```text
What you want    the task and why ("a short email to my team")
The details      facts the app can't guess (names, dates, who reads it)
The shape        length, tone, format ("5 lines, friendly, no jargon")
```

The ChatGPT docs say it in one line: "State what you want to accomplish, add the details that change the answer, and describe the format you need." The Claude Help Center adds: pretend you are writing for "someone with no background knowledge about what you are asking".

**2. Read the answer as a draft.** Every vendor warns that answers can be wrong. The [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13275745) says: "Gemini Apps can make mistakes." The [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8525154-claude-is-providing-incorrect-or-misleading-responses-what-s-going-on) says the model "can write things that might look correct but are very mistaken." The ChatGPT docs say: "Treat the first result as a draft you can inspect, challenge, and improve." Their checklist starts with: "Verify important numbers, names, dates, quotes, and claims."

**3. Follow up in the same chat.** You don't need a perfect first message. The ChatGPT docs say: "Your first prompt is only a starting point". The Claude Help Center says: "Don't hesitate to refine your prompts or ask follow-up questions." Short follow-ups work well. The ChatGPT docs give examples like "Make this shorter." and "What assumptions are you making?" Many apps also let you edit your last message and get a new answer (the Gemini Apps Help calls this "Edit your prompt").

**4. Start again when the chat goes wrong.** A chat has a size limit. The [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11647753-how-do-usage-and-length-limits-work) calls it the context window, "Claude's working memory", and says that when you hit the limit "you can start a new conversation". A new chat also helps when the old one mixed many topics, or kept going in the wrong direction. Bring only what is still true: paste a short summary of the facts and your request.

One warning: some apps have a **memory** feature. The [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context) says Claude "can also remember context from your chats and carry it into new conversations". So if a wrong fact follows you into a new chat, check the app's memory settings.

A small comparison: a chat is like working with a new helper who is fast but has never met you. You explain the job, you check the work, you give short notes, and if the notes get messy, you start a fresh page.

## Worked example

Ana leads a small team. The Friday team meeting must move to Monday at 10:00, because two people are away on Friday. She wants a short message for the team.

### Step 1: the first message (before)

```text
write email meeting
```

The answer is a long, formal email about "an upcoming meeting". It has no day, no time and no reason. The app had to guess everything.

### Step 2: the first message (after)

```text
Write a short email to my team of 6.
Our Friday meeting moves to Monday at 10:00, same video link.
Reason: two people are away on Friday.
Keep it under 5 lines, friendly, and end with "Reply if Monday doesn't work."
```

What you want, the details and the shape are all there.

### Step 3: read the answer

The new answer is short and friendly. But it says "Monday 12 October at 10:00 in Room 4B". Ana never gave a date or a room. The app invented them. **This is the step people skip.** She checks every name, date and place against what she knows.

### Step 4: follow up

```text
Remove the room, we meet on video. Remove the date, just say "next Monday".
```

The next answer is right. Two short notes fixed it. She didn't need to write a new long message.

### Step 5: the hard case, start again

Later, in the same chat, Ana asks for help with a budget table, then a job post, then a new email: "the meeting now moves to Tuesday". The answer says "Monday" again and adds a line from the job post. **This is the hard case.** The chat is long and mixed, and old details leak into new answers. She opens a new chat and pastes a clean request:

```text
Write a short email to my team of 6.
The meeting moves from Monday to Tuesday at 10:00, same video link.
Keep it under 5 lines and friendly.
```

### Step 6: why it is better

- **Clear ask.** The app didn't have to guess the day, the time or the tone.
- **Checked.** The invented room never reached the team.
- **Cheap fixes.** Short follow-ups fixed small problems.
- **Clean restart.** A new chat with only the true facts beat arguing with a confused one.

## Common mistakes

1. **Typing a few keywords, like a search box.** The app guesses the rest. Fix: say what you want, the details and the shape.
2. **Sending the first answer without reading it.** It can contain invented facts. Fix: check every name, number, date and quote before you use it.
3. **Fighting a confused chat for 20 more messages.** Old mistakes keep coming back. Fix: start a new chat and paste only the facts that are still true.

## Try it

Not graded. Open any chat app. Make this weak first message better, using the three parts.

```text
dinner ideas
```

You are cooking on Saturday for 4 people. One is vegetarian. You have about 45 minutes and a normal kitchen.

Then write one short follow-up for this problem: the answer has 10 long recipes, but you only want 3 short ideas.

### Answer

One good version:

```text
Suggest 3 dinner ideas for 4 people on Saturday.
One guest is vegetarian, so every dish must work for her.
I have about 45 minutes and a normal kitchen.
Give each idea in 2 lines: the dish and the main steps.
```

A good follow-up: "Give me only the 3 shortest ideas, 2 lines each." It is short, and it says exactly what to change.

## Recap

- Ask like you talk to a colleague: what you want, the details, the shape of the answer.
- Read every answer as a draft. Check names, numbers, dates and quotes, then fix it with short follow-ups.
- When a chat gets long, mixed or confused, start a new one with a short summary of what is still true.
