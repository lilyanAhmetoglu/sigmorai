## Goal

After this lesson you can:

- Split a task into small parts before you open a chat app.
- For each part, choose: hand it off, share it, or keep it.
- Name the parts that always need a person: the check, and the decisions that matter.

## The problem

Now you can chat with an AI app. The next question is harder: **should you use it for this task at all?** Some people use it for everything. Others use it for nothing. Both lose.

A task is rarely one thing. "Write the monthly report" is really: find the numbers, choose what matters, write the text, check it, and decide what to do next. AI is good at some of these parts and risky for others. This lesson gives you a simple way to decide, part by part. The quotes below are from each vendor's docs and help pages (October 2026).

## The idea

**Split the task first.** The [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/customer-support-chat) do this even for big projects. They call customer support "a collection of multiple different tasks" and say to "break down" the work into every task before you start. They talk about using AI to "automate portions" of the work, not all of it.

**Then ask three questions for each part.**

```text
1. Is AI good at this kind of work?       drafts, summaries, ideas, rewording
2. If it is wrong, can I see it and fix it before it matters?
3. Who owns the result or the decision?   a person, always
```

**Question 1: what AI is good at.** The vendors list the same kinds of work. The [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13275745) names brainstorming, "a quick, easy to understand summary" and "first drafts". The [ChatGPT docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/use-chatgpt) say to use chat to "brainstorm, draft or revise text, summarize files, compare options".

**Question 2: can you check it?** Answers can be wrong and still look good. The [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8525154-claude-is-providing-incorrect-or-misleading-responses-what-s-going-on) says users "should not rely on Claude as a singular source of truth". The [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-best-practices) recommend "having a human review outputs before they are used in practice", and say this "is especially critical in high-stakes domains". They also say the person who checks needs the original material, for example the original notes behind a summary. So a part is a good hand-off only if you can check it.

**Question 3: who decides?** Some decisions belong to a person or an expert. The ChatGPT docs say: "Legal, financial, medical, security, and other high-stakes decisions require appropriate expert review." And: "Use ChatGPT to support informed judgment, not replace it." The Gemini Apps Help says: "don’t rely on responses from Gemini Apps for professional advice."

**The three answers.**

```text
Hand off   AI does the first draft. You check it, then use it.
Share      AI helps you think (ideas, questions, options). You decide.
Keep       You do it. High stakes, or you can't check the result.
```

Even "hand off" has a person at the end. The OpenAI docs also say that models "may not be suitable for every use case". "Keep" is a real answer, not a failure.

A small comparison: think of a new, fast intern. You give them the first draft of a letter. You don't let them sign a contract.

## Worked example

Priya runs a running club as a volunteer. Every month she sends a newsletter to 80 members. It takes her three hours. She wants AI to "do the newsletter".

### Step 1: split the task

```text
a. Choose 3 topics for this month
b. Write the welcome text and the topic texts
c. List race dates and last month's results
d. Answer a member's question about knee pain
e. Decide if the club fee goes up next year
```

### Step 2: ask the three questions per part

```text
Part                  Good at it?   Can I check it?        Who decides?   Answer
a. topics             yes (ideas)   yes, I know the club   me             Share
b. texts              yes (drafts)  yes, I read them       me             Hand off
c. dates, results     no, it can    only against the       me             Keep
                      invent them   club's own records
d. knee pain          risky         no, I'm not a doctor   a doctor       Keep
e. fee decision       no            -                      the committee  Keep
```

### Step 3: the hard case

Part d looks easy. An app can write a friendly paragraph about knee pain in seconds. **This is the hard case.** It is health advice, Priya can't check it, and a wrong answer can hurt someone. So she keeps it: her answer is "please ask a doctor or a physiotherapist". At most, AI can help her phrase that kindly.

### Step 4: the plan

```text
Share:     ask for 10 topic ideas, pick 3 myself
Hand off:  AI drafts the texts from my notes, I read and fix every line
Keep:      dates and results from the club calendar, the knee question,
           the fee question (goes to the committee)
```

### Step 5: why it is better

- **Faster where it is safe.** The drafts take minutes, not an hour.
- **Nothing invented goes out.** Dates and results come from the club's records.
- **No one gets bad advice.** The health question goes to an expert.
- **Clear owner.** Priya still checks and sends the newsletter. The committee still decides the fee.

## Common mistakes

1. **All or nothing.** You use AI for the whole task, or not at all. Fix: split the task and decide part by part.
2. **"Hand off" with no check.** The draft goes out unread. Fix: a person reads every hand-off before it is used.
3. **Letting the app decide.** It sounds sure, so you follow it on a money, health or legal question. Fix: those decisions stay with a person or an expert.

## Try it

Not graded. You plan a weekend trip with three friends. Split the task into parts, and give each part an answer: hand off, share or keep.

```text
a. Find ideas for things to do in the city
b. Write the group message with the plan
c. Book and pay for the train tickets
d. Choose the final plan
```

### Answer

- **a. Share.** AI is good at ideas. You and your friends pick.
- **b. Hand off.** AI drafts the message from your plan. You check times and names before you send it.
- **c. Keep.** It is money, and the times and prices must be exact. Use the official booking site.
- **d. Keep.** The group decides. AI can list pros and cons, but people choose.

## Recap

- Split every task into parts before you use AI.
- For each part, ask: is AI good at this, can I check it, who decides? Then hand off, share or keep.
- A person checks every hand-off, and high-stakes decisions (money, health, law, safety) stay with a person or an expert.
