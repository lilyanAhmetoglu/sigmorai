## What the lesson covers

A vague prompt makes the model guess, and each guess is a place where the output can drift. The Claude docs suggest a test: show your prompt to a colleague with little context on the task. If they would be confused, Claude will be too.

The lesson turns that test into four habits, drawn from the Claude, OpenAI and Gemini docs:

- Replace words like "short" or "professional" with rules you can check: the reader, the format, the length and the content.
- Say what to do, not only what not to do, and give the reason.
- Use numbered steps when the order or completeness of steps matters.
- Match the detail to the model. OpenAI says GPT models benefit from more explicit instructions. Reasoning models need a precise goal and constraints, and can work out the method.

## Why it matters

A specific prompt gives a clear pass or fail for each output, so you can test it. OpenAI recommends pinning production apps to a model snapshot and building evaluation suites that measure prompt behaviour. The Gemini docs add that prompt engineering is iterative.

Example: keep 30 real support tickets with the summary you expect for each, and run every prompt change against them before it ships.
